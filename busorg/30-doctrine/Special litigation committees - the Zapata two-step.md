## Rule statement

Where demand is excused, the corporation may still reclaim the litigation by appointing a
**special litigation committee** and moving to terminate. On that motion:

- **Step one.** "The corporation [i.e., the SLC] should have the burden of proving
  **independence, good faith, and reasonableness**." If the burden is not met, the motion is
  denied.
- **Step two.** If the burden is met, the court "appl[ies] its own independent **business
  judgment**" to decide whether the motion should be granted — so "instances could arise
  where a committee can establish its independence and sound bases for its good faith
  decision and still have the corporation's motion denied."

## Elements

**1. The structural problem the doctrine answers.** The directors being sued are the ones who
appoint the SLC — "[b]ut who else could appoint them?" Ordinary deference would let defendants
choose their own judges, which is why the burden is inverted and why step two exists.

**2. Step one — what the SLC must prove.**

- **Independence** — the committee must show "there is no material issue of fact calling into
  doubt its independence."
- **Good faith.**
- **Reasonableness** of the investigation.

**3. How independence is assessed — in the aggregate.** "Taken in isolation, the facts about
Ellison might well not be enough to compromise the SLC's independence. But that is not the
relevant inquiry. The pertinent question is whether, **given all the facts**, the SLC has met
its independence burden."

The question is whether "material considerations other than the best interests of [the
corporation] could have influenced the SLC's inquiry and judgments."

**4. Independence is situational, not moral.** A finding against an SLC "is not a conclusion
that [its members] are not persons of good faith and moral probity, it is solely to conclude
that they were **not situated to act with the required degree of impartiality**." The premise
is that "persons of integrity and reputation can be compromised in their ability to act without
bias when they must make a decision adverse to others with whom they share material
affiliations."

**5. What the requirement is for.** So "that stockholders do not have to rely upon special
litigation committee members who must put aside personal considerations that are ordinarily
influential in daily behavior in making the already difficult decision to **accuse fellow
directors of serious wrongdoing**."

**6. Composition is entirely within the company's control**, which is why failure is
attributable. "Rather than form an SLC whose membership was free from bias-creating
relationships, Oracle formed a committee fraught with them."

## Standard of review / burden

| | |
|---|---|
| Who moves | The corporation, through the SLC |
| Who bears the burden | **The corporation** — inverted from the usual motion |
| Step one | Independence, good faith, reasonable investigation |
| Standard | No material issue of fact calling independence into doubt |
| Step two | The court's **own** independent business judgment |
| Can a clean SLC still lose? | **Yes**, expressly |

## Exceptions

- **Not every state follows Zapata.** New York's *Auerbach v. Bennett* reviews an independent
  committee's **procedures and methodologies** but protects its substantive decision under the
  business judgment rule — no step two.
- **Step two sits in tension with the business judgment rule itself.** A court exercising its
  own business judgment is exactly what
  [[Business judgment rule - scope of protection]] otherwise forbids. The justification is
  structural rather than doctrinal: the ordinary presumption assumes a disinterested
  decision-maker, and an SLC appointed by defendants may not be one.

## Leading case

[[In re Oracle Corp. Derivative Litigation]] — two Stanford professors recruited to the board
in summer 2001 and then asked to investigate a fellow Stanford professor and two university
benefactors, one of whom was contemplating a very large gift to Stanford while they were being
courted. "[A] social atmosphere painted in too much vivid Stanford Cardinal red."

## Best counter-case

[[United Food (Tri-State) Pension Fund v. Zuckerberg]] on independence, and the contrast is
about **who must prove what**.

The distinguishing line: **under Zapata the corporation must prove independence; under Rule
23.1 the plaintiff must plead facts creating reasonable doubt about it.** The same
relationships can therefore produce opposite results depending on which motion is before the
court. Allocation of the burden is doing at least as much work as the underlying facts.

## Professor gloss

## Common trap

1. **Giving step one and stopping.** Step two — the court's own business judgment — is the
   doctrinally distinctive half and the part that separates Delaware from New York.
2. **Arguing the SLC members were biased or dishonest.** Oracle expressly disclaims that. The
   word is **situated**.
3. **Assessing connections one at a time.** The inquiry is cumulative, and each Stanford tie
   was individually weak.
4. **Forgetting who bears the burden.** The corporation does, which is the opposite of nearly
   every other motion in the course.
5. **Assuming an SLC is available in every case.** It is the corporation's move after demand is
   excused, and appointing one concedes that the litigation is worth taking seriously.
