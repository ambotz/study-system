## Rule statement

Joint adventurers, like copartners, owe one another the duty of the finest loyalty
while the enterprise continues. The standard is "not honesty alone, but the
punctilio of an honor the most sensitive."

A **managing** coadventurer who receives an opportunity as an incident of the
management must disclose that opportunity, so that the chance to compete for it is
equalised. Appropriating the opportunity in secrecy extends a constructive trust
over what was taken, at the option and for the benefit of the excluded party.

## Elements

**1. A partnership or joint venture, while the enterprise continues.**

Fiduciary relationships of this kind can arise from the operative effect of the
parties' agreement, whatever the agreement calls the arrangement.

**2. A managing role, which raises the weight of the duty.**

The duty is not uniform across the venturers. In [[Meinhard v. Salmon]] both were
coadventurers, but "the heavier weight of duty rested upon Salmon" because he held
sole power to manage, lease, underlet and operate. Exclusive control generates the
disclosure obligation, "since only through disclosure could opportunity be
equalized."

**3. An opportunity that came to the manager as an incident of the management.**

This is the **nexus** element, and it is the scope limit on the whole doctrine:

- **Inside**: the opportunity is an extension and enlargement of the venture's
  subject-matter. The new lease in Meinhard covered the tract of which the venture's
  parcel was the keystone, and the counterparty approached the manager precisely
  because he was the man in possession.
- **Outside**: "A different question would be here if there were lacking any nexus
  of relation between the business conducted by the manager and the opportunity
  brought to him as an incident of management." A proposal to lease a building "at
  a location far removed" would have been Salmon's to keep.

**4. Failure to disclose.**

The breach is the **exclusion from the chance to compete**, not the taking. The
manager "might have warned" the coadventurer that the plan existed and that either
was free to compete. Whether a disclosing manager who then won the opportunity must
hold it for the venture is expressly left undecided.

**What is not required**

- Conscious purpose to defraud. Good faith is assumed in Meinhard and liability
  follows anyway.
- Proof that the excluded party would have won. "Such a calculus of probabilities
  is beyond the science of the chancery."

## Standard of review / burden

| | |
|---|---|
| Standard of conduct | The punctilio of an honor the most sensitive |
| Heightened for | The managing coadventurer |
| Breach | Exclusion from the chance to compete |
| Intent required | None |
| Counterfactual inquiry | Refused |
| Remedy | Constructive trust, calibrated to preserve the venture's original allocation of control |
| Duration | While the enterprise continues — and the duty does not thin as the term runs down |

## Exceptions

- **No nexus, no duty.** An opportunity unrelated to the venture's business belongs
  to the manager.
- **Contractual displacement.** Andrews' dissent argued the written contract defined
  a limited object ending at a limited time, and that Meinhard's equity was in the
  one lease alone. That position substantially governs the modern LLC materials,
  where an operating agreement may displace default fiduciary duties — see
  [[Elf Atochem North America v. Jaffari]] and the note on LLC default fiduciary
  duties. **Meinhard is the default rule; the later material is about contracting
  out of it.**
- **Remedial calibration.** The trust may attach to shares rather than the asset,
  with an extra share to the manager, where an even split would strip the control
  the venture's plan gave him.

## Leading case

[[Meinhard v. Salmon]] — with four months left on a twenty-year lease, the managing
coadventurer took for his own corporation a new lease over the enlarged tract,
telling his partner nothing until it was signed.

## Best counter-case

[[Walkovzky v. Carlton]], assigned the same day, as the structural opposite rather
than a doctrinal conflict.

The distinguishing line: **inside a fiduciary relationship the law demands the
punctilio of an honor the most sensitive; outside one, a plaintiff cannot even
reach the shareholder of a deliberately judgment-proof enterprise.** The presence
or absence of the fiduciary relation is worth more than any other single fact in
this part of the course.

## Professor gloss

## Common trap

1. **Arguing the manager earned it.** Cardozo concedes that Salmon gave time,
   labour and money, that he made the venture a success, and that Meinhard "had
   already been richly paid." He then holds those recriminations have force only
   "if conduct is to be judged by the common standards of competitors." Quote the
   concession before rejecting it.
2. **Missing the nexus limit.** The doctrine is not that a fiduciary may never take
   an opportunity. Same subject-matter, extended and enlarged, is inside; far
   removed is outside. Every hypothetical will move the opportunity along that axis.
3. **Treating the remedy as the breach.** Salmon's obligation was disclosure.
   Whether he would also have had to share a fairly won opportunity is expressly
   undecided, and claiming otherwise overstates the holding.
4. **Letting the duty expire with the term.** Four months or four years makes no
   difference — "he might do so with equal right while there remained as many
   years."
5. **Arguing the chance was worthless.** The court refuses counterfactual valuation
   as beyond its competence, not as unproven.
