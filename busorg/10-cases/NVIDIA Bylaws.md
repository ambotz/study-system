## What it is

Twenty-two pages of operating machinery for the same company — meeting mechanics,
notice periods, officer roles, share transfer, indemnification. The syllabus marks it
**{skim}**, and it means it: the great majority is administrative and will never be
tested.

Fifteen articles. Article II (stockholder meetings, with the long advance-notice and
proxy-access provisions) runs to roughly half the document; Articles IV–XIII are
short and routine.

## What to notice

Five provisions do real work for this course. The rest is machinery.

- **Section 5 — advance notice, and § 5(h) proxy access.** The long procedural gate
  on nominating directors and raising business. Note especially the **"Exclusive
  Method"** clause: these procedures are the only route by which a stockholder can get
  a nominee into the company's own proxy materials. This is the private-ordering
  counterpart to the federal proxy rules assigned in session 22.
- **Section 10 — the white proxy card.** "Any stockholder directly or indirectly
  soliciting proxies from other stockholders must use a proxy card color other than
  white, which shall be reserved for the exclusive use by the Board of Directors." One
  sentence, easy to skim past, and a genuine weapon in a contested solicitation under
  universal proxy.
- **Section 48 — amendment, and the asymmetry.** The board "is expressly empowered to
  adopt, amend or repeal" the bylaws; stockholders may too, but only by **66⅔%**. The
  existence of both powers is the § 109(a) default; the gap between them is the
  governance fact.
- **Section 49 — Delaware forum.** The Court of Chancery is the "sole and exclusive
  forum" for derivative actions, fiduciary duty claims against directors, officers or
  employees, claims under the DGCL or the constitutive documents, and internal-affairs
  claims. Compare the **federal**-forum provisions for 1933 Act claims upheld in
  *Salzberg v. Sciabacucchi*, which appears later in the casebook.
- **A drafting artifact.** Charter Article V.B.1 is expressly "[s]ubject to paragraph
  (h) of **Section 43** of the Bylaws" — but Section 43 of the current bylaws is
  *Declaration of Dividends*, and the amendment limitation now sits in **Section 48**,
  subject to Section 46(h). The two instruments have been renumbered out of sync since
  the 2021 restatement. Charter and bylaws are amended by different actors on
  different clocks, and this is what that looks like in the wild.
