## Rule

Actual authority turns on **what the principal authorized the agent to do**, not
on what the principal intended to accomplish. A principal who reviews a document
and assents to its filing has authorized that filing, even where the principal
misunderstands the document's effect and would never have wanted it.

## Facts

- JPMorgan was administrative agent and secured party of record on two entirely
  unrelated financings: a 2001 **Synthetic Lease** (~$300 million) and a 2006
  **Term Loan** (~$1.5 billion).
- General Motors set out to repay only the Synthetic Lease. Mayer Brown, GM's
  counsel, assigned the unwind to an associate, who asked a paralegal unfamiliar
  with the transaction to search Delaware UCC-1 filings against GM.
- The search returned three UCC-1s. **Only two related to the Synthetic Lease.**
  The third, number 6416808 4, perfected the Term Loan. Neither the paralegal nor
  the associate caught the difference.
- The Closing Checklist and the draft UCC-3 termination statements therefore
  listed the Term Loan UCC-1 for termination alongside the correct ones.
- **Those drafts were circulated for review to a JPMorgan Managing Director** who
  supervised the payoff and had signed the Term Loan documents for JPMorgan, and
  to Simpson Thacher, JPMorgan's counsel. *This circulation-and-assent is what
  decides the case.*
- Neither JPMorgan nor Simpson Thacher raised any concern. The Simpson Thacher
  response was: "Nice job on the documents. My only comment, unless I am missing
  something, is that all references to JPMorgan Chase Bank, as Administrative
  Agent for the Investors should not include the reference 'for the Investors.'"
- Mayer Brown then circulated a draft Escrow Agreement instructing the escrow
  agent how to close. The same Simpson Thacher attorney replied that "it was
  fine" and signed it.
- All three UCC-3s were filed on October 30, 2008. The error surfaced only after
  GM's 2009 chapter 11 filing.
- **Nobody at any of the four organisations intended to touch the Term Loan.**
  *Argued and rejected — intent to accomplish is not the same question as
  authorisation to act.*

## Court Ruling

**Held**

- JPMorgan authorised the filing of the UCC-3 that terminated the Main Term Loan
  UCC-1, and the termination was therefore effective. JPMorgan dropped to
  unsecured status on a $1.5 billion facility.
- What a principal **intended to accomplish** is "a distinct question from what
  actions it authorized to be taken on its behalf." The second question is the
  only one the UCC asks.
- Actual authority "is created by a principal's manifestation to an agent that,
  as reasonably understood by the agent, expresses the principal's assent that the
  agent take action on the principal's behalf" (Restatement (Third) of Agency
  § 3.01).
- Repeated manifestations by JPMorgan and its counsel showed that both knew Mayer
  Brown would file the statement identifying the Term Loan UCC-1, and assented.
  "Nothing more is needed."
- Reversed; remanded with instructions to enter partial summary judgment for the
  Creditors' Committee.

**Rejected**

- *JPMorgan never instructed anyone to file this UCC-3, so the filing was
  unauthorised and ineffective.* The court accepted every premise — JPMorgan
  authorised GM only to terminate Synthetic Lease interests, and instructed its
  counsel accordingly — and held the premises irrelevant. Authorisation is
  measured by manifestations to the agent, and JPMorgan manifested assent to this
  document.
- *Mayer Brown exceeded the scope of its authority.* Mayer Brown did exactly what
  the circulated and approved documents said it would do. An agent that performs
  the approved document has not exceeded its authority merely because the
  document was wrong.

## Context

The pairing with [[Detroit Lions v. Argovitz]] on the same day is deliberate.
Motors Liquidation is about the **scope** of an agent's authority — what the agent
may do and bind the principal to. Argovitz is about the agent's **duties** once
acting. Together they mark the two halves of agency law the course returns to
whenever a board acts through officers or an officer acts through counsel.

The doctrinal line runs forward into [[Smith v. Van Gorkom]] and 8 Del. C.
§ 141(e). A board relying on an officer's report faces the same structural
problem JPMorgan faced: assent given without understanding is still assent, and
the law charges the principal with the consequences of the manifestation rather
than the intention. Van Gorkom sharpens this into a duty to become informed
before manifesting assent.

The procedural history is worth noting. The Second Circuit certified to the
Delaware Supreme Court the question whether a secured lender must merely
authorise the termination statement or must intend to terminate each listed
interest. Delaware answered that authorising the filing suffices, which left the
Second Circuit only the factual question of whether JPMorgan authorised this one.

## Class layer

### Posture

- Adversary proceeding in GM's chapter 11 by the Committee of Unsecured Creditors
  against JPMorgan, seeking a declaration that the termination was effective.
- Cross-motions for summary judgment; the Bankruptcy Court held the filing
  unauthorised and ineffective.
- On appeal the Second Circuit certified the governing legal question to the
  Delaware Supreme Court, then decided the remaining factual question itself.

### Facts

A synthetic lease lets a firm move an asset — usually real property — off its
balance sheet by vesting accounting ownership in a special purpose entity and
paying rent to that entity, while still owning the asset for tax purposes and
deducting depreciation and interest. The casebook's editors note the structure
was used extensively by Enron and was curtailed by post-Enron accounting reform.
GM's 2001 Synthetic Lease raised roughly $300 million against liens on twelve
properties.

The 2006 Term Loan was a separate $1.5 billion facility from a different lending
syndicate, secured by equipment and fixtures at forty-two facilities and perfected
by twenty-eight UCC-1 filings around the country. JPMorgan happened to serve as
administrative agent and secured party of record on both.

In September 2008 GM told Mayer Brown it intended to repay the Synthetic Lease
and asked for the documents needed to release the related interests. The work ran
down through a partner, to an associate, to a paralegal who did not know what the
transaction was. The paralegal's Delaware UCC search returned three filings; two
were the right ones. The third was the keystone of a $1.5 billion facility.

The documents went to GM, Mayer Brown, JPMorgan, and Simpson Thacher. Four
organisations reviewed them. Nobody noticed.

### Issue

- Whether JPMorgan authorised the filing of a UCC-3 termination statement that
  identified for termination a security interest JPMorgan had no intention of
  releasing.

### Holding

- **Authorised, and therefore effective.** JPMorgan and its counsel reviewed the
  Closing Checklist and the draft UCC-3s, understood that Mayer Brown would file
  them on closing, and assented without objection. That manifestation of assent
  created actual authority regardless of JPMorgan's contrary intention as to the
  Term Loan.
- **Disposition:** reversed and remanded for partial summary judgment for the
  Committee, leaving JPMorgan unsecured on the Term Loan.

### Rule

- Actual authority arises from a principal's manifestation to the agent that, as
  reasonably understood by the agent, expresses assent to the agent acting on the
  principal's behalf.
- The measure is the manifestation, not the underlying intention.
- What the principal intended to accomplish and what the principal authorised are
  separate questions, and only the second controls whether the agent's act binds.
- Review of, and failure to object to, a specific document circulated for approval
  is a manifestation of assent to the act that document performs.

### Reasoning

- The court separates the two questions explicitly, and that separation is the
  whole opinion: the aims of JPMorgan and GM "were clear," and the court accepts
  them, before holding those aims beside the point.
- Restatement (Third) § 3.01 supplies the objective test. What matters is how the
  agent reasonably understood the principal's manifestation, so the principal's
  private understanding drops out.
- The evidentiary weight falls on circulation and silence. Drafts went to a
  JPMorgan Managing Director who had signed the Term Loan documents; drafts went
  to JPMorgan's own counsel; counsel's only comment was a stylistic edit to a
  signature block; counsel then approved and signed the Escrow Agreement.
- "Nothing more is needed" is the holding's operative phrase. The court sets a low
  bar for manifestation precisely because the UCC filing system depends on third
  parties being able to rely on what the record says.

### Dissent / concurrence

- None; the panel was unanimous.
- The strongest available counter-position is systemic rather than doctrinal: a
  rule this strict turns the filing system into a trap for the careful, because a
  reviewer must independently verify every file number rather than rely on
  counsel's description of what the documents do. The court's answer is that the
  alternative — letting a secured party undo a filed termination by asserting a
  contrary private intention — would make the public record unreliable for
  everyone who searches it.

### Cold-call notes

- **The question to expect** is what JPMorgan should have done. The answer is
  mechanical and unsatisfying: check the file numbers against the loan documents.
  There is no clever doctrinal escape, which is the point of assigning the case.
- **The trap** is arguing that a mistake this obvious cannot have been authorised.
  The court assumes the mistake throughout — nobody disputes that no one intended
  this — and holds it irrelevant. Answers built on "they clearly didn't mean to"
  have restated the losing argument.
- **Be ready for the policy push.** Why should a $1.5 billion interest evaporate on
  a paralegal's search error? The answer is reliance on the public record: a UCC
  search is only useful if what it shows is what is true.
- **Worth knowing**, and not on the exam: Simpson Thacher was JPMorgan's counsel,
  and the approving email is quoted in the opinion. The case is a standard object
  lesson at firms about what a two-line document review costs.
- **Numbers:** $300 million synthetic lease, $1.5 billion term loan, twelve
  properties, forty-two facilities, twenty-eight UCC-1s, three searched, one fatal.
