## What it is

The **restated certificate of incorporation** of a very large modern Delaware public
company, five pages, adopted under 8 Del. C. § 245 by the board alone because a
restatement that "only restates and integrates, and does not further amend" needs no
stockholder vote.

Assigned as the counterpart to
[[An Act to Incorporate the National Insurance Company]]. The 1815 charter is a list
of powers granted; this one is a list of **defaults declined**.

## What to notice

Each article answers the question: *what would happen if this were not here?*

- **Article III — purpose.** "[A]ny lawful act or activity for which a corporation may
  be organized under the General Corporation Law." § 102(a)(3) permits exactly this,
  and its brevity is the entire distance from § VIII of the 1815 charter.
- **Article IV — blank-check preferred.** Four billion common, two million preferred,
  with the board "authorized, by filing a certificate (a 'Preferred Stock
  Designation')... to fix or alter from time to time the designation, powers,
  preferences and rights" of any unissued series. **This is the exact mechanism
  Laster points to in [[West Palm Beach Firefighters v. Moelis & Co|Moelis]]** — the
  board could issue a single golden share whose certificate of designations becomes
  part of the charter by operation of law.
- **Article V.A.1 — board power and board size.** Management "vested in its Board of
  Directors," whose size is "fixed exclusively by one or more resolutions adopted by
  the Board of Directors." Stockholders cannot resize the board, which is what the
  Size Requirement in Moelis tried to achieve by contract.
- **Article V.A.2 — vestigial classified board.** The text still describes three
  classes, then says the classification "shall terminate at the annual meeting of
  stockholders to be held in **2014**." Dead language kept in a 2021 restatement.
- **Article V.A.3 — asymmetric removal.** With cause by a majority of voting power;
  **without cause by 66⅔%**. The § 141(k) default is a simple majority either way, so
  the charter has made removing a director without cause materially harder.
- **Article V.A.4 — vacancies to the board, not the stockholders**, unless the board
  resolves otherwise.
- **Article V.A.5 — conditional cumulative voting** if Cal. Corp. Code § 2115 applies.
  A Delaware corporation carrying a California quasi-foreign provision because it is
  headquartered in Santa Clara.
- **Article V.B.1 — bylaw amendment.** Stockholders need 66⅔%; the board "shall also
  have the power." Under § 109(a) stockholders hold the power by default and
  conferring it on directors does not divest them — so this is the standard both-hold
  arrangement with a supermajority on one side only.
- **Article V.B.3 — no written consent.** "[F]ollowing the closing of the Initial
  Public Offering no action shall be taken by the stockholders by written consent."
  Opts out of the § 228(a) default, which is assigned on its own in session 21.
- **Article VI.A — exculpation.** § 102(b)(7) recited essentially verbatim, including
  all four carve-outs, plus a forward-looking clause adopting any future amendment
  "to the fullest extent permitted."
- **Article VII.B — entrenchment.** 66⅔% to alter Articles V, VI or VII, so the
  provisions above cannot be undone by a bare majority.

**The pattern worth carrying:** every substantive article is either an election
§ 102(b) permits or a default the charter varies. Nothing here creates power.
