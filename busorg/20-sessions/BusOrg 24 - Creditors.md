## Notes

### Two Conflicts in Corp. Law (generally)

1. Management vs. Shareholder
2. Controlling shareholder vs. minority shareholder

### Preferred Shares

- Some modified version of stock that contains special attributes over common / residual

1. Designation Rights - can elect people on to Board (i.e., Series A pref. get 1 member on Board)
    - Includes veto rights

### Conversion

- Investor option: Can convert preferred to common once IPO happens
- Conversion ratio
- Drag / tag along - Common must vote yes if Board / Senior investors want to sell

### Preference

- Preference: amount of money preferred are owed before common are paid
    - Think about it like debt
- Multiple: Investor gets a size / pot of money they are entitled to after investing
- Coupon: rate at which the preference builds over time
- Participation Rights:
    - Non-participating - all value goes to common (non-preferred) (more debt-like)
    - Participating - preferred is included in value distributed to everyone (more founder friendly).

### 60M preferred Ex.

1. $60m (100%) → $0m EV to common → Maximize shares as a whole (starred)
2. $80m (50%) and $0m (50%) → $10m EV to common
    - 0 + (80-60m) = 0.5 + 20m·0.5 = 10m [?]
    - → Maximize common shares value only

- Additionally, fair price could cure unfair process in entire fairness test

### [[New Enterprise Associates 14 v. Rich (2023)|New Enterprise v. Rich]]

- Fund [?] wanted to sell; couldn't find buyer but Rich offered recap on (1) converting preferred to common (2) Rich gets only remaining pref. at 2x (3) voting agreement that if sale criteria are satisfied, they must sell (drag-along) + can't sue.

### Plaintiffs (sophisticated funds) sue

- Theory: Duty of loyalty breach (must maximize value of sale for residual claimants / company as a whole)
    - Covenant not to sue was invalid → pleaded
    - Drag rights are not contested (completely fine)
- Covenants among stockholders not to sue is enforceable, unless its an <u>intentional</u> tort or action involved.
- Theme: Shareholder voting agreements (i.e., drag along) are very common in private company.
    - How can drag along be fair if residual claimants must be prioritized?
        - Voting agreement is agreed to (based on drag along).
        - Then, once vote is approved, stockholder says Board itself shouldn't have agreed to it.
        - Drag along is valid as a stockholder agreement (only binding stockholder itself; not the Board / company as a whole).
