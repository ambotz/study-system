## Notes

### [[Books and records inspection - 220 before and after SB 21|220]] and Books and Records

- Shareholders have long been able to request books and records of the company
    - (1) Allows shareholders to understand what has happened within the company
    - (2) Allows communication across shareholders
    - Modern law: there are disclosure requirements
    - Can request charters (filed) and bylaws (not filed)
- Could always be able to request **books and records for a proper purpose**.

### Books and records

- Contains everything that has been considered and done by the company
    - How far does this go?

### Proper Purpose

- Balancing test between purpose and the requested material
    - Stronger purpose = more access to different documents

### Derivative suit

- Derivative suit → Heightened pleading standard (judge made)
    - Look at books and records first before making complaint
    - Justification of higher pleading standard based on having access to books and records

### Three ways to Acquire

1. Asset sale: All cash in exchange for target's assets
    - (1) Target's Board must consent and (2) under §271, shareholder approval from target (proxy via special meeting) if public
2. Merger
    - (1) Acquirer creates subsidiary (Merger Sub) and Target's assets go to Sub
    - (2) Target's shareholders are paid money for assets
    - (3) Require merger agreement and shareholders of target (not acquirer shs typically)
    - Asset sale and merger: Same Consent
3. Stock purchase (tender offer for Public Co. target)
    - (1) Stock of target shareholders have been bought by acquirer
    - (2) target Board **does not have a say**; their consent doesn't matter → only shareholders have to approve
        - Can be hostile to Board

### Common lawsuit

- Common lawsuit: Derivative claim after an agreement for merger (especially Target-side) [check: shareholder suits over a merger are direct claims, as his own post-closing line says]
    - Normally, [[Business judgment rule - scope of protection|BJR]] in the absence of self-dealing for Acquirer (Contra. Tesla)
    - **Target**: Not [[Business judgment rule - scope of protection|BJR]], instead enhanced scrutiny

### Target litigation

- Target litigation: Shareholders want to enjoin the vote on approving the merger.
    - Vote will usually result in a yes.
    - Post closing: Can file a direct claim (were harmed individually)
    - → Shareholders can't return to ex-ante status quo.
    - Obligations of target company to have to pay if merger doesn't close (Deal Protection ***)
