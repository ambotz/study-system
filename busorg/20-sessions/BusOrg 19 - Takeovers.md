### **Poison Pills / Shareholder Rights Plans**

1. Dividend: 1 "right" per share; stapled to stock
    - Conditional right; not inherently exercisable
    - **Redeemable for almost free**
    - Essentially an option issued by the company (option to buy more stock?)
2. Trigger
    - Ex. 20-30% tender offer on shares
    - Once trigger, right becomes:
        1. Exercisable
        2. Non-redeemable
        3. 1/100 pref share → $100
        - This is always valued/designed to be worth much less than $100. No one will ever exercise the right or the preferred share
        4. Flip-over (if target is merged)
        - Rights holder can purchase $200 of bidder stock for $100
        - Mechanism: discounted stock option either goes into the merger agreement or becomes a BoC [?]
        - Note: pill only works if there's a **back-end merger** after hostile takeover gains control
        - Sometimes raider will stop if back-end can't go through; just holds control
    - **Connection:** same game theory as two-tier offer in [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]]. Everyone would rather have the back-end premium

### Benefits

1. Doesn't require discrimination against bidder
2. Everyone can buy against bidder

### Flip-in pill

- Target's stock can be bought by rights holders at a huge discount → facially discriminatory (everyone not affiliated with the bidder can buy at this price)
    - More dominant today; easier when target can discriminate

### Beauty of the pill

1. Doesn't change target's finances / capital structure, etc.; unlike buying a new company, acquiring debt, etc.
    - Changing finances in response to takeover = (1) Board acknowledging shareholders take a hit to keep Board in control and (2) market constraints
2. Pill rights: totally notional and functionally requires tender offers to be filtered through the Board
3. Functionally, tender offers now need **Board consent**, just like merger agreements or asset purchase agreements
    - Disciplinary mechanism of the tender offer against Boards is weakened

### [[Moran v. Household International (1985)|Moran]] Court Holding

1. Didn't want to get into if 1/100 pref → $100 is a real security or preferred equity; would rather assess on equity and if the pill is used properly
2. **Court acknowledges redeemability in the first trigger → proves the Board can approve bids and will have decisive moments**
    - This **moment** can be scrutinized separately and will be analyzed under [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]] on that specific offer
