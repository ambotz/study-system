### Direct

- Each SH has the ability to establish claim that they were "directly" injured
    - Turns into a direct class action against Board or Controlling Shareholder

### Dual-Class shares

- Same economic rights
- Different voting rights
- Ensures founder group has controlling voting power
- Benefit of dual class: allows selling of shares w/o diluting control

### Facebook (cont.) - Giving pledge mechanics

1. New Class C shares
    - Amend charter (Board proposes + majority of SH votes)
2. For each current share, there is a new share, but the new shares are **non-voting**.
3. Zuck can take newly Class C shares (non-voting) and sell / donate those accordingly

- To implement Pledge, Board establish a Special Committee: Andreessen, Thiel, Desmond-Hellmann [?]

### Why this transaction requires independence

- Why is this a transaction that requires independence?

1. This deal adheres to Zuckerberg's personal ambitions → requires consent
2. But more importantly, dilutes Zuck's economic interest = Zuck caring less about what happens to company.

- When there is a controlling shareholder → don't need proxy statement, etc for conflicted transactions
- Marc tries to get independent committee vote on it

### Lawsuit

1. Direct - new shares issued changes voting power → settled (class action)
2. Derivative - lost company $$$ 100M [?] fees in settling direct suit
    - This is the case Pension Fund brings; attempts to pass demand futility test

- Passing Demand Futility: can't really lean too much on Board independence → turns into duty of care / profit-making claim. Instead, let's peek into the merits.
    - Prong 2 - likelihood of liability
- Demand board - board in place when derivative suit / demand futility pleaded

### **Demand Futility Prongs**

- Interested in transaction - received a material personal benefit
- Interested in litigation - substantial likelihood of liability
- Not independent from someone who is interested in transaction / litigation

### What independence is (Third Prong)

- If other Directors are just friends / super rich → social relationships are not enough to be deemed "not independent".

### [[In re Oracle Corp. Derivative Litigation (2003)|Oracle (2003)]]

- Insider trading allegation
    - Duty of loyalty breach (fraud / illegality)
- Derivative complaint filed → complaint succeeds [check: complaint survives, i.e. demand futility is pleaded; not decided on the merits] → SLC set up by Company to terminate derivative action
- SLC members (not originally on Board): Joe Grundfest (Stanford); Garcia (Stanford)
- Goal of SLC → independently prosecute Board (can take on SH suit)
    - Sometimes tainted [?]
- [[Special litigation committees - the Zapata two-step|Zapata]] framework on if SLC can terminate derivative action:
    1. 1) independence 2) good faith 3) reasonable
    2. Court's independent judgement
- **Under Zuckerberg framework, question is on prong 3: are the SLC members independent of Board**
    - Professor: very opposite of Zuckerberg finding on independence is years later
        - **This is again just a social relationship → hard to really say they are "dependent". Shows contrast between Zuckerberg.**
