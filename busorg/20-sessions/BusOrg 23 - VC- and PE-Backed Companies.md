### Analysis

- **[[Moran v. Household International (1985)|Moran]]: Fine to implement a pill under "clear sky"; no [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]] scrutiny (BJR)**
- **Here, Court applies [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]], because it is an activist (voting) play rather than a <u>tender offer</u>.**
    - In activism, can't wait until active contest because activism happens in "secret" so to speak.
        - Mere existence of pill = No avenue for activism
    - In tender offer, mere existence of pill doesn't prevent a takeover. Hostile bidder can take pill to court during takeover.
    - Margin note: Activists can't go to Board mid-proxy and sue to redeem pill. It will reveal activism play and stock price will change dramatically.
    - Margin note: [understand this]
- DE law more skeptical about tender offers vs. activism (voting viewed as more special vs. just selling your share)
    - Circled: protect activism / voting → tenders

### Capital lock-in

- More capital providers / stockholders → More conflicts between management and stockholder [Higher wedge for management]
    - Less shareholders = less conflicts (more control over management).
- Corporate law: Dispute between capital providers and management
- Public companies account for less than ~50% value in economy

### PE/VC

- Problems regarding management entrenchment don't exist
    - Capital providers control who is on Board; No individual Board guarding its own interests antagonistic to Fund
    - Ex. VC Firms get "preferred" equity for investments → VC gets someone on Board
- **Implication: Dispute is among capital providers themselves (Preferred 1 vs. Pref. 2)**
