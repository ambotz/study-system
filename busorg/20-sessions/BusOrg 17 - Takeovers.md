# BusOrg 17 — Takeovers

## Readings

- [[Cheff v. Mathes (1964)]]

## Notes

- **Fiduciary duty litigation - Not good for dictating management overall decisions (duty of care)**
    - Mainly used to deter bad actors / self-dealing
- Better alternatives to controlling management decisions: (1) sale / takeover or (2) vote / shareholder activism

### Sale

- Shareholders selling stock
    1. Requires enough concentration to oust management
        - Buyers acquiring for control are willing to pay a premium above market value (because they are buying control).
    - **Threat of aggregation** → Drives management to discipline themselves and keep control

### [[Cheff v. Mathes (1964)|Cheff v. Matthews]] (1964)

- Maremont secretly bought Holland stock on market
    - Once plan to acquire is revealed, stock price usually goes up
    - Price slowly rising with no earnings news or explaining variables → likely means someone is buying up the shares
- Maremont gave Holland the option to: (1) Buy back their shares at a premium or (2) entertain sale of Holland
    - Holland took the first option → Derivative case [Holland's purchase using corp assets, was harming the company]
        1. Can Board buy/sell its own shares → Yes (legally) §160
        2. Was the buying inappropriate in some way → Fiduciary question; this is the key thing the opinion turns on.

### Cheff/Holland Motivations

1. Protect shareholders against bad business plan
    - Margin: valid ✓
    - ? → necessarily economically? Less plausible (i.e. why would Maremont buy higher).
2. Protect non-shareholder constituents (salespeople, optics [?], landlords, etc.)
    - Margin: valid? ([[Shlensky v. Wrigley (1968)|Shlensky]])
3. Protect CEO/Director Cheff themselves
    - → Just need "articulable justification" to prove
