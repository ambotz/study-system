## Notes

### Voting / Control Charge

- Activist shareholders are the modern reincarnation of hostile bidders / corporate raiders
    - Instead of trying to outright buy 51% of company → buy a smaller stake and convince other shareholders to vote accordingly to gain control / gain money via decisions.
- **Fixed costs** of proxy fight are paid by activist investor

### Things that affect efficacy of shareholder voting

1. Insider Control
    - Dual class shares [Charter] → very powerful voting shares differentiated
        - Takeover / vote works less w/ this. Fiduciary Breach lit. more popular
    - Controlling stockholder (i.e. Zuck) = harder to force Change of Control
        - But is the more popular mechanism in this situation
    - Insider aggregation (non-legal)
        - Share accumulation by Board / insiders. Similar to Dual class, but no legal mechanism required
2. Staggered Board [Charter / initial Bylaws]
    - Double the cost for proxy because 2 proxy campaigns must be won back to back
        - Makes it harder for Activists to monetize on gain. IRR takes longer to achieve.
        - Must be created via legal mechanism
3. Retail Shares (non-legal)
    - Retailers vote with management 90% of time.
    - Retailers rarely vote anyway
4. Election Procedures
    - Board affecting election, especially when the above "variables" are unfavorable for Board

### NVIDIA Jensen Huang Example

- Board can always fire CEO; but if Huang had dual class → could fire Board, if Huang held enough shares

### [[Blasius Industries v. Atlas Corp (1988)|Blasius (1988)]]

- Blasius strategy: Extract value from Atlas through (1) Dividend recap or (2) Debt Acquisition / increased lever
    - Blasius share price would increase
    - Blasius plans to expand Board through written consent vote
- **Comparison to LBO / tender offer (Perelman-style): takeovers are employing same mechanism, just after control is fully acquired via shares vs. voting / management**

### Written Consent (§228)

- Blasius needs to acquire a "voting block" via convincing them / other shareholders to vote with Blasius

### Problems

1. Most public companies don't allow written consent [Charter]
2. Dispersed shares → very expensive if activist needs to convince different parties.
3. Bylaws require proposal to be filed way ahead of meeting → element of surprise diminished (plan to solicit written consent filed)

- Atlas response: Add new Board members before written consent; reaching control under Charter maximum

### What exactly can Blasius plead / use to sue Atlas?

- Atlas added directors within Charter and Bylaws
- Question remains: was the change motivated by written solicitation
    - **Note: If Atlas added two directors before Blasius filed → completely fine. This is present day Activist defense ex-ante. Impossible to draw the line elsewhere otherwise (see [[Moran v. Household International (1985)|Moran]]; [[Air Products v. Airgas (2011)|Airgas]])**
    - Margin note: Fold into theory / Exam note.

### Atlas legal rebuttals

1. No conflict so BJR → Obvi not true [?]
2. Protect shareholders [structural coercion, low price] → In [[Air Products v. Airgas (2011)|Airgas]], this worked.
    - Protect non-shareholders and protect management [not in play]
        - This motive is affirmed as its okay; STILL can't make a change as director when there is a **dispute in-progress** → Blasius NLC [?]
        - **Gets folded under [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]] after [[Coster v. UIP Companies (2023)|Coster]].**
