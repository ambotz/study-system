## Notes

### Justification of threat

1. Protect shareholders (coercion) → [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]] says this is ok/valid
2. Protect shareholders (low price)
3. Protect non-shareholders
4. Protect Board/Management themselves → not valid as seen in [[Cheff v. Mathes (1964)|Cheff]]/[[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]]

### All cash / all shares offer

- All cash / all shares offer ⇒ no coercion structurally for shareholders
    - So, Board can't really have pill in place forever. It is up to the shareholders to determine the price at some point

### [[Paramount v. Time (1990)|Paramount v. Time (1990)]]

- Time's goals: wanted to expand into entertainment, but at the same time retain Time's journalistic independence
    - Buccola pushed on this hard: this is a conflicting objective and seems like a proxy for Board just keeping control

### Warner/Time's talks

- CEOs Warner (Ross) and CEO Time (Nicholas) were in talks about merger; main issue was who was going to **stay CEO** (retain control)

### Deal

- Time creates merger sub; Warner will merge into sub
- Holders of Warner will get Time's stock
    - After proposed merger: Warner would have more stock in Time than Time itself (Warner holders have more control)
- **Stock-for-stock deal**
- **Rule: issuing 20% or more of stock = shareholders must vote**

### [[Paramount v. Time (1990)|Paramount v. Time (1990)]] cont.

- Side note: Oh! proxy statements went out. so whose voting?
- Paramount announces all cash tender to Time
    - This stops deal because Time shareholders would rather receive more cash and not get merged out of existence
- However, Time Board must unwind 15% trigger pill deal protection before tender offer can be formally given to shareholders
- **Time Board called higher price Paramount offer as non-sense because:**
    - Important ways to demean [?] higher offers
        1. Financing concerns (price too good; purely sabotage)
        2. Better course for corporation in the long-run through vision for corp. (i.e., special commitment to news/journalism) / long-term value

### Time's response

1. Renegotiates with Warner Board to go from stock-for-stock to instead an all-cash offer to Warner ($71)
    - **All-cash offer → NO VOTING required by shareholders. Remember: voting required only when issuing 20%+ for merger**
2. Paramount raises tender to $200
    - KEY: no structural coercion on all cash-offers; still Time's Board says no to redeeming pill because it's protecting long-term corp. interests

### Paramount sues

1. Time's Board not maximizing shareholder value under [[Revlon v. MacAndrews & Forbes (1986)|Revlon]]
    - [[Revlon v. MacAndrews & Forbes (1986)|Revlon]] and [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]] both implicated when Board tries to sell to **prevent** threat to corporation / implies defense
2. Time's Board was not responding to a cognizable threat under [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]]

### Holding

1. No actual sale of business = [[Revlon v. MacAndrews & Forbes (1986)|Revlon]] **not** triggered
    - Without this, wouldn't have a limiting principle when [[Revlon v. MacAndrews & Forbes (1986)|Revlon]] applies (i.e., issuing stock would be suspect)
2. There was (1) a threat to Time's shareholders and (2) proportionate
    - Time's restructuring [?] of deal after Paramount intervened was a response to a threat that was proportionate

### Threat to Time

1. Substantive coercion about strategic benefit (not structural) → Paramount's offer introduced timing uncertainty
    - On preserving culture: Buccola Q → can't shareholders determine culture of company?
        - Under BJR ([[Shlensky v. Wrigley (1968)|Shlensky]]), Board's actions are presumed in good faith. BUT, heightened scrutiny on 2nd transaction
    - **Instead, Court seems to be protecting non-shareholders or some other pathway to get back to deferring to Board**
    - Use the four justifications as a threat under [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]] **prong one** to get back to Board deference under heightened scrutiny

### [[Air Products v. Airgas (2011)|AirGas (2011)]]

- Important feature of AirGas Board: staggered
    - 3 clusters of Directors up to vote (i.e., 9 total members)
    - Effect: need two annual meetings to take control of Board
- Air Products launched hostile tender (will always be more than what the stock is currently trading at)
    - Air Gas had a poison pill at 15%
    - Staggered Board
    - 67% supermajority merger provision
- Air Products nominates its own 3 BoDs for annual meeting; shareholders vote Air Products members → still, Air Gas maintains control 6/9 of Board
    - Board says price too low ("Substantive Coercion") as a threat; won't redeem pill
- Holding: there was a threat under [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]] prong 1: substantive coercion (even though big price increase.) → Ask: does "threat" mean anything anymore?
- **Substantive coercion = price too low**
    - But in time, isn't this tucked under (prong 1 structural coercion)?

### [[Air Products v. Airgas (2011)|AirGas (2011)]] cont.

- Note/outcome: Air Gas was sold to another buyer for $140/share [?] a few years later → maybe Board was right
    - (?) Maybe quality of Boards have gotten better
- Lesson: Board can reject an offer simply because it's not enough money; even though no real coercion about offer
