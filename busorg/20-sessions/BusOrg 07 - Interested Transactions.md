## Notes

- Duty of care claims are essentially dead in litigation (unless M and A or certain injunctive relief)
- Parallel between trusteeship and corporate form

### [[Bayer v. Beran (1944)|Bayer v. Beran (1944)]]

- Directorate was small; insiders
- There was never a formal Board meeting authorizing the audio ad campaign
    - Other Directors addressed [?] / found out wife was paid only later

### Legal theories for relief

1. Agent alone, even CEO, doesn't have authority to spend this amount of $$ single-handedly
    - Court dodges question → says no Director "objected"
    - Kind of not a fair real understanding of approval from the Board based on Bueda [?]
    - → Board must have a meeting to make a formal decision
2. Directors were not independent / not disinterested in transaction; BJR doesn't apply
    - → Conflict identified, good faith presumption lost
        - But, company was able to rebut
        - Class comment: even though payment is fair, what stops Court from saying it was unfair to go with wife in the first place → very hard to prove it was motivated by conflict of interest

### Trustee per-se voidability

- **Trustee per-se voidability for conflict between trustee and 3rd party = automatically void is not alone grounds for invalidation anymore**, if cleansed by:
    - [[Interested director and officer transactions - 144(a)|144(a)]](1) Directors (disinterested; committee)
    - [[Interested director and officer transactions - 144(a)|144(a)]](2) Stockholders
    - [[Interested director and officer transactions - 144(a)|144(a)]](3) Court / fair by merit
- Boxed: "Entire fairness"
    - "Standard of review; not conduct"

### Entire fairness and definitions

- Entire fairness claims usually get a trial → discovery needed to understand process / procedures taken
- 144(e)(4) Interestedness → if someone has a material stake in the transaction
- 144(e)(4) Independent → relationship to a Board director / company not influencing decision

### Cleansing a transaction - OLD (144-post SB 21 renders this moot for most part)

1. Board / Committee voted by majority: fully informed, **uncoerced**, disinterested, **independent**
    - Margin note: both not in 144(a)(1) [?]
2. Disinterested shareholders: **fully informed**, **uncoerced**
    - Different from 144(a) language "fully informed, uncoerced"

- **Twice tested → legal authority and equitable**
- **Most actions are fiduciary duty (equity claims)**
    - Conflicted transaction that was cleansed → BJR
    - Not cleansed → Entire fairness

### New 144(a)

- 144(a)(1)
    - Independence is gone; just mentions disinterestedness now. Strong presumption of disinterestedness
    - Fully informed, disinterested → valid transaction
