## Notes

Game theory of two-tier offers (were determined coercive after Unocal, so mainly all cash or all stock offers now)
![[Pasted image 20261105125903.png]]

Tendering is therefore never worse and sometimes better — a dominant strategy — so _everyone_ tenders, the bid succeeds, and everyone ends up at $54… **even though if nobody had tendered, the bid dies and everyone keeps $60.** Individually rational, collectively self-destructive. That’s the prisoner’s dilemma, and it’s exactly the “threat” Unocal was reacting to: a structurally coercive, below-value bid that succeeds not because it’s a good deal but because shareholders are stampeded.
* Note: $60 is not necessarily what the stock is trading for at market, but more so what the stock is instrinsically worth*

### Two Tier

- Tier 1: Bidder becomes controller of the company; Target Co. merged into merger sub
- Tier 2: Remaining shares are cashed out with back-end securities / Bonds/Notes

### Reaction to takeovers in 80s

- State legislatures were not happy. Passed anti-takeover legislation requiring takeover attempts to be registered.
    - Why? Raiders would sell company for parts.
    - → Edgar v. Mite ruled this legislation was **unconstitutional**.
        - Leads to continued practice and debate.
- Easterbrook (pro-raiders) vs. Marty Lipton (pro-Board/Defense)
    - Easterbrook: Board be passive
    - Lipton: Board guard

### [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]] (1985)

- Mesa: Goal is to quietly buy up stock before tender offer is rendered
- Williams Act 13-5 [?]: Requires **disclosure** when shareholders own more than 5% of a company. [check: Williams Act disclosure requirement is Section 13(d)]

### Mesa's Two tier offer

1. Had 13% already; for remaining 37%, Mesa offered $54/share
    - Unocal must be trading well under $54
2. Remaining 49% → those shares will be tendered via bonds later
    - Cause Unocal Board to enter merger with Mesa's merger sub
    - Remaining shareholders will get "$54" in a note or junk bond worth $54.
        - Mesa will be able to pay off the notes (?) through operations
    - *Board's duty (after potential Mesa takeover) to ensure price is maximised ([[Revlon v. MacAndrews & Forbes (1986)|Revlon]])

### Unocal's Self Tender

- Unocal essentially becomes a competing bidder; conditional on Mesa getting 37% shares via tender
    - If triggered, Unocal will pay "$72" via notes
- *Game Theory: Best case scenario for shareholders is to get $72, but some will have to "sacrifice" and take the lower offer → idea is no one will sacrifice, so the condition will never be triggered
- Shareholders got mad → Board waived Mesa trigger → So shareholders do get a benefit
    - Board paying more than tender ([[Cheff v. Mathes (1964)|Cheff v. Matthews]]) for everyone's shares except Mesa
    - Discourages Mesa from going through with transaction (at least pay $72 now).

### [[Unocal v. Mesa Petroleum (1985)|Unocal]] (1985) - claims and holding

- Before Unocal's tender, Mesa sues
    1. Discriminatory tender, only excluding Mesa
    2. Directors' own shares were tendered/offered → Duty of loyalty since Directors were benefitting themselves (weak; not the main)
- Court Holding: Rapid trial escalated to Supreme Court Decision (2 weeks)

### [[Takeover defences - Unocal enhanced scrutiny|Unocal Test]]

- Intermediate Scrutiny standard when Board is responding to threat

1. **Reasonable threat** to Corporate Policy → Main prong
2. Responded proportionally to threat

### Motivations to Protect

1. Protect shareholders from bad offer
2. Protect non-shareholder constituents (workers, society, etc.)
3. Protect CEO/Board themselves (entrenching) - marked X
4. **Protect shareholders from coercive offer** - starred
    - Coercive offer - causes even rational shareholders, knowing they won't get fair value, to still tender.
        - Even if shares are worth more, shareholders are between a rock and a hard place in two-tier offers: (1) Low cash offer or (2) Notes if tender goes through.
        - If not reached, tender still gets SH market value (?)
        - T/ reached or not; NT/ reached or not

- No two-tier offers, since they can be labeled as coercive → Bidders start using all-shares/all-cash offer
