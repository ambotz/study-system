- Ultra vires → Beyond the power

### §102(a)

- §102(a) - outlines what needs to go into certificate
    - Purpose → can be very broad (i.e., all lawful acts)
    - Name (include Ass'n, incorporation, limited, etc.)
    - Address (so they can be served)
    - How many shares of stock issued total and what types of stock
        - Determines allocation of power
        - Total cap of shares that can ever be issued
        - Preferred shares → "Blank check preferred" → can issue shares in the future that has unknown/uncapped power
        - Helps the company keep discretion on power allocation

### §102(b)

- §102(b) - broad catch all and other things that can be put into the charter
- **102(b)(7) - exculpation of monetary liability from duty of care claims → only thing/main thing that charters capitalize on.** (what does NVIDIA's say?)

### §122

- §122 → enumerates powers corp. can have (broad)
    - Corp. can ~ do anything a person can do

### §141(a)

- §141(a) - Affairs of Corp. shall be managed by BoD.
    - **Gives BoD power over Corp.** (majority decisions; usually all members)
    - BoD - comprised by 1+ natural persons
    - BoD → Corp. → Agents
- Main actors: Entity, BoD, Stockholders, officers

### §109 Bylaws

- §109 Bylaws - operating agreement of corp. (more specified than cert.)
    - Annual meeting - must have an annual meeting (statutory mandate)
    - §109(a) [?] Shareholders must be allowed to change bylaws → but rarely ever happens. Instead, it's chosen/amended by the board.

### [[Gorman v. Salamone (2015)|Gorman v. Salamone (2015)]]

1. Amended bylaws
    - Written consent, via majority of stockholders vote
2. Written consent used to change bylaws
    - Gorman appoints himself as CEO

- **Why did Court rule this as invalid?**
    - 141(a) - Only Board can fire/hire CEO, as it's part of Corp. affairs
- What can Gorman do?
    - Amend Charter to allow him to fire CEO/replace
        1. BoD must propose amendment and (2) SH must vote/approve
        - Still 141(a) might not allow this (based on 122(18)?)
    - **Replace BoD**
        2. Fire BoD and install new Board that will change the CEO OR propose Charter amendment that SH can appoint CEO or the like

### [[West Palm Beach Firefighters v. Moelis & Co (2024)|Moelis & Co.]]

- Ken signed Stockholder agreement with BoD and Company/entity to give Ken power over Board (residual authority).
    - **Ken wanted to divest his shares but still retain control (contrast Gorman).**
- Why did Firefighters Pension Fund sue? (they didn't control much of the company)
    - Lawyers wanted to sue Co. and recover legal fees.
    - do biz recovery for Firefighters [?]
    - Shareholder derivative suit (Amazon [?] demand futility test)
- **After this case, 122(18) was passed by DE → allows stockholder agreements if Charter provision allows it** [check: §122(18) permits such stockholder agreements unless they contravene the certificate of incorporation or Delaware law, rather than only where a charter provision allows it]
    - Now a provision that allows BoD to allocate to Shareholders some power/discretion.
