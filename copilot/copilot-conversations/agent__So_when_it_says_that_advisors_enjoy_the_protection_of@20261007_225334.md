**ai**: Your first point is mostly right. Your second point mixes up two different doctrines.

**1. Care.** The standard for a director's breach of the duty of care is gross negligence ([[Smith v. Van Gorkom (1985)|Smith v. Van Gorkom]]), so gross negligence is enough to establish the breach. Two qualifications:
- The sentence you highlighted is about aiding and abetting liability, not about the care breach itself. A third-party advisor is not liable for gross negligence. The advisor must act with scienter, meaning it knowingly participated in the breach. See the Exceptions section of [[Aiding and abetting a fiduciary breach - knowing participation]].
- A care breach does not mean the directors pay. In RBC the directors breached the duty of care and paid nothing because § 102(b)(7) exculpated them. The breach still counted as the predicate for the advisor's liability.

**2. Loyalty.** "Fraud, illegality, or self-interest" is not the definition of a loyalty breach. Two different things are blended in that list:
- **Business judgment rule.** The list comes from the language in [[Shlensky v. Wrigley (1968)|Shlensky v. Wrigley]]. The court won't second-guess a decision absent "fraud, illegality or conflict of interest." That describes what rebuts the business judgment rule. It is not an element list for loyalty.
- **Loyalty.** A loyalty breach is conflicted or self-interested action, such as self-dealing or taking an improper personal benefit. It also includes **bad faith**, such as an intentional dereliction of duty or a knowing violation of law. Bad faith is the category your list leaves out.
- **Exculpation.** The § 102(b)(7) carve-outs are the clearest list of non-exculpable conduct. They cover breach of loyalty, acts not in good faith, intentional misconduct or a knowing violation of law, and an improper personal benefit.

**Your note.** The aiding and abetting note doesn't define loyalty at all, so you won't find that list there. If you want it in the vault, it belongs in a loyalty or business judgment rule module. I haven't checked whether one exists.
