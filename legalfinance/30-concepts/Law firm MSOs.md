## What it is

A **management services organisation**: a separate, typically nonlawyer-owned entity that owns
a law firm's business operations and sells services back to the firm, while the firm keeps
100% of the legal entity and of all legal fees. It is the live US answer to the prohibition in
[[Fee sharing and Rule 5.4]].

## How it works

- **Two entities.** The **MSO** holds finance, HR, technology, marketing, compliance, intake,
  billing and facilities. The **law firm** holds the practice of law, the client relationships
  and the fees. "[T]he MSO owns and operates the business operations, while the law firm
  retains ownership and control of the practice of law."
- **The investor buys the MSO**, capitalised by purchasing the firm's **non-legal assets**.
- **Money flows by service fee, not by fee share.** Fees are "most often structured as 'per
  lawyer' fees or 'usage' fees," from flat rates to rates varying by practice area and
  seniority, and must be at **fair market value** for services rendered.
- **Partners are paid** in "cash at closing and purchase-price payments" plus **rollover equity
  in the MSO**. Post-closing compensation **often decreases** initially, with increases
  expected over time.
- **Exit** in roughly **three to five years**, with paths set in the organisational documents
  and mechanisms to keep partners incentivised past the investor's departure.

**Why this is said to satisfy Rule 5.4**: the nonlawyer owns a services business, not a law
firm, and is paid for services, not out of legal fees. The lawyer's professional judgment,
client relationships and fees are untouched.

## Why it exists

Because the rules block the direct route and the demand is real. Rules 5.4 and 5.6 mean a firm
cannot sell equity and cannot restrain partner exit, so it has no permanent capital — see
[[The law firm as a capital structure]]. Meanwhile firms need capital for technology, AI and
contingency inventory, and partners approaching retirement want a liquidity event a partnership
cannot provide.

Note the historical symmetry. The safeguards Adams and Matheson proposed in 1998 — contractual
non-interference, separated control rights, existing ethics rules still binding the lawyers —
are exactly what an MSO builds. **Private ordering delivered the 1998 proposal's protections
while leaving the 1998 proposal's target intact.**

## Variants

- **Direct minority stake** where state law allows — Arizona since 2020 (KPMG approved
  February 2025), Utah's sandbox, and loosened rules in Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C.
- **MSO everywhere else**, which is the point of the structure.
- **Funder-owned MSOs** — Burford is pursuing both routes.
- **Healthcare and dental MSOs**, the template this is borrowed from, where the same
  corporate-practice-of-medicine prohibition produced the same workaround decades earlier.

## What can go wrong

- **"Fair market value" is doing all the work and is close to unfalsifiable.** No competitive
  market prices these services. If the fee is genuinely arm's-length there is no fee share; if
  it is set to sweep the firm's economics it is a fee share with extra steps — and nobody can
  easily tell which from outside.
- **Quasi-non-competes.** An MSA so burdensome that it restrains practice runs into **Rule
  5.6**. But the investor's return depends on the lawyers staying, and retention is the one
  thing that cannot be contracted for. That is an unhedgeable hole in the deal.
- **Independence.** The MSO "must not direct or regulate the lawyer's professional judgment,
  share legal fees or interfere with client relationships" — easy to state, hard to police when
  the MSO controls intake, billing and technology.
- **Confidentiality.** Client data sits in systems the MSO owns and operates.
- **Thin authority.** **Texas Opinion No. 706 (2025)** is the only ethics opinion squarely on
  law firm MSOs. One state bar committee is the entire body of law on a structure being built
  at scale, and the rules vary state by state.
- **Conflicts across a rollup.** An investor owning MSOs for several firms creates
  relationships no conflicts system was designed to see.
