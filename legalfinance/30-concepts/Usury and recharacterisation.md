## What it is

Usury statutes cap the interest chargeable on a **loan**. Litigation funding is structured
not to be one. The doctrine bites only if a court **recharacterises** the advance — looks
past the "sale," "purchase" or "non-recourse investment" label and finds a loan underneath.

Everything turns on that single threshold question, and the consequence is severe: in New
York a usurious contract is **void**, not merely repriced.

## How it works

Using New York, which governs most US funding documents:

- **N.Y. Gen. Oblig. Law § 5-501** — civil ceiling **16%**; **criminal usury 25%**; usurious
  contracts void.
- **The threshold**: "If the transaction is not a loan, 'there can be no usury, however
  unconscionable the contract may be.'" So the fairness of the price is irrelevant until the
  characterisation is settled.
- **The classical test for a loan**: repayment must be required "absolutely and at all
  events." A genuinely contingent obligation is not a loan.
- **The anti-evasion principle**: a usury defence still reaches transactions "disguise[d]" to
  conceal a usurious loan. The parties' label does not control.
- **Where the two meet** is the live space, and [[Fast Trak Investment Co v. Sax]] is the
  best statement of it: if the triggering condition is sufficiently certain to occur, a
  nominally contingent obligation may be treated as absolute. The court's phrase is that
  repayment was "all but guaranteed."

**The mechanism that collapses contingency is diversification.** One non-recourse advance
against one claim is genuinely at risk. An obligation spread across roughly eighteen matters
fails only if essentially all of them fail. The funder's own risk management is what converts
its product into something resembling lending.

## Why it exists

Usury law protects borrowers who cannot price credit, and it does so with a bright line
rather than a fairness inquiry because fairness inquiries are expensive and unpredictable.
The bright line is also its weakness: it is easy to draft around, which is why the
recharacterisation doctrine exists at all.

For this course the important point is **what usury does that champerty and unconscionability
do not**. Champerty asks about intermeddling. Unconscionability asks whether this bargain was
fair to this party. Usury asks a purely structural question — is this a loan — and if the
answer is yes the contract is void regardless of how fair it was. It is the cheapest doctrine
to run and the most destructive to the funder.

## Variants

- **Non-recourse advance against a single claim** — the structure most resistant to
  recharacterisation.
- **Advance diversified across many claims** — the Fast Trak problem.
- **Recourse to the lawyer's fees** — the secondary contracts in Fast Trak, where the client's
  deal was genuinely non-recourse and the **attorney** was the credit support. Also a
  [[Fee sharing and Rule 5.4]] problem.
- **"Sale and Repurchase Agreement"** — the label in
  [[Maslowski v. Prospect Funding Partners]]; a purchase of proceeds rather than a loan.
- **Statutory rate caps for consumer funding**, which sidestep characterisation entirely by
  legislating a ceiling on this product by name — Arkansas 17%, Tennessee 10% of the advance,
  West Virginia 18%, and New York's dual cap.
- **Outcome caps rather than rate caps** — New York's 25% of gross proceeds, which regulates
  the share rather than the rate and so does not depend on the loan question at all.

## What can go wrong

- **Relying on the label.** "Non-recourse purchase" is a characterisation the court may
  reject.
- **Diversifying into a loan.** The more the funder de-risks across matters, the weaker its
  argument that any individual obligation is contingent.
- **Taking recourse to the lawyer.** It converts a clean non-recourse consumer product into a
  near-certain obligation and adds a professional-responsibility problem on top.
- **Assuming the remedy is repricing.** In New York the contract is **void** — and whether a
  successful defence means disgorgement of principal or merely a rate cap was one of the two
  questions Fast Trak certified and did not answer.
- **Forgetting which state.** Rates, remedies and the recharacterisation test are all state
  law.

## Where it shows up

- [[Fast Trak Investment Co v. Sax]] — the framing of the whole problem, certified to the New
  York Court of Appeals.
- [[Maslowski v. Prospect Funding Partners]] — the "Sale and Repurchase" label, and a $6,000
  advance compounding 30% every six months to a cap of $25,245.
- [[Skiba and Xiao - Consumer Litigation Funding as Payday Lending]] — why non-recourse is a
  difference in kind, and why an APR may be the wrong measure for a contingent instrument.
- [[GAO - Third-Party Litigation Financing market characteristics]] — consumer rates of 15% to
  18% of the amount funded applied every six months, and the state caps.
- [[Non-recourse capital]] — the structure the doctrine is testing.
- [[Policing funding agreements]] — the contested choice of instrument.
