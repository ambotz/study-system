## What it is

Whether the documents a funding relationship generates — the funding agreement, the diligence
memoranda, the communications between claimant, counsel and funder — are protected from
discovery. Everything in the disclosure debate rests on this, and **no court has settled it**.

## How it works

Three distinct categories, routinely run together and worth separating:

| Document | Protection argument | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Diligence memoranda and case assessments** prepared for the funder | Classic work product: created because of the litigation, containing "lawyers' mental impressions, theories and strategies" | Strongest |
| **Communications** between claimant, counsel and funder | Work product, plus common-interest doctrine where the funder and claimant share a legal interest | Contested; turns on whether a common interest exists before the deal closes |
| **The funding agreement itself** | Burford's argument: its terms "reflect the information provided in work product protected documents" | Weakest, and the one actually in dispute |

- **Waiver is the hinge.** Disclosing attorney work product to a third party waives the
  protection unless an exception applies. Funders use **NDAs** and argue common interest; whether
  either preserves the protection varies by jurisdiction.
- **The practical answer courts have reached is procedural, not doctrinal.** In camera
  submission, privilege asserted document by document, sealing available. A categorical
  objection at the threshold fails — [[In re Nimitz Technologies]].
- **Pricing is the real fight.** The objection to producing the agreement is not that it
  contains a memo; it is that a **multiple and a waterfall encode a sophisticated party's
  assessment of the case after diligence**. A defendant who reads them learns what the funder
  thinks the claim is worth.

## Why it exists

Work product protects the adversarial process: a party should not build its case for its
opponent. The funding relationship strains the doctrine because the funder is **neither client
nor counsel** — it is a commercial counterparty that must see the case to price it.

That is why the three-way split above matters. Nobody seriously argues a funder's internal
valuation memo is discoverable on demand; nobody seriously argues the *existence* of funding is
privileged. The whole dispute is about the document in the middle.

## Variants

- **Common interest doctrine** — does a funder considering an investment share a legal interest
  with the claimant, or only a commercial one?
- **In camera review** — the courts' working compromise.
- **Ex parte submission** — proposed by funders, rejected by LCJ and ILR as contrary to
  **Canon 3(A)(4)** of the Code of Conduct for U.S. Judges.
- **Redaction of commercial terms** while producing the rest.
- **Statutory override** — S. 3826 would require "copies of all funding agreements" in covered
  cases, mooting the question there.

## What can go wrong

- **Assuming Burford's account is the law.** Its guide states that diligence materials "are
  protected under the work product doctrine in the US" and that the agreement itself is too.
  The first is the better view; the second is a litigating position, and the guide presents both
  as settled.
- **Assuming Nimitz resolved it.** It did not. The Federal Circuit denied **mandamus** and
  routed privilege into in camera review without deciding whether anything was protected.
- **Forgetting the disclosure circle widens with the structure.** A single funder is one
  counterparty; a syndication adds participants; a **securitisation** adds an arranger, a rating
  agency and noteholders. Each is another party seeing case material. See
  [[Syndication, secondaries and securitisation]].
- **Insurance has the same problem and nobody discusses it.** A judgment preservation policy is
  a document about the merits of a pending case, underwritten after diligence.
  [[Litigation risk insurance]].
- **Treating this as settled anywhere.** Steinitz notes the split and declines to resolve it
  (p. 1082 n.38); the Advisory Committee subcommittee is still gathering information.
