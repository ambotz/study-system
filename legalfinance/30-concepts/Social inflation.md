## What it is

An insurance-industry term for **increases in liability claim costs that exceed what
economic inflation and exposure growth would predict**. Swiss Re's formulation: "the social
factors that drive increases in legal claims above and beyond economic trends."

The term is doing two jobs at once, and separating them is the whole skill. It is (1) a
measurement concept — the residual left over when you strip economic drivers out of claims
inflation — and (2) an advocacy frame that attributes that residual to litigation industry
behaviour. The first is a legitimate actuarial exercise. The second is a causal claim.

## How it works

- Start with observed growth in liability claim severity.
- Subtract what wage, medical and general price inflation would explain, and what growth in
  insured exposure would explain.
- Whatever is left is labelled social inflation. Because it is a **residual**, it absorbs
  everything the model does not specify — which is why the same number can be attributed to
  litigation funding, to jury attitudes, to attorney advertising, or to under-reserving in
  earlier years.
- The observable phenomena it is used to describe:
  - **Nuclear verdicts** — outsized awards, principally for non-economic damages.
  - Awards over $5m rising from **29% to 37%** of large general liability awards, 2010–2019.
  - Median general liability award from **$8.2m to $10.3m**.
  - Trucking verdicts from **$2.3m to $22.3m**, 2010–2018.
  - Combined ratios above 100 across general liability (105.7%), medical malpractice
    (117.5%) and commercial auto (104.1%) in 2020.

## Why it exists

The concept exists because insurers must **reserve** for claims years before they are paid,
and a persistent gap between reserved and paid amounts is an existential problem for an
underwriter. Naming and sizing the residual is a genuine actuarial need, and it predates
litigation funding by decades.

It matters on this syllabus because it is the frame through which the defence side argues
for regulation. If funding causes social inflation, and social inflation reaches consumers
through premiums, then funding is not a private contract between sophisticated parties — it
is a cost imposed on people who never agreed to anything. That is the strongest version of
the case against, and it is why the causation question in
[[Litigation funding and liability costs]] carries so much weight.

## Variants

- **Claims inflation** — the broader measure, including economic drivers.
- **Loss cost trend** — the actuarial term for the same underlying series.
- **Nuclear verdict** and **thermonuclear verdict** (conventionally $10m+ and $100m+) — the
  tail of the distribution rather than the trend.

## What can go wrong

- **Treating a residual as a cause.** Social inflation is defined by what it is not. Naming
  it does not identify its source, and Swiss Re concedes that funding interacts with trial
  bar strategy, juror attitudes and advertising.
- **Reading a combined ratio as claim inflation.** A ratio above 100 can reflect soft
  pricing, reserve strengthening for prior years, or reinsurance structure, not only rising
  claims.
- **Correlation across two secular trends.** Funding grew and verdicts grew over the same
  decade; so did attorney advertising spend, litigation finance's own cost of capital, and
  median jury awards in unfunded cases.
- **Selection in the duration finding.** Funded cases take longer, but funders select large,
  contested cases. Nobody on this syllabus has separated selection from treatment.
