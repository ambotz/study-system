## What it is

Three related medieval prohibitions on interfering in someone else's lawsuit, per Neuberger:

- **Maintenance** — "financial assistance to another person to institute proceedings."
- **Champerty** — the aggravated form, where the maintainer takes "a share of the proceeds."
- **Barratry** — being "a common mover or stirrer up of suits."

Minnesota's working definition of champerty: "an agreement to divide litigation proceeds
between the owner of the litigated claim and a party unrelated to the lawsuit who supports or
helps enforce the claim." On that definition, almost every transaction in this course is
champertous.

## How it works

- Where the doctrine survives, a champertous agreement is **unenforceable** — a complete
  defence, without any inquiry into fairness or price.
- It is **state law in the US**, and the answer differs by state. There is no federal
  doctrine.
- Three positions a jurisdiction can occupy:

| Position | Examples | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Never recognised** | Texas | Funding agreements face no champerty risk at all |
| **Abolished** | Minnesota, by [[Maslowski v. Prospect Funding Partners]] (2020); England, by Criminal Law Act 1967 ss. 13–14 | Unenforceable only on other grounds — unconscionability, usury, public policy |
| **Retained** | Several states, in varying strength | A live defence to enforcement |

- England's abolition kept a **public-policy saving**, so courts still police arrangements
  that "undermine the ends of justice." Abolition of the crime and tort is not abolition of
  judicial oversight.
- Even where abolished, **control is the residual limit**. Minnesota: it is "difficult to
  conceive of any stipulation more against public policy" than requiring a financier's
  permission to settle.

## Why it exists

Not to protect litigants from predatory capital — the reverse. The doctrines are feudal. When
barons could no longer wage private war they carried "unscrupulousness and trickery" into the
courts, and the prohibitions stopped the **powerful** from buying into other people's
litigation to harass rivals and extract land.

That origin is why the doctrine is so unstable today. Its historical target was the strong
using litigation against the weak; its modern application is to stop the weak from buying
capital to sue the strong. Neuberger's conclusion follows: "the exact reverse of the
prohibition is justified for the same reason."

The decline tracks the loss of that rationale. Bentham called the rules "barbarous
precautions" of a "barbarous age"; by the late nineteenth century courts were defending them
only on the vague ground that litigation might "be increased in a way that would be
mischievous." Meanwhile the law abandoned neighbouring prohibitions — **contingency fees**
were once champertous and are now universal, and **assignment of choses in action** was
barred on the same reasoning and is now routine. Keeping champerty for third-party funding
alone is a line drawn by history rather than principle, which is the argument that carried in
Maslowski.

## Variants

- **Statute of Westminster 1275**, plus eighteen further Acts to 1576 — the English statutory
  arc.
- **Criminal Law Act 1967, ss. 13–14** — abolition as crime and tort, with the public-policy
  saving.
- **Consumer litigation funding statutes** — the modern replacement channel. Maslowski counted
  eight states in 2020 (Arkansas, Indiana, Maine, Nebraska, Ohio, Oklahoma, Tennessee,
  Vermont); by 2026 add New York and the 2025–26 wave.
- **Unconscionability** — what Minnesota kept when it let champerty go.
- **Usury** — the parallel doctrine, reaching the same conduct through characterisation rather
  than through intermeddling. See [[Usury and recharacterisation]].

## What can go wrong

- **Assuming a national answer.** There is none. Texas never had it; Minnesota abolished it;
  other states retain it. The first question in any problem is which state's law applies —
  and forum selection is therefore a substantive term, not boilerplate.
- **Treating abolition as deregulation.** Maslowski replaced champerty with unconscionability
  and equitable review, and singled out settlement control as still contrary to public policy.
- **Importing English law.** England abolished by statute in 1967 and self-regulates through a
  funder code. The US did neither.
- **Forgetting it can be avoided by moving.** In [[Frankel - Sysco and Burford]] the claims
  ended up assigned to a Burford entity that refiled in **Texas**, which has never recognised
  the doctrine, under an agreement originally governed by New York law.
