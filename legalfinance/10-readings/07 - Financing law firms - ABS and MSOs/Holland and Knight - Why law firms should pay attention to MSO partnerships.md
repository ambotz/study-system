## Claim

- An MSO is a separate, typically **nonlawyer-owned** entity providing operational support —
  finance, HR, technology, marketing, compliance, facilities — to a law firm that retains
  100% ownership of the legal entity and of all legal fees.
- The division is the whole design: "the MSO owns and operates the business operations, while
  the law firm retains ownership and control of the practice of law."
- The MSO is paid a **flat fee or a fair-market-value fee for services**, never a share of
  legal revenue. That is what is said to keep it outside Rule 5.4.
- Four drivers: capital for growth and technology without selling firm equity; operational
  complexity (analytics, cybersecurity, HR) beyond in-house capacity; regulatory momentum from
  Utah, Arizona and others; and client demand for integrated legal and business services.
- The constraint is stated plainly: the MSO "must not direct or regulate the lawyer's
  professional judgment, share legal fees or interfere with client relationships."

## Who is speaking

A large firm's client alert, written to be found by law firm management committees
considering an MSO — and by the investors courting them. Holland & Knight would advise on
these transactions.

That shapes the balance. The ethical constraints are stated accurately and the risk section is
real, but the piece is framed as **how to do this properly**, not whether the structure is
sound. Note what it does *not* emphasise: private equity valuations and partner succession,
which the investor-facing [[Hunton - Understanding law firm MSOs]] treats as central. Two
client alerts, two audiences, two versions of the same structure.

## Seminar hooks

- **"Fair market value" is where the whole structure lives or dies.** If the MSO fee is a
  genuine arm's-length price for services, there is no fee share. If it is set to sweep the
  firm's economics, it is a fee share with extra steps. Nobody prices these services in a
  competitive market, so "fair market value" is largely unfalsifiable — and that is the
  question to put to the room.
- **Texas Opinion 706 (2025) is the only ethics opinion squarely on law firm MSOs.** One state
  bar committee is currently the entire body of authority on a structure the market is
  building at scale. It is on the Canvas reading list — get it, because everything in Class 7
  turns on what it actually permits.
- **Compare the safeguards to Adams and Matheson's 1998 list.** Contractual non-interference,
  separated control, existing ethics rules binding the lawyers. The MSO delivers the 1998
  proposal's protections while leaving the 1998 proposal's target — Rule 5.4 — untouched. Ask
  whether that satisfies the rule's reasoning or only its wording.
