## Claim

- Burford is moving from funding **cases** to owning pieces of **firms**, and positioning
  itself explicitly against private equity as the buyer of choice.
- Travis Lenkner, chief development officer: "We're not structured predominantly as a private
  equity fund, so we can be a very patient, permanent investor."
- Two routes: a **direct minority stake** where state law permits, and an **MSO** everywhere
  else — Burford owning back-office services and assets while legal work sits in a
  lawyer-owned entity.
- Where it is already possible: **Arizona** (where KPMG was approved in February 2025), plus
  loosened rules in **Utah**, **Puerto Rico** and **Washington, D.C.**
- No investment figure disclosed. Burford's own market capitalisation is given as about
  **$3 billion**, and Lenkner claims a "significant pipeline" of talks with leading and
  boutique firms.

## Seminar hooks

- **This closes the loop on Morley.** A firm cannot lock in capital because Rules 5.4 and 5.6
  forbid it; the capital arrives anyway, from the funder, as ownership of everything except
  the practice of law. The "patient, permanent investor" pitch is aimed precisely at the
  fragility [[Morley - Why Law Firms Collapse]] describes — permanent capital is the thing
  partner equity structurally cannot be.
- **Two movements in opposite directions, same year.** Big Law's share of new funding
  commitments fell from **37% to under 25%**, while Burford reaches for equity in firms. The
  reconciliation is probably disclosure exposure: a funded case is discoverable in live
  litigation, an MSO stake is not. Ask whether the regulatory pressure in Class 8 pushed
  capital from the case layer to the ownership layer.
- **Burford would be on both sides.** A funder that owns a firm's MSO and funds that firm's
  cases has an economic interest in the claim, in the fees, and in the enterprise. Set that
  against [[NYC Bar Formal Opinion 2018-5]], which forbids a funder a contingent interest in
  legal fees, and ask what is left of the rule when the same party holds the management
  company.
