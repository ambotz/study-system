## Claim

- Funders use AI for **sourcing and screening** — finding promising cases in court dockets,
  automating first outreach, running case management. It stops short of underwriting.
- Named tools and firms: **Legalist** ($901m AUM, founded 2016 by Eva Shang with Thiel
  Foundation backing) and its "**truffle sniffer**" algorithm sorting by court, judge and case
  type; **Qanlex** (2020) and its "**Case Miner**," screening and ranking cases in Latin America
  and Europe, investing **$100,000 to $3m** per case; **Apex Litigation Finance** in the UK,
  which experimented via CourtQuant and now uses predictive tools **mainly to reject** unsuitable
  cases; **Burford**, testing AI on closed cases and using it to identify specialised lawyers;
  and **Parabellum Capital**, with a proprietary portfolio management tool.
- The limit is unanimous among the people doing it. Shang: "It does not do things that an
  underwriter would normally do. There's still a very important human component." Power of Apex:
  AI is "only as good as the data that it has available to it."
- **The data problem is structural**: settlement amounts are confidential, so algorithms lack
  the dependent variable and must substitute proxies — jurisdiction, judge characteristics.
- Industry sized here at **$13.5 billion**.

## Who is speaking

Emily Siegel's sixth and last appearance on this syllabus. Trade press, well sourced, built on
named market participants who talk for their own reasons — here the funders themselves, who
have an interest in appearing technologically sophisticated to their own investors while not

Note what that mix produces: the *capabilities* are described by people selling them, and the
*limits* are described by the same people. The consistency of the limits across five
independent firms is the more credible half.

## Seminar hooks

- **Apex uses AI mainly to reject.** That is the most revealing detail in the piece and it
  inverts how the technology is usually described. Screening out is a far easier problem than
  pricing in — you need only identify the clearly unfundable, not value the fundable. It is also
  exactly what [[Antill and Grenadier - Financing the Litigation Arms Race]] predicts a
  non-recourse funder must do, and what
  [[Avraham and Sebok - Empirical Investigation of Third Party Consumer Litigant Funding]]
  measured at a 48% approval rate. AI is being pointed at the screening function the economics
  already required.
- **Qanlex invests $100,000 to $3 million per case.** That is an order of magnitude below
  Burford's $3m floor, and it is happening in Latin America and Europe. If Marra is right that
  AI relaxes the diligence-cost constraint, the evidence for it will show up as **smaller
  cheques**, and here is a funder already writing them.
- **The missing variable is the whole problem.** Confidential settlements mean the outcome
  these models are trying to predict is unobservable in about 90% of cases, so they train on
  proxies. That is the same data vacuum GAO found, Steinitz found, and
  [[Litigation finance as an asset class]] turns on — and it now blocks the technology too.
  The industry's opacity has become a constraint on its own tooling.
