## Claim

- Three live uses: **sourcing** (combining "public data with internal insights" to find lawyers
  and cases matching investment criteria — Burford ran a web-scraping "skunkworks project");
  **early-stage assessment**, where AI is "most effective in early-stage issue-spotting and
  identifying and assessing elements of the underlying cause of action"; and **internal
  modelling** of "potential profitability, duration, potential settlement value."
- One hard limit, stated plainly: AI "**is not yet capable of making investment decisions**."
- And the reason, which is the most important sentence in Class 9: "**models are only as good
  as the data fed into them**." Roughly **90% of commercial disputes settle confidentially**, so
  public data is about **10%** of what exists — and the missing 90% contains the **settlement
  values**, which is precisely the variable a pricing model needs.
- "[I]f models are constructed using flawed or limited data, the predictions they generate will
  also be flawed."

## Who is speaking

David Perla is a Burford executive, writing in the *New York Law Journal* and republished on
Burford's own site. A funder's public statement about its technology, aimed at general counsel
and at Burford's own investors.

The interesting thing is the **direction of the modesty**. A funder had every reason to claim
proprietary predictive advantage, and instead published the argument that the data required
does not exist. That is either unusual candour or a competitive move — a large incumbent with
underwriters has reason to argue that underwriting cannot be automated, because automation
would let smaller entrants compete on cost. Both readings are available and worth putting to
the room.

## Seminar hooks

- **The 90/10 figure is the number to carry out of Class 9.** Nine of ten commercial disputes
  settle confidentially, so the outcome variable is missing for nine of ten observations.
  Every claim about AI pricing legal risk has to get past that, and no reading on this syllabus
  does.
- **The data vacuum is now load-bearing in four separate arguments.** GAO could not size the
  market; Westfleet's figures are voluntary and unaudited; secondaries have no benchmark pricing
  because marks are unauditable; and now models cannot be trained. **The confidentiality that
  protects the industry commercially is the same fact that limits its measurement, its
  valuation, its regulation and its tooling.** That is the through-line of the whole course and
  Class 9 is where it becomes undeniable.
- **Put Perla against Marra directly.** Marra says machine learning "decrease[s] the risk" and
  should therefore decrease the cost of funding. Perla says the data to do that does not exist.
  Both are funders, both wrote in 2024. Ask which claim a funder would make if it were true, and
  which if it were not — and note that the incumbent with the largest underwriting team is the
  one arguing automation cannot work.
