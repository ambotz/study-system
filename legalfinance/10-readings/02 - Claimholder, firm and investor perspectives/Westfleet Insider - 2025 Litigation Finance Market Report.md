## Claim

- New capital commitments **rebounded roughly 23% in 2025** after two consecutive years of
  contraction, though "market conditions remain tight relative to historical norms."
- The industry is **small and concentrated**: about **39 active commercial funders**, of
  which only **12 to 15 drive most deal flow**, against roughly **$16.1bn** of assets under
  management.
- **Big Law is retreating.** The 200 largest US firms took just under **25%** of new
  commitments as of mid-2025, down from **37%** the year before.
- This is the only recurring public measurement of the US commercial market, and it exists
  because a broker built it — not because anyone is required to report.

## Argument

- The report is a market census rather than an argument, and its structure is: size the
  commitments, split them by deal type and matter type, and report year-on-year movement.
- The narrative across editions is a **capital cycle**. 2022 was the peak; commitments fell
  16% in 2024 to a market "nearly 30% smaller than levels reached in 2022," with funders
  "ration[ing] their limited capital, resulting in an environment in which litigation
  financing was much more difficult to secure compared with prior years." 2025 is the
  rebound off that trough.
- **Portfolios dominate and are growing.** In 2024, portfolios were **67%** of new capital
  commitments against 33% single-matter, and portfolio deals averaged **$16.5m** against
  **$6.6m** for single-matter. Both averages rose sharply year on year ($9.9m and $4.8m in
  2023).
- **Patent litigation took 32%** of all capital commitments in 2024 — roughly a third of the
  market in one subject area, which no advocacy document on this syllabus mentions.
- The Big Law retreat is the story the trade press picked up, and
  [[Siegel - Big Law Cuts Back on Investor-Funded Lawsuits]] reports it from the same data.

## Evidence

- **Methodology, stated in the report.** Funders "submitted confidential information from
  which we aggregated a complete picture" through a third party, and "that third party
  shared no attributable data with anyone, even with Westfleet." Inclusion criterion: "the
  organization had substantial participation in the U.S. commercial litigation finance
  market." The 2024 edition covered the twelve months to June 30, 2024 — so the reporting
  year is **not** a calendar year, which matters when comparing to anything else.
- **Limits Westfleet states itself.** "The more granular data included in our report was
  only gathered from those funders that directly participated in our survey," and the report
  **excludes mass tort financing, consumer litigation financing, and non-US matters**.
- That exclusion is the single most important thing about this document. By Swiss Re's
  figures, **mass tort is 38% and personal injury 25%** of invested dollars. Westfleet
  measures the remaining slice. Any sentence beginning "the litigation finance market is
  $X" sourced to Westfleet is a statement about **US commercial funding only**.
- **Participation is voluntary and unaudited.** It is a blind aggregation of self-reported
  figures from funders who chose to participate, and "most of the major litigation funders"
  is not quantified.
- **The 2025 report is gated behind a download form.** The figures above come from the
  public landing page, from [[Siegel - Big Law Cuts Back on Investor-Funded Lawsuits]]
  reporting Westfleet's data, and from the publicly posted 2024 edition for methodology and
  the base year. **Pull the 2025 PDF and check the numbers against this file** before
  quoting any of it in the paper.

## Who is speaking

Westfleet Advisors is a **broker**, not a funder and not a neutral. It advises claimants and
law firms on sourcing litigation finance and is paid when deals get done. Charles Agee, its
chief executive, is the most-quoted market source in the trade press.

A broker's interests are unusual and worth naming precisely: it benefits from a **large,
liquid, competitive** market, so it has reason to publicise growth — and equally to
publicise funder concentration and opacity, because those are the conditions that make an
intermediary valuable. Westfleet's credibility rests entirely on the report being accurate,
since funders participate voluntarily and would stop if it were used against them. That is a
real discipline, and it is not the same as independence.

## Seminar hooks

- **39 funders, 12 to 15 doing most of the deals, $16.1bn under management.** Put that next
  to the rhetoric in Class 1. Swiss Re describes a force reshaping American liability law;
  the market doing it is smaller than a single mid-size private equity fund and is
  concentrated in about a dozen firms. Whatever is driving nuclear verdicts, the commercial
  funding industry is not large enough to be the whole story.
- **Patent litigation is 32% of commitments.** A third of the commercial market is in one
  subject area with its own economics — non-practising entities, fee-shifting under § 285,
  and the disclosure fight in [[In re Nimitz Technologies]]. The access-to-justice framing
  fits patent monetisation very awkwardly, and no reading on this syllabus takes that on.
- **Big Law's share fell from 37% to under 25% in one year.** Morley explains why a firm
  needs the capital; something made the largest firms want it less. Ask whether that is
  regulatory scrutiny, conflicts policy, or the capital cycle — and note that the Class 7
  readings on MSOs describe firms reaching for outside money at the same moment the biggest
  ones are stepping back from funding.
