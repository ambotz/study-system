## Claim

- Federal **statutory** disclosure, aimed at the aggregate forums: **class actions,
  multidistrict litigation**, and large coordinated federal proceedings involving **one hundred
  or more cases**.
- Disclose the funder's **identity**, whether the funder is a **foreign state, foreign person,
  sovereign wealth fund, or a commercial enterprise owned or controlled by a foreign entity**,
  and **copies of all funding agreements** — to the court and to the named parties.
- Information is transmitted to the **Administrative Office of the United States Courts**, which
  publishes an update **every 120 days** identifying foreign funders in covered civil actions,
  with case names, courts and funding amounts.
- Substantive restrictions beyond disclosure: funders are barred from **influencing litigation
  strategy** or accessing **protected discovery material** without express court authorisation,
  and courts may hold violators in **contempt**.
- **Version note:** as introduced, February 2026. This is the successor to earlier LFTA
  versions that Steinitz described in 2019 as reaching class actions and MDL. Record which print
  you are describing.

## Argument

- **Forum selection is the design choice.** Where LCJ and ILR would amend Rule 26 to reach
  **every** federal civil case, this bill reaches only aggregate proceedings. That tracks
  Steinitz's own factor 3 — in "class and mass cases, wherein the lawyers rather than the
  clients drive and control the case," the claimant cannot police the funding and the court must.
  The bill and its leading academic critic agree about where the problem is worst.
- **It goes further than disclosure in two respects**, and these are easy to miss:
  - **A conduct rule.** Barring funders from influencing litigation strategy is a **substantive
    prohibition on control**, not a transparency measure — the federal analogue of what
    [[New York Consumer Litigation Funding Act]] did for consumers by statute and what
    [[Maslowski v. Prospect Funding Partners]] flagged as against public policy.
  - **An information barrier.** Blocking funder access to protected discovery material without
    court authorisation addresses a term LCJ found in its nine contracts — mandatory sharing of
    discovery documents with the funder.
- **The foreign-funding apparatus is new and is the centre of gravity.** Identification of
  foreign states, foreign persons, sovereign wealth funds and foreign-controlled enterprises,
  plus a **public 120-day register** maintained by the AO with case names, courts and amounts.
  No earlier LFTA version had a public register.
- **Enforcement by contempt**, rather than by evidentiary sanction or fee-shifting.

## Evidence

- **A bill, not law.** Introduced February 2026 by Grassley with Tillis, Kennedy and Cornyn —
  four Republican Judiciary and Finance members, and note that **Tillis is also the sponsor of
  the excise tax bill** in Class 5. The same coalition is running disclosure and taxation in
  parallel, which is worth saying out loud: two instruments, one strategy.
- **congress.gov disallows automated retrieval (robots.txt) and GovTrack returned 403**, so the
  provisions above come from contemporaneous analysis of the introduced text rather than from
  the text itself. **Pull the bill from the syllabus link and confirm the operative language
  before quoting it in the paper** — particularly the 100-case threshold, the 120-day publication
  cycle, and the exact wording of the influence-on-strategy prohibition.
- **No findings section is reported** and no evidence of foreign-funded litigation harming US
  interests is cited in the coverage. The premise is asserted.
- **That premise is exactly what Steinitz refused to endorse** when the House Oversight
  Committee invited her to. She called the anticompetitive and foreign-interest concerns
  "hypothetical and future-looking," and said these "outlier scenarios" "should not drive
  regulation," while allowing that judges should reach them when they arise. This bill makes the
  outlier the organising principle.

## Who is speaking

Chuck Grassley chairs or sits senior on Judiciary and has pursued transparency legislation for
decades; the co-sponsors are the same tort-reform bloc behind the excise tax and the Chamber's
Rule 26 campaign. The Chamber ILR issued a press release welcoming it, which locates the bill
squarely in the coalition behind
[[LCJ and ILR - Proposal to amend Rule 26 on TPLF disclosure]].

Read the foreign-funding provisions as doing two jobs. One is substantive — there is a real
question about sovereign wealth funding US litigation. The other is coalitional: national
security broadens the constituency for a measure that would otherwise divide on
plaintiff/defendant lines, and a **public register** generates recurring news coverage that
sustains political attention. Both can be true at once.

## Seminar hooks

- **Two bills, one coalition, two instruments.** Tillis sponsors the **40.8% excise tax**
  (Class 5) and co-sponsors this. Taxation needs no court to decide anything and can move
  through reconciliation on a simple majority; disclosure needs a rule or a statute and has
  stalled for a decade. Ask which is actually the live threat to the industry — and note that
  the answer is probably the one nobody is debating on the merits.
- **The bill quietly bans control, and nobody calls it that.** "Restricts funders from
  influencing litigation strategy" is not a transparency provision; it is the federal version of
  the New York consumer prohibition, applied to commercial funding in aggregate cases. That is a
  far larger intervention than disclosure, buried in a bill named for transparency.
- **A public register is a different animal from disclosure to a court.** Steinitz's whole
  framework turns on *to whom* — tribunal only, tribunal and parties, or public — and treats
  public disclosure as the most intrusive setting, justified rarely. S. 3826 selects the most
  intrusive setting for one category of funder, **by status rather than by conduct**, every 120
  days. Whether foreign status alone justifies that is the sharpest question in Class 8.

## Citable detail

- Litigation Funding Transparency Act of 2026, S. 3826, 119th Cong. (introduced Feb. 2026).
  Sponsor: Sen. Chuck Grassley; co-sponsors Sens. Tillis, Kennedy and Cornyn.
- Covered proceedings: class actions, multidistrict litigation, and coordinated federal
  proceedings involving **100 or more cases**.
- Disclose: funder identity; whether the funder is a foreign state, foreign person, sovereign
  wealth fund, or "a commercial enterprise that is owned or controlled by a foreign entity";
  and copies of all funding agreements — to the court and to the named parties.
- Transmission to the **Administrative Office of the United States Courts**, which publishes
  every **120 days** a list identifying foreign funders in covered civil actions, with case
  names, courts and funding amounts.
- Funders restricted from influencing litigation strategy and from accessing protected discovery
  material without express court authorisation; **contempt** available against violators.
- Predecessor versions described in [[Steinitz - Follow the Money]] at p. 1077 as requiring
  disclosure "in class actions and multidistrict litigation in federal courts to the court and
  to all parties," following the Fairness in Class Action Litigation Act of 2017.
