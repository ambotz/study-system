## Rule

- A district court has **inherent authority to investigate who is really behind a case** —
  funding sources, real parties in interest, compliance with its own standing orders, counsel's
  compliance with professional conduct rules, and possible fraud on the court.
- Those concerns are "**related to potential legal issues in the case**, subject to the
  'principle of party presentation'" and to the court's authority over its own functioning.
  They are not idle curiosity: the district court "did not seek information simply in order to
  serve an interest in public awareness, independent of the adjudicatory and court-functioning
  interests reflected in the stated concerns."
- **A blanket privilege objection does not defeat the inquiry.** Where **in camera** review is
  available and privilege can be asserted document by document with sealing requested, there is
  an adequate alternative remedy and mandamus fails.
- Practical upshot: **procedure, not doctrine.** The court never decides whether funding
  documents are privileged or work product; it decides that the question is answered
  document-by-document in camera, not by a categorical objection at the threshold.

## Facts

Nimitz Technologies LLC filed patent infringement suits in the District of Delaware — four
consolidated cases before **Chief Judge Colm F. Connolly**. Nimitz named **Mark Hall** as its
sole owner.

In April 2022 Chief Judge Connolly issued two standing orders applying to his cases:

1. **Ownership disclosure** — identify "every owner, member, and partner" through the complete
   chain of ownership, until every individual and corporation with a direct or indirect
   interest is disclosed.
2. **Third-party funder disclosure** — identify the funder, state whether it has approval
   rights over settlement, and describe the nature of its financial interest.

Nimitz **denied having third-party funding**. The court then found evidence of undisclosed
relationships with **IP Edge LLC** — an entity that arranged patent assignments to a series of
plaintiff LLCs — and with **Mavexar**, whose relationship to Nimitz was unclear. Hall appeared
connected to IP Edge by email.

By order of **November 10, 2022** the court directed Nimitz to produce communications between
Hall, Mavexar and IP Edge; correspondence between Nimitz's counsel **George Pazuniak**, Mavexar
and IP Edge; documents on Nimitz's formation, assets, scope of liability and settlement
discussions; and **monthly bank statements**.

## Court Ruling

**Petition for mandamus denied** (Lourie, Reyna and Taranto, JJ., Dec. 8, 2022).

- **Relief sought.** Nimitz asked the Federal Circuit to vacate the November 10 production
  order and to **terminate the district court's investigation** outright, arguing the order
  would compel production of attorney–client privileged material and work product.
- **Standard.** The three-part *Cheney* test: no adequate alternative remedy; a "clear and
  indisputable" right to relief; and appropriateness in the circumstances. Nimitz failed all
  three.
- **Adequate alternative.** Nimitz could submit the materials for **in camera inspection**
  while asserting privilege and requesting sealing. That is an adequate route, so mandamus is
  unavailable.
- **No clear right.** "While Nimitz asks the court to terminate the district court's inquiry
  under the standing orders, it has not shown a 'clear and indisputable' right to such relief."
- **The district court's interests were legitimate.** Its concerns — counsel's compliance with
  professional conduct rules, compliance with court orders, hidden real parties in interest,
  and potential fraud on the court — are "related to potential legal issues in the case."
- **Rejected:** the blanket privilege argument. Privilege is asserted and adjudicated document
  by document, not raised as a categorical bar to the inquiry.

**Not decided:** whether any particular document is privileged or work product; whether the
standing orders are valid as a general matter; anything about the merits of the patents.

## Context

Nimitz is the disclosure fight arriving **through the back door**. Every other item in Class 8
argues about a rule — Steinitz's balancing test, LCJ and ILR's Rule 26 amendment, S. 3826,
N.J. L. Civ. R. 7.1.1. Nimitz shows what happens when a judge does not wait: a standing order,
an inquiry, and an appellate court declining to stop it.

It is also a **patent** case, and that matters more than it looks. Patent litigation is
**32% of US commercial funding commitments** by Westfleet's 2024 figures — a third of the
market. The NPE structure Connolly was probing, where an entity like IP Edge assigns patents
into thin LLCs that sue, is the place where "who is the real party in interest" is hardest to
answer and where the access-to-justice framing fits worst. The same structural problem
[[Parikh - Opaque Capital and Mass-Tort Financing]] describes in mass torts — money at the
entity level, the nominal claimant a shell — appears here in patent.

The doctrinal contribution is narrower than the case's reputation. Nimitz does **not** hold
funding agreements discoverable, and does **not** resolve work product. It holds that a court
may ask, and that a party must answer through the ordinary privilege machinery rather than by
refusing at the threshold. That is why both sides cite it: the defence bar for the authority to
investigate, funders for the preservation of in camera review and document-by-document
assertion. See [[Work product and privilege in litigation funding]].

## Class layer

### Posture

- **Federal Circuit, on petition for writ of mandamus** — an extraordinary writ, not an appeal.
  The standard is deliberately severe, so a denial says relatively little about the merits.
- Panel: **Lourie, Reyna and Taranto**, JJ. Order dated December 8, 2022, No. 2023-103.
- The petition asked for two things and the second is the ambitious one: vacate the production
  order **and terminate the investigation**. Asking an appellate court to shut down a district
  judge's inquiry into compliance with his own standing orders was always going to be hard.
- The posture is why the language is permissive rather than prescriptive. The court is saying
  *this is not clearly wrong*, not *this is right*.

### Facts

The entity structure is the point:

| Entity | Role |
|---|---|
| **Nimitz Technologies LLC** | Named plaintiff; Mark Hall its sole owner of record |
| **Mark Hall** | Sole owner; appeared connected to IP Edge by email |
| **IP Edge LLC** | Arranged patent assignments into plaintiff LLCs |
| **Mavexar** | Relationship to Nimitz unclear — the subject of the inquiry |
| **George Pazuniak** | Nimitz's counsel |

Nimitz's initial denial of third-party funding is what escalated matters. The court did not
begin with a document demand; it began with a disclosure statement it had reason to doubt.

### Issue

1. May a district court, under its own standing orders and inherent authority, compel a
   plaintiff to produce communications, formation documents and **bank statements** to
   investigate ownership, funding and real parties in interest?
2. Does a claim of attorney–client privilege and work product entitle the party to mandamus
   terminating that inquiry?

### Holding

Mandamus denied on all three *Cheney* prongs. In camera review with document-by-document
privilege assertion is an adequate alternative; no clear and indisputable right to terminate
the inquiry; the district court's stated concerns are legitimate and case-related.

### Rule

- **Mandamus standard**: *Cheney v. U.S. District Court* — no adequate alternative, a "clear
  and indisputable" right, and appropriateness.
- **Inherent authority**: a court may investigate compliance with its orders, counsel's
  compliance with professional conduct rules, hidden real parties in interest, and potential
  fraud on the court.
- **Limiting principle**: the inquiry must serve "adjudicatory and court-functioning interests"
  rather than "an interest in public awareness" standing alone. That sentence is the boundary,
  and it is the one a funder resisting a standing order would build on.
- **Privilege**: preserved but not self-executing. In camera submission plus sealing requests is
  the mechanism; a categorical objection is not.

### Reasoning

The court does the minimum. It never reaches whether funding documents are work product,
because on mandamus it does not have to — the existence of in camera review disposes of the
first *Cheney* prong, and the legitimacy of the district court's stated concerns disposes of
the second.

That restraint is substantive in effect. By routing the question into in camera review, the
Federal Circuit made **procedure** the answer to a doctrinal question nobody has settled. A
party who objects still gets privilege adjudicated; it just does not get to refuse at the
threshold, and the judge sees the documents in the meantime. Steinitz leaves work product
unresolved at p. 1082 n.38; Nimitz shows that courts are managing without resolving it.

Notice also what the court declines to say. It does not bless the standing orders in general
terms, and it does not endorse routine funding discovery. The opinion is written narrowly
enough that both camps can quote it, which is exactly what has happened.

### Dissent / concurrence

None. Unanimous order.

### Cold-call notes

- **What did the Federal Circuit hold?** That **mandamus** was unavailable. Not that funding
  agreements are discoverable, and not that they are unprivileged. The commonest error in
  discussing this case is overstating it.
- **What was the district court actually worried about?** Four things, and they are not
  "funding" as such: compliance with its orders, counsel's professional conduct, hidden real
  parties in interest, and fraud on the court.
- **What is the limiting principle?** The inquiry must serve adjudicatory and court-functioning
  interests, not "public awareness" alone.
- **What happens to privilege?** In camera, document by document, with sealing available. The
  blanket objection fails.
- **Why bank statements?** Because the question was whether Nimitz was a real entity with real
  assets or a shell — an ownership question, not a funding-terms question.
- **How does this differ from [[New Jersey Local Civil Rule 7.1.1]]?** New Jersey is a
  **district-wide rule** requiring a short statement automatically. Connolly acted by
  **standing order** and then by **targeted inquiry** once he doubted the statement. Same
  objective, two very different instruments.
- **Why does it matter that this is patent?** Patent is 32% of US commercial funding
  commitments, and the NPE shell structure is where "who is the real party in interest" is
  hardest to answer.
