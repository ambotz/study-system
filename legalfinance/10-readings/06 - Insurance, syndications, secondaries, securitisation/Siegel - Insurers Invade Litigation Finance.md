## Claim

- Insurers are moving into territory litigation funders had to themselves, competing on
  product **and** hiring funders' people.
- The market they are entering is sized here at **$13.5 billion**.
- The funders are not fighting for all of it. Stephen Kyriacou of Aon: "Funders have started
  to kind of cede that ground to us and focus on other avenues" — said specifically about
  judgment preservation insurance.
- The beneficiary is the **law firm**, which now has more than one kind of counterparty for
  the same risk.

## Who is speaking

Emily Siegel's fourth appearance on this syllabus, and the second in Class 6's orbit. Trade
press, well sourced, reliant on named market participants who talk for reasons of their own —
here brokers (**Aon**, **CAC Specialty**, **Willis Towers Watson**, **Marsh**), MGAs
(**Atlantic Global Risk**, **DUAL North America**) and funders (**Burford**, **Omni
Bridgeway**, **Woodsford**, **D.E. Shaw**, **Delta Capital**, **Corbin Capital**, **TRGP**).

Note that the quoted voice is a **broker**, who is paid when insurance is placed. "Funders
have ceded that ground to us" is a claim about market share made by the party gaining it.

## Seminar hooks

- **The $13.5bn figure needs a boundary.** Westfleet measures **US commercial only** — about
  $2.8bn of new commitments against $16.1bn AUM — and excludes mass tort and consumer
  funding, while Swiss Re put global TPLF at $17bn including both. A number quoted in trade
  press without a scope statement is not comparable to either. Ask what $13.5bn is counting.
- **Insurance and funding are converging from both ends.** Insurers write judgment
  preservation and adverse judgment cover; funders lend against insured claims at a lower
  cost of capital because the insurance is collateral. The interesting question is not who
  wins but whether the combined product is **cheaper for the claimant** — and whether that
  shows up in the claimant's share, which is where Class 1's 43%/55% fight was.
- **Watch the talent flow.** Insurers hiring from funders is the strongest evidence in the
  piece, because it is a costly signal rather than a quoted opinion. Underwriting talent moves
  to where the risk is priceable, and an adjudicated judgment is far more priceable than an
  unresolved claim.
