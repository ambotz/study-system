## Claim

- New York has enacted the most complete consumer litigation funding regime in the country,
  and it is **not** a single-instrument statute. It combines licensing, a price cap,
  prescribed disclosure, prohibited practices, an attorney gatekeeping duty, a rescission
  right and AG enforcement.
- It resolves by legislation three questions this course treats as open: **the funder may
  not control settlement**, **the price is capped**, and **the lawyer must certify that the
  client was told the terms**.
- It reaches consumer funding only. Commercial funding is untouched.

## Argument

The statute is a regime rather than an argument, and the useful way to hold it is by
instrument. **Version note: signed December 19, 2025 as Chapter 645; effective 180 days
after enactment; does not apply to agreements made before the effective date.**

| Instrument | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Licensing** | Registration with the Department of State before operating. $500 initial fee, $200 renewal every two years, expiring September 30. Character-and-fitness evaluation; bond of up to **$50,000** may be required |
| **Price cap** | Charges may not exceed **25% of gross proceeds** of the claim, where the attorney or consumer attests to the final gross recovery — **and** may not exceed the maximum APR for military consumer credit under **10 U.S.C. § 987(b)** |
| **Disclosure** | In **12-point bold**: amount funded; one-time charges itemised; maximum total owed; payment schedule at **180-day intervals**; notice of the 10-day cancellation right; statement that no prepayment penalty applies; warning against signing an unfilled contract |
| **Rescission** | Cancel within **ten business days** of the funding date by returning the full disbursement, "without penalty or further obligation" |
| **No prepayment penalty** | "No prepayment penalties or fees shall be charged or collected on consumer litigation funding," and such terms are unenforceable |
| **Control** | The funder "shall have no role in deciding whether, when and how much" the claim settles for; the attorney retains independent professional judgment; the company may not "interfere" |
| **Prohibited practices** | No referral fees to attorneys or medical providers; no false or misleading advertising; no referring consumers to specific attorneys; no funding a consumer with a prior unsatisfied funding unless the earlier funder is paid; no waiver of jury trial rights; no colluding with lawyers to induce a client to terminate counsel |
| **Attorney acknowledgment** | Written confirmation that the disclosures were reviewed with the consumer; that the attorney is on contingency; that **all proceeds pass through the attorney trust account or settlement fund**; that no referral fee was received from the funder; and that no tax or financial advice was given. **The contract is void** if the disbursement acknowledgment is missing |
| **Enforcement** | Willful violation forfeits **both** the funded amount and the charges in that case; civil penalties up to **$5,000 per violation**; enforced by the Attorney General |

## Evidence

- This is enacted law, so the question is not whether the claims are supported but **what
  the drafting actually does**. Three provisions repay close reading:
  - **The two-part cap.** 25% of gross proceeds is an *outcome* cap; the § 987(b) military
    APR ceiling is a *rate* cap. They bind in different cases — the outcome cap bites on a
    large recovery, the rate cap on a long one. Work out which binds first on a given fact
    pattern before saying what the statute costs a funder.
  - **"Gross proceeds," attested.** The cap is measured against gross recovery, not net of
    attorney fees, and it depends on an attestation. Who attests and what happens if the
    attestation is wrong are both worth checking in the text.
  - **Voiding for a missing acknowledgment** is an unusually severe remedy. It makes the
    plaintiff's own lawyer the condition of the funder's enforceable contract.
- **The chapter amendment, S.8808, matters.** New York chapter amendments routinely alter
  the bill as passed, and this file records the regime as described on the Senate page.
  **Read S.8808 directly against S.1104-A before relying on any figure here** — that is
  exactly the version-control problem the class override warns about.
- Untested. Effective 180 days after December 19, 2025, so roughly mid-June 2026, and
  prospective only. No enforcement history exists.

## Who is speaking

A state legislature, enacting after a multi-year campaign in which the insurance and
defence industries pressed for restriction and the funding industry resisted. The statute's
shape shows both sides leaving fingerprints: licensing, caps and AG enforcement are the
restrictionist agenda; confining it to **consumer** funding and leaving commercial funding
alone is the industry's most important win.

Note what it does **not** do. There is no disclosure to opposing parties or to courts —
nothing here touches the Class 8 fight. The disclosure is to the **consumer**, which is
consumer-protection regulation, not litigation regulation.

## Seminar hooks

- **New York enacted Skiba and Xiao's proposals and the remedy they argued against, in one
  statute.** They wanted a plain repayment amount and date plus attorney acknowledgment, and
  said characteristic restrictions "hurt low-income borrowers and are easily evaded." New
  York took the disclosure, took the attorney acknowledgment, and added a 25% cap. Whether
  supply contracts in New York after mid-2026 is now an observable natural experiment, and
  Avraham and Sebok's data gives a pre-period baseline.
- Run the **cap against the actual numbers**. Avraham and Sebok found a median embedded rate
  of 115% over the funding period but a median *realised* return of 50%. A 25%-of-gross cap
  sits well below both. If the realised return was already only half the contracted one
  because of haircuts, a binding cap on the contract may change the price much less than it
  appears — or it may remove the cushion that funded the haircuts in the first place.
- The statute makes the **plaintiff's lawyer the gatekeeper**, and voids the contract
  without the acknowledgment. That is a quiet but large shift: it converts a consumer
  protection problem into a professional responsibility problem, and it means the funder's
  enforceable rights depend on a third party's paperwork. Ask what a funder does when the
  lawyer simply will not sign — and note that Avraham and Sebok found pricing already turned
  on which firm the client used.

## Citable detail

- New York Consumer Litigation Funding Act, 2025 N.Y. Laws ch. 645 (S.1104-A / A.804-C), as
  amended by S.8808. Signed December 19, 2025. Effective 180 days after enactment;
  prospective only.
- Registration with the Department of State; $500 initial, $200 biennial renewal expiring
  September 30; character and fitness review; bond up to $50,000.
- Charges capped at **25% of gross proceeds** on attestation of final gross recovery, and at
  the maximum APR for military consumer credit under **10 U.S.C. § 987(b)**.
- Disclosures in **12-point bold**: amount funded; itemised one-time charges; maximum total
  owed; payment schedule at 180-day intervals; 10-day cancellation notice; no-prepayment-
  penalty statement; warning against signing an unfilled contract.
- "No prepayment penalties or fees shall be charged or collected on consumer litigation
  funding."
- The funder "shall have no role in deciding whether, when and how much" the claim settles
  for, and may not "interfere" with the attorney's independent professional judgment.
- Prohibited: referral fees to attorneys or medical providers; false or misleading
  advertising; referring consumers to specific attorneys; stacked fundings without
  satisfying the prior funder; jury-trial waivers; collusion to induce termination of
  counsel.
- Attorney acknowledgment must confirm review of disclosures, contingency representation,
  proceeds through trust account or settlement fund, no referral fee, no tax or financial
  advice. Contract **void** without the disbursement acknowledgment.
- Rescission within **ten business days** by returning the full disbursement.
- Willful violation: forfeiture of funded amount and charges; civil penalties up to **$5,000
  per violation**; AG enforcement.
