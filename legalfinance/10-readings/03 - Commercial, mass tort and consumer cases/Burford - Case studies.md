## Claim

- Worked examples of Burford's three product shapes, told from the client's side: a problem,
  a structure, an outcome.
- **The assignment is to choose two topics and discuss them**, so this file is a menu and a
  recommendation rather than a summary.
- Every study is written by the funder about its own successful deals. There is no
  loss-rate, no declined deal, and no transaction that disappointed — which is the single
  most important thing to say about them out loud.

## Who is speaking

Burford, in origination mode. These are sales collateral in narrative form: the client is
sympathetic, the structure is elegant, the outcome is good. That does not make any
individual account false, and the structures described are real and standard. It does mean
the set is **selected**, and selection is the whole methodological problem in this market —
the same problem GAO ran into, and the reason nobody knows the industry's loss rate.

Read them for **mechanics**, which are accurate and useful, and not for evidence about
outcomes, which they are not.

## Seminar hooks

- **Pick one monetization and one portfolio study.** Those are the two structures the
  October 13 term sheet exercise will turn on, and they are the two that Class 1's readings
  described only in the abstract. *Immediate capital for family business* or *Reallocating
  cash from pending claim to revenue-generating investment* gives you the monetization
  mechanics; *De-risking pursuit of opt-out claims* or *Leveraging portfolio finance to
  pursue securities claims* gives you cross-collateralisation with a real fact pattern.
- **Patent and IP is four of the twelve**, and that is not an accident of curation — patent
  litigation was **32% of all US commercial funding commitments** in Westfleet's 2024 data.
  A third of this market is one subject area, and the access-to-justice framing fits patent
  monetisation very badly. Raising that is a good use of the case-study slot.
- **"Representing client interests at settlement negotiation table"** is the study to read
  against [[Burford - Legal Finance 101]]'s claim that Burford is "a passive outside
  investor" with no "rights to control settlement." A title that advertises the funder's
  presence at settlement negotiations is in tension with the control language, and it is the
  same tension [[Parikh - Opaque Capital and Mass-Tort Financing]] presses through Sysco.
- What is absent from all twelve: consumer funding, mass torts, and any case that lost.
  Burford does not operate in the first two — which is itself the point, since Classes 1 and
  3 are largely about products Burford does not sell.
