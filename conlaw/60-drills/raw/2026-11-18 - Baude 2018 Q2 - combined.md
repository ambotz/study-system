# Baude 2018, Question Two — 50 points, 1750 words

Two parts, two openings. 3 min read · 4 min outline · 7 min on the Part A
opening · 7 min on the Part B opening.

## Prompt

Federal law provides a criminal defense attorney to all persons charged with serious crimes who are financially unable to afford one. The defense attorneys are a mix of public defenders, who are employees of the United States, and private attorneys who are compensated by the federal government for the cases they agree to take on.

Traditionally, both of these groups of attorneys have been heavily supervised by the federal courts in which they practice. Those courts, for instance, have appointed the head federal public defender in each office, decided on the criteria for allowing private attorneys to be appointed, reviewed requests for compensation and reimbursement, and decided how to allocate cases between all of those lawyers.

A federal committee spent years studying the operation of the criminal defense system and wrote a major report concluding that the system needs to be restructured. The report concludes that giving local federal courts the ability to govern the lawyers who practice in front of them creates numerous conflicts of interest to the detriment of the justice system.

The report proposes the creation of an **Independent Defender Commission**, within the judicial branch but outside the supervision of any federal court, and also outside the supervision of the judicial conference (which handles the administration of the federal courts).

The Commission would be given the independent power to establish any policies and rules necessary to carry out the federal defense system; the power to appoint the heads of each federal public defender office; responsibility for supervising, monitoring, and compensating defense lawyers; and the responsibility for submitting annual appropriations for the criminal defense system to Congress.

The Commission would have seven voting members. All of them would be appointed by the President, with the advice and consent of the Senate, for three-year terms **without removal** (except, of course, for impeachment). Their appointment would also have the following statutory constraints: No more than three of the commissioners could be sitting federal judges. No more than four of the commissioners could belong to any one political party. And the President must make his choice from a slate of candidates prepared by the Chief Justice of the United States and a group of federal defense attorneys.

**One commissioner dissented in part** from this proposal, and argued that instead of being appointed by the President and the Senate, the commissioners should all be sitting federal judges, and that rather than being selected from a slate by the President and the Senate, they should be selected by other federal judges. He suggested that the district judges in each regional federal circuit could each elect one of their judges to the commission. So the commission would have up to eleven members, with one district judge each from the First Circuit, Second Circuit, Third Circuit, Fourth Circuit, etc.

**Part A. (40 points)** You are a lawyer working for the Senate Judiciary Committee, which is considering legislation to implement the report. You are asked to prepare a short memo analyzing any constitutional problems raised by these two proposals.

**Part B. (10 points)** What would be the best way to structure the commission that avoids all of these constitutional problems?

## Afterwards, not before

How the two circulated answers opened Q2:

> "**A** / **Proposal 1** / **Congressional Power:** Congress has the power to regulate the criminal justice system, regardless of the large number of links in the chain between any enumerated power and that system (Comstock)."

> "**Part A** / I will begin by discussing the potential constitutional issues specific to the main proposal and the dissenting proposal before examining the problems raised by both proposals."

The second is the only genuine roadmap in the Baude set, and it pays off — its
third heading is literally "3. Common Concerns." Note also that the first uses
run-in bold labels ("Congressional Power:", "Removal:", "Appointment:") rather
than standalone headings, which is the most space-efficient structure in the
corpus: the navigational benefit of a heading for the cost of two words.
