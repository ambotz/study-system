## The phrases, with their authority

Congress may give the executive broad discretion in carrying out a statute, but may not hand over the power to ==make the law itself==.

**J.W. Hampton, Jr., & Co. v. United States (1928)**, the governing test, restated by Kagan's plurality in **Gundy (2019)**: a delegation is valid if Congress lays down an ==intelligible principle== to which the delegee ==is directed to conform==.

**American Power & Light**, quoted in **Gundy**, states the same test the other way: Congress must supply ==the general policy== the delegee must pursue and ==the boundaries of his authority==.

**Mistretta**, quoted in **Gundy**, on how rarely this bites: a delegation fails only where Congress ==failed to articulate any policy or standard== to confine the delegee's discretion.

**Gorsuch, dissenting in Gundy**, defines the legislative power he says cannot be delegated: the power to adopt ==generally applicable rules of conduct governing future actions by private persons==.

**Schechter Poultry (1935)** on why § 3 of the Recovery Act failed: instead of prescribing rules of conduct, it ==authorizes the making of codes to prescribe them==.

**Kagan's plurality in Gundy**, on the consequence of reading SORNA narrowly: the delegation was ==distinctly small-bore==, and so the constitutional question ==all but answers itself==.

## Name the authority from the proposition

The case supplying the governing delegation test, and the phrase it coined :::J.W. Hampton, Jr., & Co. v. United States (1928) — "intelligible principle"

The 1935 pair, the only two statutes ever struck down on nondelegation grounds :::Panama Refining and Schechter Poultry

The case holding Congress may let the executive "fill up the details," cited by Gorsuch in Gundy :::Wayman v. Southard

The 2019 case upholding SORNA's delegation to the Attorney General, and who wrote :::Gundy v. United States — Kagan, for a plurality of four

Who in Gundy would discard the intelligible-principle test, and in what posture :::Gorsuch, dissenting, joined by Roberts and Thomas

## Benchmarks worth citing

Four standards the Court has already upheld as definite enough — useful for arguing a delegation clears the bar
?
"Public interest" · "fair and equitable" prices · "just and reasonable" rates · air quality "requisite to protect the public health."

Gorsuch's three categories of permissible delegation, from his Gundy dissent
?
1. Congress makes the policy and lets the executive "fill up the details" (*Wayman v. Southard*).
2. Congress conditions a rule's application on executive fact-finding.
3. The delegation overlaps with power the executive already holds independently.

## The traps

Why is "Gundy reaffirmed the intelligible-principle test" wrong?
?
Only Kagan's four applied and endorsed it. Gorsuch, Roberts and Thomas would replace it; Alito concurred in the judgment endorsing neither analysis; Kavanaugh did not sit. **The test survived by default, not by a majority opinion.**

How did Kagan's plurality in Gundy avoid the constitutional question?
?
By construing SORNA's "specify the applicability" to mean *how* the statute applies to pre-Act offenders during the transition, not *whether* it applies at all. The Attorney General had to apply it "as soon as feasible," which made the delegation "distinctly small-bore."

A statute gives an agency discretion over the route of a post road. Why is that the easier case?
?
Discretion over the government's own operations or property tolerates broader delegation than discretion over private conduct — on Gorsuch's definition the power is not legislative at all. Compare [[The Post Roads Debate]].

Nondelegation has invalidated nothing since 1935. So what is doing the work now?
?
The same concern under other names: the major questions doctrine, constitutional avoidance, and narrow construction of the delegating text. Gundy is itself an avoidance case, which is why Gorsuch calls the test toothless as applied.
