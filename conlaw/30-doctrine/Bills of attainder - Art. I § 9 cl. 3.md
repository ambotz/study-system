## Rule statement

Article I, § 9, cl. 3 forbids Congress to pass any bill of attainder, and "[a] bill of attainder is a legislative act which inflicts punishment without a judicial trial": "legislative acts, no matter what their form, that apply either to named individuals or to easily ascertainable members of a group in such a way as to inflict punishment on them without a judicial trial are bills of attainder" ([[United States v. Lovett]]). Form is irrelevant, so Congress cannot do through the purse what the Clause forbids it to do directly; punishment "through the instrumentality of an Act specifically cutting off the pay" is "no less galling or effective than if it had been done by an Act which designated the conduct as criminal." Permanent exclusion from government service, or from any chosen vocation, "is punishment, and of a most severe type." Frankfurter's concurrence states the competing formulation: the Clause is one of the specific provisions "defined by history," and a historical bill of attainder specified an offense and declared guilt, neither of which § 304 did on its face — so the constitutional question could and should have been avoided by reading the rider as a stoppage of disbursement that left the government's debt intact.

## Elements

1. **A legislative act.** The Clause reaches what Congress enacts, not what a court adjudges or an executive officer decides; the vice is "the substitution of legislative determination of guilt and legislative imposition of punishment for judicial finding and sentence" (Frankfurter).
2. **Specificity.** The act must apply "either to named individuals or to easily ascertainable members of a group." Section 304 named Lovett, Watson and Dodd; a general rider barring pay to members of organizations advocating violent overthrow of the government was standard and went unchallenged.
3. **Punishment.** Ask what the act does to the person, not what it is called:
   - permanent exclusion from government service, "a legislative decree of perpetual exclusion" from a chosen vocation, is punishment;
   - exclusion from a profession by loyalty oath was punishment in Cummings v. Missouri and Ex parte Garland, neither of which has been overruled;
   - a disqualification for non-retributive reasons is not — "[a] man may be forbidden to practice medicine because he has been convicted of a felony, or because he is no longer qualified" (Frankfurter);
   - later cases ask whether the burden is punitive in history, function and motive (Nixon v. Administrator of General Services).
4. **Absence of a judicial trial.** In [[United States v. Lovett]] the substitute was a legislative one: a House subcommittee held secret hearings, excluded lawyers, described its work as "in the nature of an inquest of office," and found each respondent guilty of "subversive activity" and "unfit" for government employment.
5. **Form does not save it.** An appropriations rider, a pay cutoff, a jurisdictional device or any other packaging is measured by purpose and effect; § 304's purpose "was not merely to cut off respondents' compensation through regular disbursing channels but permanently to bar them from government service."

## Standard of review / burden

There is no tier of scrutiny: the Clause is a flat prohibition, and the only questions are specificity and punishment. The Court construes the statute "in light of the circumstances leading to its passage," so the legislative history that produced the rider — the Dies Committee, the floor attack on thirty-nine named employees, the subcommittee's findings of guilt — supplies the determination of guilt the text omits, and a narrower reading is rejected because it "would completely frustrate the purpose of all who sponsored Section 304." Congress's control of the purse does not displace review and does not make the question political: otherwise "congressional action, aimed at three named individuals, which stigmatized their reputation and seriously impaired their chance to earn a living, could never be challenged in any court," and "[o]ur Constitution did not contemplate such a result." A suit for compensation for work performed is ordinary Court of Claims business, which supplies the case. The competing discipline is the avoidance canon: on Frankfurter's view "the most fundamental principle of constitutional adjudication is not to face constitutional questions but to avoid them, if at all possible," and the available construction — a stoppage of routine disbursement leaving the obligation to pay intact — would have given the same judgment without deciding anything. Because the Clause binds Congress directly, the answer does not depend on what the President thinks; Roosevelt signed § 304 while calling it "not only unwise and discriminatory, but unconstitutional," and the Solicitor General agreed, so Congress had to authorize special counsel to defend it.

## Exceptions

- General rules defined by conduct or class membership rather than by name: the standard appropriations rider barring pay to members of organizations advocating overthrow of the government is not an attainder.
- Burdens that are regulatory or qualifying rather than retributive, such as disqualification for want of qualification or on conviction after trial (Frankfurter's medicine example).
- Naming one person is not by itself enough; a statute directed at a single former President's papers was upheld in Nixon v. Administrator of General Services on the history-function-motive inquiry.
- Punishment imposed after judicial trial, which is what the Clause channels the government toward; the Constitution itself reserves exclusion from office as a sanction for crimes such as treason and bribery.
- Ordinary appropriations choices. Congress may generally refuse to fund operations it disagrees with, because no money leaves the Treasury without a law; the limits come from the Constitution's other provisions, including this one. See [[The power of the purse - Art. I § 9 cl. 7 appropriations]].
- Abolishing or defunding an office rather than a person. Section 2262 of the 2011 appropriations act defunded specified advisory positions, and the objection raised against it was separation of powers, not attainder ([[Obama, Statement on H.R. 1473]]).
- Not decided in [[United States v. Lovett]]: whether § 304 usurped the President's removal power, and whether it denied due process. Both were argued; the Court rested on attainder alone. See [[Removal - Art. II § 1 and the Humphrey's exception]].
- Not resolved: whether Frankfurter's historical test — a specified offense plus a declaration of guilt — or Black's purpose-and-effect test states the clause's original meaning; the Court answered him with Cummings and Garland rather than with history.

## Leading case

[[United States v. Lovett]]

- Section 304 of the Urgent Deficiency Appropriation Act of 1943 cut off the pay of three named federal employees unless the President reappointed them with the Senate's consent; the Court held it a bill of attainder and affirmed judgments for the three.
- The driving fact is the legislative trial behind the rider: a special House Appropriations subcommittee held secret hearings, excluded counsel, called its work "an inquest of office," rejected the Secretary of the Interior's "strong appeal" to keep Lovett, and found the three guilty of subversive activity and unfit for government employment.
- The second driving fact is the purse dynamic: the Senate voted 69 to 0 against § 304 and yielded only because the House refused to pass any appropriation without it, which shows how one chamber can force an unrelated measure through a must-pass bill.
- The agencies kept all three at work and only their pay stopped, which is what gave the Court of Claims a compensation claim and defeated the argument that nothing justiciable was presented.
- Worth quoting: the rider "operates as a legislative decree of perpetual exclusion" from a chosen vocation; punishment by pay cutoff is "no less galling or effective"; and an earlier version drew the charge of "legislative lynching."
- Black is functional about form and formal about the category: legislative punishment is forbidden outright, whatever the statute looks like.
- The case is also the model for the modern signing statement: Roosevelt signed, objected in constitutional terms, complied, and left the question to the courts — the path [[Obama, Statement on H.R. 1473]] follows on two of its three objections.

## Best counter-case

[[Obama, Statement on H.R. 1473]]

- The same constitutional collision — Congress using an appropriations rider against the executive — resolved with no attainder claim at all, which is the cleanest test of how much work the Clause actually does.
- The distinguishing fact is who is named: § 2262 withheld funds for several positions advising the President directly, not for identified individuals, and it carried no legislative finding of guilt or unfitness. That moves the objection from Art. I, § 9, cl. 3 to the separation of powers — the President's "well-established authority to supervise and oversee the executive branch, and to obtain advice."
- Arguing the attainder side anyway: if a position is created or defunded in order to remove the particular person holding it, [[United States v. Lovett]] says courts look at purpose and effect rather than form, and the legislative record would supply the same proof the Dies subcommittee supplied in 1943.
- Arguing the other side: a funding denial is the ordinary operation of the Appropriations Clause; nobody has a constitutional right to be paid from the Treasury; and on Frankfurter's historical test there is no specified offense and no declaration of guilt, so the Clause is not in play.
- The remedial asymmetry is the point to bank. Obama announced that "the executive branch will construe section 2262 not to abrogate these Presidential prerogatives," which is nonenforcement under another name, and in [[Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer]] terms it is category three, where the President must show a power that disables Congress from acting. Even a void restriction creates no appropriation, so for a pure funding denial the President's remedy is limited to not spending. See [[Presidential power - Art. II and the Youngstown categories]] and [[Enforcement discretion - Art. II § 3 Take Care]].
- The internal counter-case is Frankfurter's concurrence in [[United States v. Lovett]] itself, which would have reached the same judgment on the statute alone: specific clauses "were defined by history" and must be held to their historic limits, and Holmes's warning that legislatures "are ultimate guardians of the liberties and welfare of the people in quite as great a degree as the courts."

## Professor gloss

## Common trap

- Looking for a crime. The Clause is not limited to criminal punishment; § 304 designated no conduct as criminal and was still an attainder, because permanent exclusion from a vocation is punishment.
- Thinking the appropriations label is a defense. "[N]o matter what their form" is the operative phrase, and the Court expressly rejected the argument that a pay cutoff is an unreviewable political matter.
- Treating any statute that names a person as an attainder. Specificity is necessary and not sufficient; there must be punishment, which is why the statute aimed at one former President's papers survived in Nixon v. Administrator of General Services.
- Ignoring Frankfurter's historical objection. A bill of attainder classically specified an offense and declared guilt; § 304 did neither on its face, and Black's answer is precedent (Cummings and Garland), not history. On an exam, say which side has the better original-meaning argument and why.
- Saying [[United States v. Lovett]] decided that Congress may not defund executive officers. The removal claim was argued first and not reached; the holding binds Congress through Art. I, § 9, cl. 3 whatever the President's removal power turns out to be.
- Assuming the Court will always look behind the statute's text. It did so here because the purpose was documented and a narrower reading "would completely frustrate" it; where the record is a budget cut with no findings of disloyalty, the retributive element Frankfurter demanded is missing and the result likely changes.
