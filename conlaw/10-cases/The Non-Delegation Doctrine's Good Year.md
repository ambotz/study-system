## What it is

A casebook note on the two 1935 cases in which the Supreme Court held a delegation of legislative power unconstitutional: [[Panama Refining Co. v. Ryan|Panama Refining]] and [[A.L.A. Schechter Poultry Corp. v. United States|Schechter Poultry]]. The syllabus calls it "One Good Year," after Sunstein's quip that the doctrine "has had one good year, and 211 bad ones (and counting)." It sets up [[Gundy v. United States]].

## What to notice

- **The test both before and after 1935.** The Court upheld every delegation that contained an "intelligible principle," meaning a standard against which the executive's conduct could be judged. The 1935 statutes failed that test.
- **The statute in Schechter.** Section 3 of the National Industrial Recovery Act let trade groups propose "codes of fair competition" to the President, who could make them binding if the group was "truly representative" and the code "not designed to promote monopolies." He could add conditions and exceptions "as the President in his discretion deems necessary." The Live Poultry Code was one; the Schechters were convicted of nineteen violations.
- **Hughes's reasoning (unanimous).** Section 3 "supplies no standards for any trade, industry, or activity." It "does not undertake to prescribe rules of conduct," but "authorizes the making of codes to prescribe them." The President's discretion "is virtually unfettered."
- **The two readings the casebook offers.** A landmark for civil liberties and limited government, or a Court "run amok" whose doctrine would destroy the administrative state. Both are live in Gundy.
- **What followed.** Congress rewrote the second wave of New Deal statutes to survive the test, and built the administrative agencies on broad standards: working conditions, financial transactions, consumer products, the environment. Agencies write binding regulations that Congress never votes on, supervised by the White House and by courts under the Administrative Procedure Act.
- **The casebook's open question.** "It is not clear that these agencies or this system of administrative law could survive the rigorous application of Schechter Poultry."
- **The dates matter.** 1935 is the year before the Court's switch in 1937. Schechter was unanimous, including Brandeis and Cardozo, so it cannot be dismissed as the work of the conservative bloc alone.
