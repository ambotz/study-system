## What it is

Executive Order 14242 of March 20, 2025, "Improving Education Outcomes by Empowering Parents, States, and Communities," printed at 90 Fed. Reg. 13679. Section 2(a) directs the Secretary of Education to "take all necessary steps to facilitate the closure of the Department of Education and return authority over education to the States and local communities," qualified by "to the maximum extent appropriate and permitted by law" and by a proviso preserving "the effective and uninterrupted delivery of services, programs, and benefits on which Americans rely." Section 2(b) conditions the allocation of Department funds on "rigorous compliance with Federal law and Administration policy."

Congress created the Department of Education by statute in 1979 and funds it by annual appropriation. The order does not purport to repeal that statute.

## What to notice

- **The qualifier carries the whole document.** "To the maximum extent appropriate and permitted by law" concedes that the President cannot abolish by order a department Congress created by law. The live question is what remains available short of abolition: declining to fill positions, reorganising functions, slowing disbursement, returning "bank functions" to another entity.
- **The purse runs both ways.** Lovett is Congress using appropriations against the executive. This is the mirror: the executive declining to administer what Congress funded. Both ask whether the power to spend or withhold can accomplish indirectly what the Constitution forbids directly.
- **Section 2(b) is a condition the executive writes itself.** Congress attaches conditions to grants, and *South Dakota v. Dole (1987)* sets the limits. Here the Secretary attaches "Administration policy" as a condition on money Congress already appropriated, which is a different question: not whether a condition is coercive, but whether the executive may add one at all.
- **The preamble is argument, not operative text.** The recitals about NAEP scores, the $1.6 trillion loan portfolio and the Wells Fargo comparison are policy justification. Separate them from § 2 when the question is what the order actually does.
- **No appropriations language appears anywhere in it.** That silence matters: an order cannot by itself move money, so the mechanism has to be found in the Department's existing authorities.
