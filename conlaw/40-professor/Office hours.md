## 9/28

*captured Sunday, September 27, 2026 11:53 AM*

Non delegation
How is allowing the AG to determine how to “apply” SORNA not just a version of prosecutorial discretion? Doesn’t the executive maintain discretion to prosecute?
Gorsuch is arguing a slight difference in the dissent. He is not saying that the AG is just given the power to determine if the law applies, but rather he is given the power to determine what the law is. Not just if it applies, but what it is in general. These are two different things, and the latter is a step above prosecutorial discretion.
If a statue is interpreted as delegating powers unconstitutionally, is that constitutional precedent or statutory precedent? In practice, seems like Congress responds to this via new legislation correcting the wrong (see post 1935), but if something is determined as proper use of delegation, it can only be corrected by subsequent SCOTUS decisions, like constitutional interpretation?
It is constitutional precedent usually. If something is previously considered improper use of delegation, Congress can't do anything about it (they must make the law less of a delegation). But, latter courts can overturn their Constitutional approach to the question, and say the previous courts got it wrong by interpreting the Constiution wrong. That is overturning Constitutional precedent
They won't say that the meaning of the statue changes, but rather will apply the intelligible principle test differently.
Admin law & Loper Bright
Essentially held that courts can determine what the meaning of a statute is, over the agency's meaning
But if the statute is very ambiguous to begin with, and already gives broad power to a specific agency to reconcile the issues, it's hard to give power back to the court
Admin law essentially overcomes nondelegation arguments by having the statute be deemed to provide an intelligible principle
