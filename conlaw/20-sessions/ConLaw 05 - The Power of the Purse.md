## Notes

### Signing Statement

- Signing Statement - President signs bill but has qualms about it → expresses them
    - Why sign? Wouldn't have any money at all if he vetoed → no money for vetos?

### Power of the purse as a check

- What if Congress said "no money" for executive orders? No money for vetos?
    - They can't do this, as it hinders with executive power
    - Can't use the power of purse as a means to an executive end / **impermissible** end (can't accomplish something you can't do any other way through the power of the purse)
    - Power of purse is an important check

### [[Obama, Statement on H.R. 1473|Obama Statement]]

- Was the comments/statements a line-item veto?
    1. Didn't change the text → just suggests Congressional action is unconstitutional
    2. Doesn't bind future people

### Must POTUS spend if Congress authorizes?

- Scalia in [[Clinton v. City of New York (1998)|Clinton]]: "NO" → impeachment (Nixon)
    - Traditional discretion exists, but if Congress mandates that all money be used, POTUS must spend it

### [[Executive Order 14242|EO 14242]] - Closing the Dept. of Education

- DoE still exists, but if Executive doesn't hire anyone → can't spend money on the dept.
    - **Inherent Executive power: Don't need to spend money if POTUS doesn't want to (Impeachment) → debated**

### Standing

- Congress: spend money; POTUS: Not spending money
    - Standing: Easy → whoever was supposed to get the money
- Congress: don't spend money; POTUS: spending money
    - Standing: Harder → particularized injury is hard; Congress' institutional power is diminished and is, hence, the injury [?]

### Enforcing Congressional appropriations

- How can Congressional appropriations be enforced?
    - Must give House standing to sue Executive
    - Can't [?] rules Executive can't spend
    - But, what if the President spends anyway?
- **Standing issue:** Congress never demands Account of Statement [check: the Art. I § 9 cl. 7 requirement is a "regular Statement and Account"] for CIA budget → people sued → SCOTUS ruled no one had standing to sue
    - Baude agrees that sometimes no one has standing
- If Congress itself has standing → must give standing to an entire chamber at least (not one person)
