## Notes

### Removal Theories

**Removal theories spectrum**

```text
Unitary Executive <------------------------> Congressional Control
                          |
                Congress says POTUS
                 can remove people
```

### Timeline

- **History is a "wash"**
- 1789 - [[The Decision of 1789|Decision of 1789]]
- 1866 - Johnson / Tenure in Office Act (POTUS can't fire Cabinet officers)
- 1926 - [[Myers v. United States|Myers]] - POTUS can remove w/o cause
- 1935 - [[Humphrey's Executor v. United States (1935)|Humphrey's Executor]] (independent agency heads can't be removed w/o cause)
- 1988 - [[Morrison v. Olson]] (independent prosecutors can't be removed by POTUS)
- 1997-2000 - Clinton / Starr
- 2010 - PCAOB v. [[Free Enterprise Fund v. Public Company Accounting Oversight Board|Free Enterprise Fund]] (double agency can be removed) [check: Free Enterprise Fund held that two layers of for-cause removal protection are unconstitutional]
- 2020 - [[Seila Law LLC v. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau|Seila Law]] (one-person agency can be removed w/o cause)
- 2026 - [[Trump v. Slaughter (2026)|Slaughter]] and [[Trump v. Cook (2026)|Cook]] (can fully remove any agency head, one or multiple people, except the Fed)

### Unitary Executive Args.

- Federalist Papers - "Energy" / vigor / accountability for President
    - Can be susceptible to political pressure
    - Still, firing someone ≠ ability to execute national policy
        - Still need to appoint someone new and confirmed by Senate (depending on the position)
- Take Care Clause (Art. 2, sec. 3)
- Vesting Clause (Art. 2, §1, cl. 1): Executive power doesn't contain "herein" whereas Art. 1 does
    - "A President" = 1 person; Art. 3 specifies inferior; Art. 2 doesn't

### Congressional Control Args.

- The Unitary Executive / Energy arg. works only when Congress doesn't speak
    - Can constrain future president's offices to law
- Art. 1 allows Congress to "establish" offices, which can include how the heads of those offices can be appointed and removed
    - Art. 1, §8, cl. 18 - Necessary and Proper Clause applies when the Constitution is silent
- Exam Rate [?] → Can these two Args. reach a middle ground?
    - Limitation to "Officers" in Appointment Clause
    - But independent route via Take Care Clause (Art. 2, sec. 3)

### [[Humphrey's Executor v. United States (1935)|Humphrey's Executor]]

- Opinion doesn't just adopt the Congressional Control theory
    - FTC isn't fully an Executive agency / isn't Executive power
        - "quasi-legislative / quasi-judicial body"
        - Determines what new laws are needed
        - How laws (prosecution) are executed = executive
        - Investigations / subpoenas and reporting to courts = judicial

### [[Trump v. Slaughter (2026)|Trump v. Slaughter]]

- Almost full endorsement of unitary executive; limited on if civil servant counts as officers / Heads of Agency
- Can the Admin State be a 4th branch?
    - Constitution doesn't allow for it
    - Not strictly an enforcement agency as they are deciding what laws to pass and involving the courts
- Can President remove people from Civil Service?
    - 5-6 layers removed from the Agency Head
    - How far can the Unitary Executive power go?
    - Georgetown? No, since not every chartered entity wields sovereign power w/ no govt. authority
        - Congress created them via charter
    - Slaughter doesn't address these
- Non Art. 3 - Judges → doesn't apply to these judges (what are they then, if not part of Art. 3)
    - These "courts" aren't in the judicial branch
- Registrar of Copyrights
    - Immune from the Presidential removal power
    - Registrar is appointed by Librarian of Congress (appointed by president)
    - Answer: for these "weird" positions, the person who appointed their boss could be possible to remove them

### [[Trump v. Cook (2026)|Trump v. Cook]]

- Fed Reserve: "independent"
    1. Maybe like Georgetown? Not really exercising sovereign power (but it is)
    2. Core of Fed likely not a govt. power; instead court differentiates by history
