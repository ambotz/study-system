### [[McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)|McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)]]

- I - Can Congress Incorporate Bank?
- II - Can a state tax a nationally Incorporated Bank?
- **Exam note:** Enumerate each person's view and how Marshall responded

### Enumerated Powers

- Implies there are incidental powers
- **Political theory:** Const. created a nation, which is the end of the debate → US Chooses otherwise

### Marshall Args. (unique) I.

- Necessary ≠ Indispensable, because what if there are two ways to do something. Neither is truly indispensable, but both are necessary.
    - Jefferson response (boxed label beside this point)
- "Incidental" → Marshall agrees with Madison's approach, but seizes [?] the Bank is incidental / a "N&P" means to a legitimate end.
    - Madison thinks this is an **end**, not a means
    - Madison response (boxed label): Corporations are created **to do other** things, not as an end in itself.
    - **post office Example** → Classic Response for reading in legitimate means
- "Necessary" ≠ Everything convenient.
    - **END MUST BE LEGITIMATE**
        - If so, all means to that end are appropriate = Constitutional.
    - Legitimate END = something that is itself constitutional.
        - If means = pretext for illegitimate end, courts intervene (Contra. [[Wickard v. Filburn (1942)|Wickard]]).

### Marshall II - Not as canonized as Part I

- Maryland taxed national bank, in part to achieve standing to be able to challenge via a suit.
- Note: Congress never prohibited states from taxing → still, states can't tax.
    - Why can't states tax?
        - Outside of the State's jurisdiction, even if in Maryland
        - Tax = destroy (when only discriminating and to the bank)
- Still, Marshall leaves room for State to tax **all equally** (i.e., real property tax).
- OH: Is this a relevant ancestor of being able to remove federal things to federal court under SMJ?

### [[Nature of the Union - compact theory, nationalism and secession|Nature of Union]]

1. Jefferson [wrong]: we the people = we the States (of the people)
    - No federal body
2. Madison: we the people = States + people
    - Quasi
3. Marshall: we the people = States + people
    - Federal govt. is supreme sovereign, but only in its limited powers
4. Wilson [wrong]: we the people = we the nation
    - No real State convention; just people agreeing to a national Body
