### Provisions noted

- 14A, Section 2: must let the person vote, or (les) [?], will lose the ability to count them for representation purposes.
- Art. 3, Section 3: Treason: levying war against US / adhering to enemies.
    - Treason is the only crime defined in the Constitution.
- Art. 4, Section 3: WV/Virginia, Maine and Massachusetts, etc. were formed from previous states' jurisdiction.
    - Solution: "consent" of the legislature and the states [check: the text requires the consent of the legislatures of the states concerned as well as of Congress].
    - Punctuation and capitalization may not necessarily have too much meaning.
- Art. 1, Section 2, Clause 3: "indentured servants" = significance of slavery without mentioning slavery [check: the text reads "those bound to Service for a Term of Years"].
- Art. 2 [check: the impeachment-trial oath is Art. I, §3, cl. 6], Section 3, Clause 6: impeachments require "oath or affirmation".
    - Art. VI, cl. 3 already mentions the oath.

### The Constitution does three major things

1. Rights: Bill of Rights (Amendments 1-10) and same in the Constitution (treason and habeas corpus [?]).
2. Separation of powers: Legislative (I), Executive (II), Judicial (III).
3. Federalism:
    - Art. IV
    - Art. VI
    - Amendment 10: "question begging" since it only reserves states' powers.
    - Art. 1, Section 8 (enumerated powers)

### Nature of constitutional law

1. Supreme over statutes and other regulations; more fundamental.
2. More ambiguous.

- "Secret Congress": legislation tucked away in an omnibus spending bill that no one will take credit for → avoids blame and animosity.
- Constitutional fights: higher order of law at a higher level of abstraction.
- **Exam:** make constitutional arguments to a fact set.
