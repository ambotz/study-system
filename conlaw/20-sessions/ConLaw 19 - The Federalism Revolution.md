## Notes

### [[McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)|McCulloch]], but no [[United States v. Darby (1941)|Darby]]?

- Can't use "means" when the means themselves are an end →
    - Marshall's list of invalid means: war, taxes, regulating commerce; [[McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)|McCulloch]] & Marshall hold this in [[McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)|McCulloch]].
    - This is the pathway to approve [[McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)|McCulloch]] but not validate [[United States v. Darby (1941)|Darby]].

### [[United States v. Lopez (1995)|Lopez (1995)]]

- Three categories:
    1. Channels
    2. Instrumentalities
    3. Substantially Effects interstate Commerce
    - Channels and Instrumentalities are interrelated.
    - "Economic Activity" for Substantial effects, not necessarily channels/instrumentalities.
- Note: Congress + Executive are in agreement. Judicial branch steps in to protect Federalism.
- Court is not just saying gun is non-economic. Instead, they first create a test that substantial effects must involve economic activity. Then, apply that to guns.
    - Limiting principle needed to curtail interstate Commerce.
- Hypo: train robberies? Likely under channels/instrumentalities.
    - Can still criminalize, even though there is no direct economic nexus. Substantial effects is the only prong that requires economic nexus.
- How far would J. Thomas go?
    - Ok with [[McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)|McCulloch]] and real economic effects.
    - Ok with [[Hammer v. Dagenhart (1918)|Hammer]] and banning goods in interstate.
    - [[United States v. Darby (1941)|Darby]] and direct regulation of wages/production → line drawing problem. Same problem as the broad reading side. Where does "substantially effects" sit?

### [[United States v. Lopez (1995)|Lopez (1995)]] cont.

- Jurisdictional hook of "traveled in" or;
    - → Lower courts have upheld, citing civil rights cases.
- Substantially effects
    - → [[United States v. Lopez (1995)|Lopez]] Court could say non economic = no substantial effect.

### [[Gonzales v. Raich (2005)]]

- If weed is a commodity, aren't guns also a commodity?
    - CSA was a broad interstate regulation.
- Almost a reverse of [[Wickard v. Filburn (1942)|Wickard]] → CSA is we don't want weed market.
- Aggregation principle
    - [[United States v. Lopez (1995)|Lopez]]: substantial effects must be economic activity.
    - [[Gonzales v. Raich (2005)|Gonzalez]]: economic activity is seen as a "class of activities".
- So, the question becomes: class of activities → what to define this as? It's a framing activity. How large is the scheme of regulation?
- Can Congress regulate everything except: guns ([[United States v. Lopez (1995)|Lopez]]); civil remedies against domestic violence (Morrison).
