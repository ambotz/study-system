## Notes

### Exam Tips/what is expected

- Three types of arguments. Recognize them:
    1. Policy arg.
    2. Constitutional args.
    3. Constitutional args. a Court will agree with (Doctrinal framework)
- **Keep track of what is happening** → will be asked to make them separately
- Even though there is no right answer there is a lot of bad answers: avoid inconsistency
- **Exam note:** Understand [[Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer (1952)|Youngstown]] & Separation of Powers fully
    - Executive power must be rooted in Statute or Constitution

### Bank Debate

- Why do states care about a national bank?
    - Gives Federal govt. more power to exercise its policy
    - Policy: Detracts away from state bank businesses
- Members of Congress debating: (1) [[The Post Roads Debate|Post Roads]], (2) [[The Decision of 1789|Debate of 1789]], (3) [[The Bank Debate|Bank Debate]]
    - Lots of examples pointing to a "Departmentalist" read, where Congress is individually exploring Constitutionalism
- Text in play: Art. 1, Sec. 8, Cl. 18 [?] vs. 10th Amendment

### Madison's pillars of Argument

1. Tax & general welfare
2. Borrow Money
3. [[Enumerated powers and the Necessary and Proper Clause - Art. I § 8 cl. 18|Necessary & Proper]]
    - **Necessary = "Incidental" not "Important"**
    - Necessary for "executing the enumerated powers." In Madison's view, there are other ways to carry out the enumerated powers

### Madison Bank Debate (First)

- Pamphlet/Guide on how to practice Constitutional Law → employs various Constitutional args.

### Jefferson's Arguments

- 10th Amendment
    - Very circular → "Powers not delegated to the US" → isn't this the whole question
- Tax
    - Bill to raise revenue must be originated in House (this originated in the Senate).
- Necessary = that for w/o the grant of power, the power would be negatory [check: "nugatory"] (written to the right, between Tax and Hamilton's Arguments)

### Hamilton's Arguments

- Necessary and proper
    - Necessary ≠ absolutely needed; just means the means is needed to achieve the end

### Two Wrinkles

1. Articles of Confederation had a Bank → Helps Madison
2. Bank was expressly listed in the first draft of Constitution, but then taken out before ratification → Supports Madison/Jefferson, but Madison doesn't lean on this fact
