## Notes

### Congress

- Does Congress have to think if a law they pass is Constitutional?
    - There is evidence Congress does think about it (i.e., [[The Post Roads Debate|Post Roads Debate]])
    - But not clear if Congress HAS to, although they also take an Oath (which was [[Marbury v. Madison (1803)|Marbury]]'s reasoning for judicial review).

### President

- Also takes an Oath and reviews legislation and enforces legislation
    - If president thinks legislation is unconstitutional and Court has not resolved the question:
        1. He doesn't have to enforce it: He took an Oath to independently uphold the Constitution
    - If court has said legislation is unconstitutional
        2. He likely can't contradict branches because judicial review
    - If POTUS and legislature think a legislation is constitutional but judiciary doesn't → can still be held unconstitutional (See Line Item Veto Act)
    - Margin: buss [?]

### Departmentalism vs. Judicial Supremacy

- Departmentalism - all branches can equally interpret Constitution
- Judicial Supremacy - Judiciary can interpret Constitution better than other branches

### [[The Lincoln-Douglas Debates|Lincoln-Douglas Debates]]

- Lincoln
    - What makes good precedent: Unanimous, uncontested, clear interpretation of the facts

### During Civil War

- Legislation was passed that again banned slavery in the territories.
- Constitutional?
    1. Civil War justification - weakest
    2. Departmentalism - better

### Brown v. Board vs. [[Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857)|Dred Scott]]

- Brown was not followed (just like how [[Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857)|Dred Scott]] wasn't followed)
    - What's the difference?
        - Brown was a federalism question (Supremacy clause)
        - Even though state officials took an Oath to uphold the Constitution → can't interpret the Constitution individually (hard for departmentalism to extend past the three branches of govt.).

### [[Cooper v. Aaron (1958)|Cooper v. Aaron]]

- Clear cut claim that Judiciary is supreme to anyone.
    - Constitution ↔ SCOTUS

### What happens when Executive doesn't like Judiciary Ruling?

1. Most Departmentalists: Must obey the judgement at hand, but can ignore it as a matter of law
2. Hardcore Departmentalist (including Paulsen [?]): Can also ignore the judgement at hand.
    - But, other people in the Executive Branch can disobey the President themselves.
