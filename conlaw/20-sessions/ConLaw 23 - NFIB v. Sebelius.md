### [[NFIB v. Sebelius (2012)|NFIB]]

- Mandate - tax / Commerce Clause and N&P
- Expansion - Congressional spending

### Mandate - under interstate commerce?

1. Does ACA regulating activity of "not buying health insurance" fall within regulating **economic activity**?
1. Is there economic activity? Not buying insurance could be economic, but can't be **activity**.
    - Ginsburg Activity argument: Some point people will engage in healthcare market. So not buying health insurance ≠ not engaging in economic activity **ever**.
    - Baude's response to Broccoli parable: Maybe could force people to buy Broccoli, but can't force people to eat it (nor can they sell/feed anyone else)

### Necessary and proper

- Is it **necessary and proper** to have the mandate to support the guaranteed issue (which is squarely within interstate commerce)?
    - The mandate cannot be an incidental power → it is too great to be incidental; must be an independent power
        - Ginsburg's attack on this as an unworkable test. What can be an incidental & what is too much to be incidental
    - Roberts' response: Mandate is too affirmative to be incidental (see [[Printz v. United States (1997)|Printz]]); People can't be commandeered.

### Tax (Must be a back up)

- Constitutional avoidance after most natural reading
- Not purchasing health insurance as per the mandate = payment
    - Must be tax
    - Tax or Penalty
    - [[Bailey v. Drexel Furniture Co (1922)|Bailey]] invoked → Why would we Bail out Congress? Const. avoidance

### Tax vs. Penalty

- "[[Bailey v. Drexel Furniture Co (1922)|Bailey]] is very ambiguous" Baude

### If tax, Direct?

- Income Tax = Direct tax (Pollock)
- Capitation and land tax ([[Hylton v. United States (1796)|Hylton]]) = Direct tax
- Roberts: This doesn't fall into all direct tax categories, so indirect tax

### If indirect tax, can you tax inactivity?

- Seems like you can via capitation [?]
- Baude: New definition of direct tax? A tax on inactivity?? A tax one cannot get away from = direct tax (ex., capitation)
- Hypo: Can we tax to create gun free School Zone? Probably. Justification: A tax is not punitive; like going to jail.
- **Note:** could have called it an income tax and not get into direct/indirect

### Medicaid Expansion

- Valid spending?
    - Passes [[South Dakota v. Dole (1987)|Dole]]'s four factors, but flunks the Coercion dicta from Dole. Why?
        1. A lot of $$ being offered
        1. OLD funds threatened to be taken away / Reliance
    - Hypo: take 2 steps instead of one
        1. Repeal old Medicaid; decouple from new program
        1. Pass new Medicaid with current terms

### Legal Consequences

1. Mandate being a tax = don't have to buy health insurance (can just pay fine and move forward)
1. Congress can "zero" the tax → then, it might not be a tax.
1. Expansion - without threat, states can turn down New medicaid funds
- Takeaway! Baude **REALLY** likes this opinion
