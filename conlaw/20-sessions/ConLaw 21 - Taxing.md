## Notes

### Tax Power

- **Apportionment**: meant to protect against uniform taxes that have disparate impact.
- **Uniformity**: to protect from disparately targeted statutes.
- Margin: He called all the heavy hitters in a row.

### [[Hylton v. United States (1796)|Hylton]]

- They used to have justices each write their own opinions.
- Taxpayer standing is fine if you are challenging taxes, this is very orthodox standing.
- If it's direct it must be apportioned.
- Reasoning seems to be that if apportionment doesn't make sense, we are going to say its not direct.
- Historically: direct tax was capitation or land tax.
- Opinion contemplates taxes that are neither duties nor direct taxes.

### Pollock

- Hard to reconcile w/ [[Hylton v. United States (1796)|Hylton]].
- Some people thought that this was the Court trying to preserve the constitutional system, other thought that they were being too friendly to robber barons.
- 16th amendment was passed which overturned it.

### Later cases

- License Tax Cases - taxation is not limited to things you can regulate.
- Veazie - not going to review "excessive" taxes.
- [[McCray v. United States|McCray]] - don't review motive.
- [[Bailey v. Drexel Furniture Co (1922)|Bailey v. Drexel]] → one time the court said the tax went too far.
    - De facto scienter requirement.
    - Court says that this is an illegal regulation.
- We have inconsistent case law w/ no real limits but also a technical limit.
    - Margin: What Stone think funny about this [?]
- Consider ban on brewing whiskey at home to help collect taxes → tax + [[Enumerated powers and the Necessary and Proper Clause - Art. I § 8 cl. 18|necessary and proper]] power.
