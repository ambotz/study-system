## Notes

- [[Nondelegation - Art. I § 1 and the intelligible principle|Non-delegation doctrine]] falls under legislative power

### Why delegation?

- Easier for Executive to make choices than all of Congress to decide the method to achieve

### Why not delegation?

- Congress has certain duties/obligations

### What are the ways to draw the line on delegation?

1. Policy - what is most prudent / easiest to administer
2. Historical Context
    - What was England doing that we wanted to avoid
    - What were the colonies doing at the time?
3. Practice → what is done / actually carried out now?

### What's the practical consequence of delegation?

- After all, Congress and Executive are acting in agreement → public good is involved, so agreement among the branches is not enough

### Why can Congress delegate?

- **In the pure form, Congress cannot delegate "legislative power"**
- Zeno's paradox problem: Congress can't possibly establish all facets of how to carry out a legislation (i.e. So many subroutes [?] for routes in-between post office in [[The Post Roads Debate|post roads debate]]).
