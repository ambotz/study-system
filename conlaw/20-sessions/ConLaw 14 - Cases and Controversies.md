## Notes

- Departmentalism → Judicial Supremacy (General trend since the founding).
- Generally, not enforcing a statute is unconstitutional (as a general policy); But President can distinguish by saying the statute is unconstitutional.

### Baude position on opinion / judgement position

- If Court has jurisdiction, then its judgement binds those parties.
- Everything else, (i.e., should other courts follow it as precedent) is not binding

### [[The Correspondence of the Justices|The Correspondance of Justices]] (1793)

- Justices say they can't give advice on the creation of treaty with France:
    1. Separation of powers
    2. Judges of Last Resort
    3. The Opinion Clause - Art. 2. limited to Executive department
        - Why can't Executive department "delegate" the same way Congress can? → Office Hour Q
- Office Hour Question: Dicta vs. advising opinion?

### Discussion

1. [[Marbury v. Madison (1803)|Marbury]] claims (although post-hoc) that judiciary's role to say what the law is → this can't extend to advisory opinions. Why?
    - Court becomes ally to Executive Branch
    - Future rulings / judicial opinions compromised by advice on this topic
2. This becomes a record-setting moment → U.S. Courts don't give advisory opinions (different in some state courts, other countries, etc.)

### [[Ex parte Levitt (1937)|Ex Parte Levitt]] (1937)

- No opinion on the merits because there was no standing
- Must show particular injury (why you need relief) before asking for relief
    - If plaintiff had a pending case in front of the Court? Better argument for standing (see [[Buckley v. Valeo (1976)|Buckley v. Velo]]). "I'm injured through unconstitutional exercise of power over me"
- Still, people who brought cases against appointment of Black and in front of SCOTUS = 0 → No one brought a case
- Why did Executive Branch and Legislature let this happen if they thought about the Constitution? They did, but concluded it was Constitutional
- Standing is jurisdictional. If no standing → No ruling on the merits

### [[Massachusetts v. Mellon; Frothingham v. Mellon (1923)|Mellon]]

- No such thing as taxpayers standing
- States must have a specific theory when suing the federal govt.
- Unlawful spending: if Congress / one House has no standing, then Court will never be asked to adjudicate unconstitutional spending
    - If someone has to have standing to resolve unconstitutional spending, it has to be Congress suing the President / Executive.
    - Side note: But, didn't Congress pass the bill to begin with.
