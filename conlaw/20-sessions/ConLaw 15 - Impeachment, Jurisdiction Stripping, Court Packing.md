## Notes

### Impeachment

- "High Crimes and misdemeanors" (Art. 2 sec. 4)
    - Doesn't necessarily have to be an indictable crime
    - Term of art not limited to indictable crimes (see use in England) → Abuse of office/violation of public trust
- Upshot: what counts as High Crimes and Misdemeanors
    - [[The Impeachment of Justice Chase|Impeachment of Justice Chase]]: Shows mere disagreement of policy/partisanship ≠ High crime/misdemeanor (at least in a ex post fashion, based on Senate's acquittal).
        - 2/3 is a high bar to overcome.
    - Professor Berger [?] view: repeated bad decisions, maybe impeachable, but still a high enough bar to not threaten impeachment for every unfavorable decision
- Norm: No impeachment for incorrect SCOTUS decisions → difficult to understand if this is the norm based on practice or reading of Constitution

### Ex Parte McCardle

- Congress repealed habeas jurisdiction under the 1867 Act, so SCOTUS doesn't have jurisdiction
- Baude: Sure, under Art. 3, sec. 2, cl. 2 → Exceptions exist. But here, Congress suspended jurisdiction **after** oral argument.
- Office Hours (margin): Does it matter it's habeas corpus vs. something else's use [?]
- **Exam Note:** What are Congressional limits to jurisdiction stripping? → Exam Q
    1. Can't strip jurisdiction after the court rules (although Constitution doesn't say so). Why not after oral argument where & how to draw the line? [ANSWER].
- Policy (See [[Marbury v. Madison (1803)|Marbury]]): Could Court have gotten away with saying [?] Reconstruction was unconstitutional? Might have to acquiesce to ensure judicial authority is intact

### Ex Parte McCardle (11/2, continued)

- Lower Courts: General consensus that Congress can strip lower Courts jurisdiction (since Congress creates lower courts Art. 3 sec. 1) → SCOTUS is different
- **Art 3, Sec 2, Cl. 2 → Exceptions ≠ total ban (core theory).**

### Court Packing

- Office Hours (margin): If Senate doesn't want to add justices, how would Roosevelt get it done?
- Can Executive change number of justices? If so, when/for what reasons? [check: Congress, not the Executive, sets the number of justices] (cloud note: Art. 3, sec. 1)
    - Roosevelt: American people can change court layout whenever (add justices) whenever they want
    - [[Senate Report on Court Packing|Senate Report]]: Can only add when there's legitimate reasons outside political disagreement (Cf. [[Ex parte McCardle (1869)|McCardle]]); like administrative reasons

### What's the most sound constitutional response to judicial disagreement?

1. [[Impeachment - Art. II § 4 high crimes and misdemeanors|Impeachment]] (constitutionally sound); text in Constitution
2. Jurisdiction Stripping → more narrow (allows surgical strikes); "Court can't do any harm" (margin arrow: compared to other two mechanisms)
3. Court packing (no text in Constitution)

- **Impeachment has a higher bar (2/3 senate), compared to simple majority for other two limits.**
