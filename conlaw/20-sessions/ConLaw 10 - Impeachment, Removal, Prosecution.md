## Notes

### [[The Impeachment of Andrew Johnson|Andrew Johnson]]

- Was trying to "go easy" on the South during reconstruction so that reconstruction efforts and execution would go smoother
    - Overall North wants more "concrete" reparations

### [[Impeachment - Art. II § 4 high crimes and misdemeanors|"High Crimes and Misdemeanors"]]

- Doesn't necessarily mean high crimes = real crime in criminal code; still must be more than unsatisfactory executive performance
- Removal case on Secretary of War
    - Most concrete grounds for impeachment → today, [[Trump v. Slaughter (2026)|Trump v. Slaughter]] shows us this is fair

### SCOTUS on impeachments

- That is a political question; SCOTUS won't oversee → defer to judgement of Congress/Senate
- House impeaches → Senate 2/3 ratification [check: the Senate convicts and removes by a 2/3 vote]
    - Chief Justice does "preside" over impeachment (Vice President is too biased either way)

### [[United States v. Nixon (1974)|Nixon]]

- How can the US sue Nixon? Can't POTUS tell DOJ to stop?
    - Special prosecutor was given power thru the AG
    - Why doesn't Nixon fire AG to get rid of Special Prosecutor?
        - He tried, but Congress got New AG to not fire Special prosecutor (old AG fired)
- Is this a better way to resolve the impeachment problem (the Court) vs. Johnson?
    - Court heard a case brought by Special prosecutor
        - For: Framers didn't think about how strong political parties would be
    - But isn't Congress still playing a dormant role? Isn't that why Nixon resigned?
        - This was just a step zero before holding him in contempt directly

### [[Trump v. United States (2024)|Trump v. US]]

- Core: President is allowed to tell AG to do whatever he wants (directing a cabinet officer)
- Presumably immune: talking with VP

**Core power scope**

```text
Core power / scope
 |
 |- Directing AG
 |
 |- VP?
 |
 +-----------
*Rest of acts are not clarified
```

    - Rest of acts are not clarified
- Just because POTUS has power to direct cabinet members, does that mean he can use the power however he wants?

### Court's defense

1. Presidential immunity against criminal prosecution: only applies to President, but no one else in executive office
    - Contra. President can offer anyone a pardon
2. Stop president from weaponizing the DOJ against other Presidents
    - Contra. Didn't this decision stop Biden from [prosecuting] Trump?

### Dissent Hypothetical

- Order Seal Team 6 to kill political enemy → No response from majority
- Can't trust ordinary criminal process for President → Why can't constitutional logic apply here. Why are Art. 2 constitutional powers so much greater than other constitutional rights?
