## Notes

### [[Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857)|Dred Scott]] (1857)

### Jurisdiction

- Jurisdiction: Art 3. Cl. 1 (between citizens of different states) [check: diversity jurisdiction is Art. III § 2 cl. 1]
    - Is Dred Scott a citizen of the US? Court: No, black people can't be a citizen of the US
- Why?
    1. History / context (Originalism) [Original intent]: Founders did not perceive black men as "people" during the drafting of the declaration of independence
        - **But, History cuts the other way**: there were people (i.e. preachers) who conveyed that "all men" really means all men, including black people
    - Still, if Dred Scott not citizen, shouldn't Court not have jurisdiction? Technically true, but C.J. Taney calls this too important to not rule on
        - Passes through the no jdx gate C.J. Marshall established in [[Marbury v. Madison (1803)|Marbury]]

### Merits

- Merits: Missouri Compromise held unconstitutional (second statute, first is Judiciary Act in [[Marbury v. Madison (1803)|Marbury]])
    - Art IV, Sec. 3, Cl. 2 Territory clause → doesn't apply to new territories. Note: this clause gives Congress the power to make laws in new territories
        - "Ridiculous argument" from a textualist perspective
    - Still, under Fifth Amendment, Congress violates "due process" by making a law that takes away property w/o due process of law
        - → Hook for why Mo compromise is unconstitutional
    - What's the point of mentioning Territories clause? This is what Congress can legislate (its limits). Might be a gloss to bolster the 5th A DPC argument
- Margin: MN Time [?]

### IL and Mo laws

- Mo law determines what happens in Mo; not IL law
- Scott returned back to Mo, so he is a slave again.
- Isn't it unconstitutional for IL to do the same thing the Congress tried to do? 5th amendment doesn't apply to the states → only the federal govt. 14th A was written to apply against the states too

### Policy Args.

- Margin: Monday [?] filibustering [?]
- Was C.J. Taney trying to stop a Civil War?
    - He was just trying to keep the terms of the deal? But no, because this ruling gives way into slaves being kept in the north (as long as they were from the South). Pragmatic; **NOT Originalism**

### Political nexus

- James Buchanan wrote to Taney to take this argument off the table

### Was it wrong for Court to try and resolve the issue?

- No jurisdiction
- Political question (left up to the political branches)
    - SCOTUS invalidated a law that Congress passed
- First real test of [[Marbury v. Madison (1803)|Marbury]]
    - Was [[Marbury v. Madison (1803)|Marbury]] wrong? In allowing Courts to invalidate acts of Congress (Judiciary Act of 1789)
    - Margin: (1) Textual analysis in [[Marbury v. Madison (1803)|Marbury]] better
    - Margin: (2) also jdx in [[Marbury v. Madison (1803)|Marbury]]; whole rest of opinion advisory

### Lessons

1. SCOTUS weighing in is always important, even if the decision is wrong / dangerous
2. SCOTUS should never weigh in against democracy; they will inevitably overstep and get it wrong

- This is the dilemma: Hard to only allow the Court to act heroically, but not allow them to also make bad decisions (agony of judicial restraint and judicial activism).
