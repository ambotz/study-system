# ConLaw 8 — Suspending and Dispensing Powers?

## Readings

- [[United States v. Cox (1965)|United States v. Cox]]

- [[The Thompson Memo]]

## Notes

### [[United States v. Cox (1965)|US v. Cox]]

### Holdings

- Jones (+3) - Can't make U.S. Attorney sign indictment
    - He's appointed by the President, exercising executive power
    - Take Care Clause (Art. 2, sec. 3) = discretionary power
- Dissent - Must bring the case if grand jury finds probable cause
    - Executive can then choose to not go forward, but then the decision will be **in public** at least
    - What Rules of Criminal Procedure technically says
    - Usually don't need to show "good faith" because the govt. is the one who is actually enforcing the law
- Brown - 'middle ground'. Must prepare but don't have to sign
    - Aids public shaming effort
    - What if someone else signs it?
        - **No if unitary executive**
        - **Yes, maybe if there are independent special prosecutors**
- Wisdom - Strong sense of prosecutorial discretion
    - Federalism concern on top of separation of powers concern
    - Mississippi grand jury and judge against national AG → clash of policy

### Suspension Power?

- Constitutional? There needs to be a limiting power.
    - Congressional command could be a limiting power (forcing prosecution)

### [[Adams v. Richardson (1973)|Adams v. Richardson]]

- Prosecutorial discretion power was overruled through:
    1. Statutory mandate
    2. General policy
    3. Resources are provided and misused under the guise of prosecutorial discretion
    4. Civil [?]
- POTUS can issue pardons under Art. 2, sec. 2, clause 1 (arrow from "Civil?")

### Marijuana hypo

- What if prosecution of marijuana is completely stopped?
    - If you leave a case-by-case backdoor but generally don't enforce it → typically allowed, but shows major rule of law concerns

### [[The Thompson Memo]]

1. Prioritization of certain illegal aliens
    - Clean use of prosecutorial discretion
2. Deferred Action
    - Showcases a general policy, attaches benefits on top of no prosecution
    - Still, no statutory mandate is being overridden.
    - **But must Congress have to add "this law must be enforced" after every law it passes?**
    - Ultimately held unlawful by 5th circuit because of general policy + granting of benefits

### Current examples

- Executive not enforcing ban against Tik Tok, National Firearms Act → SCOTUS necessarily may not agree
    - **It's hard for these cases to get into court. Who is being harmed by these "non-enforcements"? Who has [[Standing - Art. III § 2 cases and controversies|standing]]?**
- Would it be different if POTUS says no enforcement because it is unconstitutional? Rather than policy disagreement?
