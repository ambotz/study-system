## Notes

### Federalist papers

- [[The Federalist No. 70|Federalist No. 70]]
    - Emphasizes the benefit of one person as executive (energy & unity)
- [[The Federalist No. 76|Federalist No. 76]]
    - Appointment Clause → compromise that allows president to make choices with a legislative "check" by the Senate
    - **Founders didn't imagine a two-party system at the time of the founding → leads to separation of parties, not powers**
    - State govts: Executive positions are elected separately in state govts. Only national govt. has just POTUS & no other separate elections
        - Hamilton wrong for Unitary executive?
        - Are states too fragmented or is the federal govt. too unitary?
        - Maybe national powers are inherently broad and one person is better

### [[Appointments - Art. II § 2 cl. 2 and the officer line|Art 2. §2. Cl 2.]]

- Inferior officers
    - Sometimes president doesn't have the authority to appoint these officers (contra. [[The Federalist No. 76|Federalist 76]].)

### [[Buckley v. Valeo (1976)|Buckley v. Valeo]]

- Arguments against decision / conflicting
    - Pragmatism
    - [[Enumerated powers and the Necessary and Proper Clause - Art. I § 8 cl. 18|Nec. + Proper Clause]] (Art. 1, §8, cl. 18)
        - Limited by what is "constitutional"(?)
    - "Officers of the United States"
        - What counts: "**significant authority**" (not for every one(thing)); just like [[INS v. Chadha (1983)|Chadha]]

### Senate vs. House

- Why Senate > House for Executive hand-holding?
    - Senate more refined, less elections, less populist, more decorum

### [[Buckley v. Valeo (1976)|Buckley v. Valeo]] Cont.

- For the Pres + House + Senate (2 appointments) → why is this unconstitutional?
    - Can they sever it?
    - Why is this different than having an expert qualification (i.e. learned in the law?) → this is allowed

### Removal Clause

- [[Impeachment - Art. II § 4 high crimes and misdemeanors|Art 2, §4]]
    - Requires impeachment, conviction, or other high crimes
    - Impeachment = House + 2/3 Senate [check: impeachment is by the House; the 2/3 vote is the Senate's vote to convict]

### [[The Decision of 1789|Decision of 1789]]

- Allowed executive removal of officers (without cause) who execute the law

### Removal Options (theory, not practice) at 1789

1. Impeachment (House + 2/3 Senate) - No one agreed with this
2. President + Senate - No one agreed with this
3. President
4. Congress (via Nec. + proper clause) → this could've been a valid position
    - **Hard to tell if this side could've also won (but just got washed over).**
