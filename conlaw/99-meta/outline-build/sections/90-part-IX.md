# Part IX — Issue Maps: Past Exams and Predicted Fact Patterns

## Past Baude exams, mapped

**Word ratio.** The ratio moved: 2018 ran 35 words per point (1050/30, 1750/50, 700/20); 2021 and 2023 ran 30 (1500/50, 600/20, 900/30; 1350/45, 900/30, 750/25). Budget at 30 unless the cover page says otherwise. Holdings below are stated under **current** doctrine, which has moved since each exam: [[Trump v. Slaughter (2026)|Slaughter]] and [[Trump v. Cook (2026)|Cook]] (removal), [[Trump v. United States (2024)|Trump v. United States]] (immunity), and *Loper Bright* (2024, statutory deference only; not a constitutional holding).

### 2018 Q1 — Wildfire spending condition + wildfire tax (30 pts, 1050 words)

- **Role and ask.** Adviser to the Governor of California: can the State or its citizens sue, what are the arguments, and which is strongest. The ranking is graded.
- **Headings.** `Part One: Spending Condition` / `Part Two: Wildfire Tax`, each opening with `Standing`, then the merits gates.

| Gate | Holding | Authority | Strongest counter | Fact I'd need |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standing — State | California has standing for the spending part: loss of its own grant is a direct injury, not parens patriae | [[Ex parte Levitt (1937)\|Levitt]]; [[Massachusetts v. Mellon; Frothingham v. Mellon (1923)\|Mellon]] bars only suits on behalf of citizens against the United States | Unripe until a fire and a Secretary finding | Whether California must spend now to assess compliance |
| Standing — citizens | Citizens may challenge the tax they pay; taxpayer standing is fine against your own tax (Baude gloss) | [[Standing - Art. III § 2 cases and controversies]] | No injury until a fire rages | Whether a fire has already triggered the tax this year |
| Spending — Dole prongs | The condition should fail the unambiguous prong, the prong Baude says led between Dole and NFIB | [[South Dakota v. Dole (1987)\|Dole]]; [[Spending power - Art. I § 8 cl. 1 general welfare and conditional grants]] | "Substantially mitigate" is a standard States can meet; judicial review of the Secretary cures vagueness | The Secretary's criteria, if any exist in regulations |
| Spending — coercion | Size alone fails: $600M is 0.3% of a $200B budget, near Dole's figure (5% of highway funds, under half of one percent of South Dakota's budget), far from NFIB's over-10% | [[NFIB v. Sebelius (2012)\|NFIB]] (Roberts) | NFIB's second fact — pre-existing programs relied on — is present here | Whether USFS services count as "funds" at all |
| Spending — withheld services | Withholding direct federal firefighting, not a grant, may not be a spending condition at all; argue it as a threat, not an offer | [[Spending power - Art. I § 8 cl. 1 general welfare and conditional grants]]; Property Clause, Art. IV § 3 cl. 2 for fires on federal land | Congress may choose not to spend at all | Share of USFS firefighting on federal vs. state land in California |
| Delegation | The Secretary's compliance finding survives the intelligible-principle test | [[Gundy v. United States (2019)\|Gundy]] (Kagan); [[J. W. Hampton, Jr., & Co. v. United States\|J.W. Hampton]] | Gorsuch (Gundy, dissent): the Secretary sets the policy | — |
| Tax — classification | A per-person amount "on all persons" reads as a capitation, which must be apportioned and is not | Art. I § 9 cl. 4; [[Hylton v. United States (1796)\|Hylton]] | Roberts: a tax "triggered by specific circumstances" is not a capitation | — |
| Tax — uniformity | If not direct, it is an indirect tax laid only in five named States, which fails geographic uniformity | Art. I § 8 cl. 1; [[Taxing power - Art. I § 8 cl. 1 and direct taxes]] | Designation tracks fifteen years of fire data | Whether the designation list updates |
| Tax — penalty | Not a penalty on the four Bailey factors: capped near $2000, no scienter, no prescribed code of conduct, revenue-raising | [[Bailey v. Drexel Furniture Co (1922)\|Bailey]]; NFIB | 20% of income for a filer at the threshold is prohibitory | Collecting agency |

- **Strongest, ranked.** (1) Spending ambiguity — the exemplars' lead, because compliance is decided after the fire by the Secretary. (2) Coercion via reliance on pre-existing programs (NFIB's second fact). (3) Tax form — the whipsaw: either a capitation not apportioned or an indirect tax not uniform; doctrinally strong but less developed in the exemplars. (4) Delegation, which should be named and dismissed.
- **What scored.** Clause cite in sentence one ("Art.I-7"); budget arithmetic as percentages of the state budget; "unclear whether that holding had 5 votes"; an explicit "California's strongest challenge is"; one answer added Origination (Art. I § 7 cl. 1) as a free point. Equal sovereignty (*Shelby County*) appeared in both exemplars but is not on this year's syllabus — one sentence at most.
- **Budget.** Spending 500 · Tax 450 · Standing 100.

### 2018 Q2 — Independent Defender Commission (A 40 pts / B 10 pts, 1750 words)

- **Role and ask.** Senate Judiciary Committee lawyer: memo on constitutional problems with two proposals (A), then the best structure that avoids them (B).
- **Headings.** `A. Proposal 1` → `Branch` · `Commissioners: Appointment` · `Commissioners: Removal` · `Office Heads` · `Appropriations`; `A. Proposal 2` → `Appointment by Judges` · `Judges as Commissioners`; `B. Redesign`.

| Gate | Holding | Authority | Strongest counter | Fact I'd need |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Branch | Rulemaking, appointing and supervising defenders is executive power whatever the statute calls it | [[Trump v. Slaughter (2026)\|Slaughter]] (rulemaking "executive through and through"); [[Separation of powers - vesting clauses and checks and balances]] | Defense has always sat with the courts; Slaughter reserved bodies outside the executive; *Mistretta* put a commission in the judicial branch | Whether the Commission adjudicates anything |
| Officer status | Commissioners exercise significant authority on a continuing basis | [[Buckley v. Valeo (1976)\|Buckley]] | — | — |
| Principal or inferior | Principal: no superior directs or removes them | *Edmond*; [[Morrison v. Olson\|Morrison]] factors | Morrison majority: limited jurisdiction | — |
| Slate from Chief Justice | The slate transfers the choice of officer and should fall | Buckley; Art. II § 2 cl. 2; Baude gloss: prescribing who may serve vs. choosing who serves | A slate is a qualification, like "learned in the law" | Whether the President may reject the slate and ask again |
| Party balance / judge cap | Qualifications narrowing the field are valid | Appointments gloss; Mistretta | Slaughter: partisan balance does not save a removal protection | — |
| Removal only by impeachment | Unconstitutional for officers exercising executive power | Slaughter; [[The Decision of 1789]] (impeachment-only rejected); [[Myers v. United States\|Myers]] | Slaughter's reservation for non-executive and non-Article III bodies | — |
| Office heads | Inferior officers; the Commission may appoint them only if it is a "Head of Department" | [[Free Enterprise Fund v. Public Company Accounting Oversight Board\|Free Enterprise Fund]] (multimember head) | A judicial-branch body is not an executive "Department" | — |
| Appropriations | Submitting requests is fine; nothing may be drawn without a statute | Art. I § 9 cl. 7; [[The power of the purse - Art. I § 9 cl. 7 appropriations]] | — | Whether "submitting appropriations" means binding amounts |
| Proposal 2 | Judges electing principal officers fails; "Courts of Law" may appoint only inferior officers | Art. II § 2 cl. 2; Morrison (incongruity) | Commissioners as inferior officers of a court | Whether a circuit's district judges act as a "Court of Law" |
| Judges doing executive work | Extra-judicial duties raise the [[The Correspondence of the Justices]] objection | Correspondence; Mistretta | Mistretta allowed it | — |

- **Part B redesign.** An executive-branch commission; PAS commissioners removable at will (after Slaughter, for-cause protection for principal officers wielding executive power survives only for the Fed, limited-duty inferior officers, and the reserved non-Article III and adjudicatory bodies — none fits this commission); a non-binding recommendation list from the Chief Justice; party balance retained as a qualification; office heads appointed by the Commission as a department head; an intelligible principle stated in the statute.
- **What scored.** "Morrison majority would argue … But I think Edmond controls"; Bland and Smith in 1789 by name; layering problems flagged (double for-cause, [[Free Enterprise Fund v. Public Company Accounting Oversight Board|Free Enterprise Fund]]).
- **Budget.** A 1400 (Proposal 1 ≈ 900, Proposal 2 ≈ 350, common 150) · B 350.

### 2021 Q1 — Climate bill: doctrine (20) and departmentalist (30) (50 pts, 1500 words)

- **Role and ask.** White House lawyer before signing: (1) constitutional under current doctrine; (2) as a departmentalist, under the best understanding — state the method, defend it, apply it.
- **Headings.** `1. Current Doctrine` / `2. Best Understanding`, each with the same four issues: `Congressional Power` · `Delegation` · `Removal` · `Tax`.

| Gate | Doctrine holding | Authority | Best-understanding move | Fact I'd need |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Power | Regulating emissions from economic activity reaches commodity markets in the aggregate | [[Wickard v. Filburn (1942)\|Wickard]]; [[Gonzales v. Raich (2005)\|Raich]] (Stevens: production, distribution, consumption of commodities); [[United States v. Lopez (1995)\|Lopez]] | "Proper" limit and the means–end grammar ([[Enumerated powers and the Necessary and Proper Clause - Art. I § 8 cl. 18]]) | Whether regulations reach non-economic conduct |
| Delegation | "Prevent human activities from changing the global climate" should pass as an intelligible principle | [[Gundy v. United States (2019)\|Gundy]] (Kagan); contrast [[A.L.A. Schechter Poultry Corp. v. United States\|Schechter]] | Madison wins [[The Post Roads Debate]]; Gorsuch's three categories | Whether the statute prioritizes competing goals |
| Removal | A single head removable at will is the Myers default | [[Myers v. United States\|Myers]]; [[Trump v. Slaughter (2026)\|Slaughter]] | Liquidation of 1789 settles at-will removal | — |
| Tax — penalty? | A $51/ton charge on all emitters raises revenue and is a tax despite EPA collection | [[United States v. Kahriger\|Kahriger]]; Bailey agency factor | Tax power as independent means (Hamilton) | Size relative to typical emissions |
| Tax — uniformity | An excise exempting all of West Virginia fails geographic uniformity | Art. I § 8 cl. 1 | "General welfare" as a substantive limit on a vote-buying carve-out | — |
| Signing | The President may sign and must then decline to enforce only what is unconstitutional; severability decides whether the exemption falls alone | Baude, *Signing Unconstitutional Laws*; [[Obama, Statement on H.R. 1473]] | Departmentalist duty under the oath, Art. II § 1 cl. 8 | Severability clause |

- **What scored.** The exemplar's parallel structure (the same issues under each half, with General Welfare added in the second); a stated, defended method in its own paragraph; "I think it's a mistake that the Court has basically given up on reading 'general welfare' as a substantive requirement"; a fourteen-word section ("Removal Power — … this doctrine seems fine to me").
- **Budget.** Doctrine 600 · Best understanding 900 (method 200, application 700).

### 2021 Q2 — One-house subpoenas of Justices (20 pts, 600 words)

- **Role and ask.** District-court clerk: objections to the House and Senate subpoenas under Supreme Court doctrine; how the judge should rule.
- **Syllabus gap.** *McGrain v. Daugherty* and *Trump v. Mazars* are not on the Autumn 2026 syllabus. Map the question with this year's materials.
- **Headings.** `Standing` · `House Subpoena` · `Senate Subpoena`.

| Gate | Holding | Authority | Counter | Fact I'd need |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standing | The Justices have standing as owners of the subpoenaed records | [[Ex parte Levitt (1937)\|Levitt]] (direct injury) | Dropbox is the subpoenaed party | Whether Dropbox will comply |
| One-house action | A subpoena does not alter rights "outside the Legislative Branch" the way Chadha's veto did, so § 7 does not apply | [[INS v. Chadha (1983)\|Chadha]]; [[Bicameralism and presentment - Art. I § 7]] | Compelled production does alter a third party's legal duties | — |
| House purpose | Investigation in aid of the House's "sole Power of Impeachment" is valid | Art. I § 2 cl. 5; [[Impeachment - Art. II § 4 high crimes and misdemeanors]] | [[The Impeachment of Justice Chase\|Chase]]: judicial method is not impeachable | Whether the Justices misrepresented their method |
| Senate purpose | Court size is a legislative subject, so the Senate investigation has a valid object | Art. III § 1; [[Senate Report on Court Packing]]; [[Congressional control of the courts - Art. III jurisdiction and court size]] | The Senate's impeachment role is only to try | Whether court packing legislation is actually pending |
| Personal documents | Narrow the order to official and seance-related records | Proportionality, not a holding | — | Volume and nature of personal records |

- **What scored.** Opening that converts the issue into three numbered questions; [[Senate Report on Court Packing]] quoted against itself; impeachment framed by Hamilton's "abuse or violation of some public trust."
- **Budget.** Standing 60 · House 250 · Senate 250 · close 40.

### 2023 Q1 — Student-loan non-enforcement (A/B/C, 15 pts each, 1350 words)

- **Role and ask.** (A) a court bound by precedent; (B) impeachment — could and should; (C) a successor reinstating enforcement with interest.
- **Headings.** `A.` · `B. Could?` / `B. Should?` · `C.`

| Part | Holding | Authority | Counter | Fact I'd need |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — category | No statute, so twilight at best; lowest ebb if the loan statute mandates collection | [[Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer (1952)\|Youngstown]] (Jackson); [[Presidential power - Art. II and the Youngstown categories]] | Take Care as a source of discretion | The loan statute's text on collection |
| A — discretion | Category-wide, permanent forbearance is suspension, not discretion; the court should hold it unlawful | [[Adams v. Richardson (1973)\|Adams]]; [[United States v. Cox (1965)\|Cox]]; [[The Thompson Memo]]; [[Enforcement discretion - Art. II § 3 Take Care]] | [[Heckler v. Chaney]]: non-enforcement presumptively unreviewable | Whether any case-by-case backdoor remains |
| B — could | Yes: "high Crimes and Misdemeanors" reaches abuse of office, not only indictable crime | Art. II § 4; Hamilton; [[The Impeachment of Andrew Johnson\|Johnson]] | Chase and Johnson acquittals: good-faith legal disagreement is not impeachable | Whether the President claimed unconstitutionality or mere policy |
| B — should | Commit either way; the exemplars split, and the defense is what scores | Federalist 49 via [[Judicial supremacy and departmentalism - Art. VI oath]] | Escalation norm: impeachment for constitutional error weakens the signal | Whether Congress first legislated against the policy |
| C — reinstatement | The successor may resume collection: non-enforcement never repealed the debt | [[Clinton v. City of New York (1998)\|Clinton v. City of New York]] (no unilateral repeal); [[Trump v. Slaughter (2026)\|Slaughter]] (Secretary at will) | Reliance and fair notice for interest and penalties | Statute of limitations; whether penalties accrue by statute or agency choice |
| C — retroactive penalties | The Ex Post Facto Clause reaches criminal punishment only (*Calder v. Bull*), so argue due process and fair notice instead | Art. I § 9 cl. 3; [[Bills of attainder - Art. I § 9 cl. 3]] | Penalties for a period the government said no payment was due | — |

- **Current-doctrine updates.** [[Trump v. United States (2024)|Trump v. United States]] makes core Article II acts immune from prosecution, which raises impeachment's importance as the check (Part B). *Biden v. Nebraska* (2023) is not on the syllabus.
- **What scored.** Bracketed assumption ("[I'm assuming this is a departmentalist judge …]"); `Could?/Should?` headings; "The House should impeach and the Senate should convict"; a recommended intermediate step (pass a statute first, then impeach on defiance).
- **Budget.** 450 each; in B, could 200 / should 250.

### 2023 Q2 — Gun Free Sensitive Places Tax (30 pts, 900 words)

- **Role and ask.** District judge sorting which parts are constitutional.
- **Headings.** `Tax?` · `Post Office` · `Federal Courts` · `Train Stations` · `State Courts` · `Schools` — the prompt's four location categories (post office; state or federal courthouse; school; train station), with the exemplars splitting courthouses into federal and state.

| Gate | Holding | Authority | Counter | Fact I'd need |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tax? | Under doctrine it is a penalty on the Bailey factors: $25,000 per possession, "knowingly," collected by non-revenue agencies, with location rules as the prescribed code of conduct | [[Bailey v. Drexel Furniture Co (1922)\|Bailey]]; [[NFIB v. | Post office | Valid under Art. I § 8 cl. 7 plus Necessary and Proper | [[McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)\|McCulloch]] (punishing mail robbery) | "Great substantive and independent power" | — |
| Federal courts | Valid for inferior courts (Art. I § 8 cl. 9; Art. III § 1) and for the Supreme Court building under the District power, Art. I § 8 cl. 17 | McCulloch; [[United States v. Comstock\|Comstock]] | No text for the Supreme Court's premises | Location of the courthouse |
| Train stations | Uphold: stations are instrumentalities of interstate commerce | [[United States v. Lopez (1995)\|Lopez]] category 2; [[Gonzales v. Raich (2005)\|Raich]] (class of activities) | The train is the instrumentality, not the station | Whether the station serves interstate lines |
| State courts | Unconstitutional unless the tax survives | Lopez (non-economic possession); [[Printz v. United States (1997)\|Printz]] not triggered — private persons regulated | Guarantee Clause; Art. VI duty of state judges | — |
| Schools | Lopez controls; fails without the tax | Lopez (Rehnquist; Kennedy concurring) | Raich class framing | Jurisdictional hook (gun moved in commerce) |

- **What scored.** "Assuming all the plaintiffs have suffered an injury in fact, they have standing to sue (Levitt)" — threshold disposed in one line; ranking within a heading ("strong argument … slightly weaker theory"); avoidance canon from NFIB; "It's shakier, but she should uphold this provision."
- **Budget.** Tax 200 · five headings 130 each · close 50.
