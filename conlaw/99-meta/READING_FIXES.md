# Con Law reading files — errors found during the module pass (2026-09-25)

Found while checking the 31 doctrine modules against the casebook (PMBB 4th ed. EPUB) and the 5th-ed. print packets. The modules are already corrected; the reading files in `10-cases/` are not. This is the starting list for the vault-wide grammar/substance pass.

## Substantive errors

- **Hylton v. United States** (Context) — says Roberts "noted that Pollock's extension had been undone by the Sixteenth Amendment." Roberts says only the income-tax result was overturned; the Court "continued to consider taxes on personal property to be direct taxes" (p. 640).
- **Bailey v. Drexel Furniture Co** (Rejected, 3rd bullet) — "the independence of the taxing power matters only where the same sovereign holds both powers" garbles Taft: the tax/penalty distinction is immaterial where one sovereign holds both powers and matters when they are split.
- **Bailey v. Drexel Furniture Co** and **Implied Limits on the Power to Tax** — call Bailey the only case to strike a tax. True within the casebook's six-case survey, but NFIB pairs Butler with Drexel as cases "invalidating punitive exactions."
- **South Dakota v. Dole** (O'Connor dissent bullet) — presents the 84 percent fatality figure as O'Connor's own; it is Sen. Humphrey's remark, quoted in her dissent.
- **The Impeachment of Andrew Johnson** (Note 1 bullet) — list of powers is wrong; casebook p. 357 lists suspending, removal, directing, appointment and war powers (pardon and veto come from Ashley's 1867 resolution). Also presents "misconstruction of a doubtful law" as Trumbull's words; it is Currie's paraphrase.
- **Ex parte Merryman** (Cold-call notes) and **Bates, Opinion on the Suspension of Habeas Corpus** (Note 1 bullet) — say Jackson in Youngstown relied on the Suspension Clause's placement in Article I. Jackson says only that it is the sole express emergency provision.
- **Ex parte Merryman** (Rule) — "where the courts are open" has no source in the casebook.
- **In re Neagle** (Context) — says Jackson's Youngstown concurrence treats protective power as the residue of inherent executive authority. The casebook cites Neagle only in Clark's footnote 3.
- **In re Neagle** (cold-call "tension with anti-commandeering") and **Article VI - other materials** (What to notice, bullet 4; "What the article does not say") — say Printz "rests on" / "builds a whole doctrine on" Article VI's naming of judges. Overstated: Printz concedes "no constitutional text speaking to this precise question" and uses the judges clause only to distinguish Testa.
- **Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer** (Reasoning, Context) and **The Federalist No. 70** — say Jackson cites Weimar's Article 48. The casebook excerpt cuts that passage; Weimar appears only in the editors' Note 4 (p. 62).
- **Madison's Notes on the War Power** (Note 2) — says The Prize Cases and WPR § 2(c)(3) both treat an attack on "armed forces" as sufficient. The Prize Cases involved an attack at home.
- **Terminating War** and **Declarations of War** — state as fact that the funding cutoff ended U.S. involvement in Vietnam; the casebook only asks the question.
- **Ex parte McCardle** (Context) — presents Klein, Yerger, Bank Markazi, Patchak, Felker and Hart/Amar without flagging that none is in the casebook or packets. Dates McCardle 1869; the casebook says 1868 (argued 1868, decided April 1869 — reconcile).
- **Ex parte Levitt** (Cold-call notes) — cites [[Standing, Mootness, and Ripeness]] for a rule (Congress cannot convert a generalized grievance into injury) that the note does not contain.
- **Adams v. Richardson** (Facts) — quotes "law to apply"; the casebook phrase is "no law to apply."
- **Buckley v. Valeo** (Cold-call notes) — "legislative or . . . judicial aid" should read "as a legislative or as a judicial aid."
- **Constitutional Themes** — voting-amendments list omits the Twenty-seventh, which the casebook includes (pp. 36–37).
- **The Dead-Hand Problem** ("The link to interpretation") — presents the mapping of the four answers onto interpretive methods as casebook content; it is the note-writer's gloss.
- **National Pork Producers Council v. Ross** (Context) — calls Corfield's dormant-commerce paragraph "unreported"; the case is reported (6 F. Cas. 546), the casebook just doesn't print that passage.
- **Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority** (Context) — Gregory v. Ashcroft's clear-statement rule is supplied from outside the casebook, which cites Gregory only for "dual sovereignty."
- **Corfield v. Coryell** (Context) — Toomer, Baldwin and the two-step "substantial reason / close relation" test come from outside the casebook; label them.
- **Gundy v. United States** — paraphrases Gorsuch as "fewer, slower, more consensual, more stable and more accountable"; not the casebook's language.
- **NFIB v. Sebelius** (minor) — never makes clear that Ginsburg's "no entitlement" premise is what Roberts answers with "begs the question."
