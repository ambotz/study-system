# Con Law — open flags after the 2026-09-26 proofread

Every file in `10-cases/` and `30-doctrine/` was proofread and cite-checked against the 4th-ed. casebook text and the 5th-ed. packets. Every item on the 2026-09-25 list below has been fixed in its file, except three that turned out to be wrong themselves:

- **Weimar (Youngstown, Federalist No. 70) — withdrawn.** Jackson's Weimar paragraph *is* in the casebook excerpt (4th ed. p. 56 and the Class 02 packet). Only the "Article 48" label was removed, since the source never uses it.
- **Vietnam funding cutoff (Declarations of War, Terminating War) — revised.** The casebook does state it as fact: the funding decision "ultimately led to the U.S. military's withdrawal from, and the fall of, South Vietnam" (p. 315). Both files now attribute the point to the casebook.
- **NFIB "begs the question" — revised.** Roberts's line answers Ginsburg's "not threatened to withhold funds earmarked for any other program" point, not her "no entitlement" premise (p. 643).

**Corrected from general knowledge because the casebook is wrong or silent:**
- **United States v. E.C. Knight:** 8–1 (Harlan dissenting). The casebook says 5–4.
- **Bailey v. Drexel:** the child labor tax came from the Revenue Act of 1918, enacted Feb. 1919.
- **Lincoln's First Inaugural:** about five weeks before Fort Sumter.
- **Texas v. White:** Chase states the rule on which rebel acts survive in a passage the casebook omits (74 U.S. at 733).

**Also worth a look:**
- **Kahriger:** the casebook says "Two justices dissented." The vote was 6–3 (Frankfurter, Douglas, Black). No file states a count, but watch for it.
- **Seila Law:** the severability part is described as "for seven Justices." It was a three-Justice plurality plus four concurring in the judgment.
- **Campbell title:** probably "Four Views **of** the Nature of the Union." Renaming the file means changing every link.
- **Riddles of Article V:** the file gives pp. 804–812; the questions run 804–810 (4th ed.).
- **Texas v. White:** the claim that Printz and the Garcia dissents quote "indestructible States" is unverified. One cold-call bullet about bona fide purchasers is doubtful.
- **Lincoln, First Inaugural:** "the Republican platform he quotes here was built on it" is doubtful. The file also says the casebook assigns Davis as the second position, but Davis is a Canvas reading.
- **Dred Scott:** Lemmon v. The People is called "(not assigned)," but its opening sits on the last assigned page.
- **Buckley v. Valeo:** the statement that the statute placed the FEC in the legislative branch is not in the excerpt.
- **Frontmatter:** McCardle's `year: 1869` is right by decision date (the casebook says 1868; the body explains why). Merryman's citation says C.C.D. Md., the casebook "D. Md."
