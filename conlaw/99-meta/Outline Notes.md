4. Darby
	1. **The shipment ban** (prohibiting interstate transport of goods made below the labor standards) rests on the **Commerce Clause directly** — the power to regulate, which _includes the power to prohibit_, things moving across state lines (_Champion v. Ames_, the Lottery Case). For this, McCulloch/N&P isn’t needed. Stone leans on _Gibbons v. Ogden_ here for the idea that the commerce power is “plenary and complete in itself… acknowledges no limitations other than are prescribed in the Constitution,” and adds the motive-blindness rule: “The motive and purpose of a regulation of interstate commerce are matters for the legislative judgment… over which the courts are given no control.”
	- **The wage-and-hour regulation of production** (telling a Georgia lumber mill what to pay workers and how many hours they work) is the _harder_ half — because production/manufacturing is **not itself “commerce”** (that was exactly _E.C. Knight_’s and _Hammer_’s premise). To reach a _local, intrastate_ activity, Congress needs a bridge, and **that bridge is McCulloch**. This is where Darby cites it: Congress can regulate intrastate activities that “so affect interstate commerce… as to make regulation of them **appropriate means to the attainment of a legitimate end**.” That phrase is McCulloch’s Necessary-and-Proper formula almost verbatim.
	- So it’s not “McCulloch via N&P” _vs._ “Darby via Commerce Clause.” It’s: **Darby = Commerce Clause for the easy part (the border crossing) + McCulloch’s N&P means-end logic for the hard part (reaching production that affects commerce).**
	- Modern Court describes the **“substantial effects” prong as resting on the Commerce Clause _together with_ the Necessary and Proper Clause** — it says so explicitly in _Raich_ (Scalia’s concurrence) and _NFIB_. Darby is the case that fuses them.
	- **Holmes = goods must cross the line (border) → Darby = reach production that _affects_ commerce (effects) → Wickard = _aggregate_ effects, no crossing required at all** (wheat grown and eaten on the same farm)
5. Interstate Commerce lineage
	1. Shreveport/Swift → **Jones & Laughlin (the modern origin)** → **Darby (generalizes it + kills _Hammer_ and the Tenth Amendment limit)** → **Wickard (adds aggregation)**.
	2. Darby and Jones & Laughlin spoke mostly in **Commerce Clause** terms (“affecting commerce,” “close and substantial relation”), _using_ McCulloch’s means-end logic as support rather than formally grounding the power in the Necessary and Proper _Clause_. The explicit “the substantial-effects prong actually rests on the N&P Clause” framing is a **modern gloss** — _Lopez_ (1995, the three categories), Scalia’s _Raich_ concurrence (2005), and _NFIB_ (2012).
		1. So it’s most accurate to say: **substantial effects is a Commerce Clause doctrine that runs on McCulloch’s N&P _reasoning_, and which the modern Court later re-grounded in the N&P _clause_ by name.**
	3. **Framing A — “the Commerce Clause alone.”** The activity _itself_ counts as within the commerce power _because_ it substantially affects interstate commerce. You collapse the local activity _into_ “commerce.” The only question is **magnitude of effect** — and once you allow aggregation (_Wickard_), that’s barely a limit at all. The power is self-justifying: if it affects commerce, it _is_ commerce, full stop. One screen, and a porous one.
	- **Framing B — “Commerce + Necessary and Proper” (means-end).** The local activity is **not** itself commerce; Congress reaches it only as a **means** to the **end** of regulating interstate commerce. The power is **derivative**, and _that_ is what carries the extra limits — two of them, neither of which exists in Framing A: (Scalia in Raich; dilutes the substantial effects prong of interstate commerce to this)
	    1. **There must be a genuine commerce-regulating END to be a means _to_.** The local reach is parasitic; it has to effectuate an actual regulation of the interstate market. This is why _Lopez_ and _Morrison_ fail — guns-near-schools and gender-violence are non-economic and sit in _no_ larger economic scheme, so there’s no commerce “end” the reach could be a “means” to — and why **Raich succeeds**: Scalia upholds the ban on purely intrastate, non-commercial home-grown marijuana _not_ because it “substantially affects commerce” on its own, but because banning it is a **necessary and proper means to make the comprehensive interstate drug-market regulation effective** (stop leakage). The tether to a real scheme is the limit.
	    2. **“Proper.”** The means can’t be a “great substantive and independent power” or one that subverts the structure. This is **NFIB**: Roberts assumes the mandate might be _necessary_ to the insurance reforms, but holds it is **not _proper_** — compelling people into commerce would “work a substantial expansion of federal authority,” a _new_ freestanding power dressed up as an incident. Framing A has no such screen; “affects commerce” never asks whether the means is a legitimate _incident_ versus a new sovereign power.
	    3. **So the paradox dissolves:** the limit doesn’t come from N&P being a “limiting clause” that magically imports a constraint. It comes from the **grammar of derivation**. To get the _enhanced reach_ (into activity that isn’t commerce), you must argue _means-to-an-end_
	    4. Why the modern Court bothered to **re-route** substantial effects through N&P at all: by _Wickard_, the bare “substantial effects” framing had dissolved every limit (aggregate anything and it “affects” commerce). _Lopez_ tried to rebuild a limit _inside_ the Commerce Clause framing (the activity must be “economic”), but that was clumsy. _Raich_ and _NFIB_ instead re-conceived the reach-to-local-activity as an **N&P means question**, precisely so the Court could deploy the “proper”/incidental/great-power screen — **a structural limit the magnitude test couldn’t supply.**
	1. So the arc is: **Marshall reserves the pretext check → Wickard/Jackson releases it to politics → Lopez/Morrison (Commerce side) and NFIB (N&P side) reclaim it**
6. 3 Interstate Commerce levels
	1. (1) Channels, (2) Instrumentalities, (3) Substantial Effects
	2. **Economic character establishes the nexus at the _class_ level, categorically.** If the regulated activity is economic, you get _aggregation_ (Wickard, Raich): Congress regulates the whole class on a substantial-_effects_ theory, and **no jurisdictional hook is needed** — the economic nature of the activity does the work. This is category 3 proper.
	- **A jurisdictional element establishes the nexus at the _case_ level, individually.** It sidesteps the aggregate-effects theory entirely. A statute that only reaches a gun “that has moved in interstate commerce” isn’t really relying on _substantial effects_ at all — it’s invoking something closer to **category 1/2** (the _thing itself_ traveled in commerce). You’ve converted a hard category-3 question into an easy “this object crossed a state line” showing, proven prosecution-by-prosecution.
		- So the hook is the **fallback for _non-economic_ statutes.**
	- Lopez vs. Raich -- Fairly confident the ambiguity between these two cases will be exploited
		- **Regulated _activity_ vs. mere _object_ (the thin one).** _Lopez_ regulated gun **possession** in a school zone. Possession is status/conduct. _Raich_ regulated **cultivation and consumption** — production and consumption of the commodity itself — which _is_ economic. The existence of a gun market is beside the point, because _Lopez didn’t regulate the gun market or gun manufacture_ — it regulated _where you may carry_ one. The line is a blur.
			- Stevens defines “economics” as “the production, distribution, and consumption of **commodities**.”
			- CSA targets a **commodity market** (by stamping it out).
			- So § 922(q) is **severable** from the economic parts of § 922; homegrown weed is **load-bearing** within the CSA.
		- **Comprehensive scheme vs. freestanding statute (the strong one).** This is where the cases really part. The **GFSZA was a standalone**. The **CSA is the opposite** — an enormous integrated regime, and the home-grown ban is one gear in it 
			- _Lopez_ carve-out is phrased as “an essential part of a larger regulation of **economic activity**,
			- Test for part of a comprehensive scheme
				- **Umbrella test:** Is the challenged provision embedded in a broader statute that regulates a **commercial market / economic activity** (production, distribution, sale of a commodity) — not a standalone criminal/police statute?
				- **Leakage / essentiality test (the real workhorse):** Would exempting the local activity **undercut** that market regulation — i.e., is the local activity **fungible with, or a substitute input to**, the regulated interstate market, so that leaving it alone would defeat the federal regime?
